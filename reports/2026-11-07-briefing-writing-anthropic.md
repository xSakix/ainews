## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- Anthropic: how Claude's agent memory works, and what "dreaming" adds — Anthropic's engineering lead on memory argues that memory is a prediction about what a future session will need. The post then tests that claim. On Continual Learning Bench (CL-Bench) 1.0, newer Claude models write better memory notes unprompted, and telling Opus 5 and Opus 4.7 to "record only failures" erased much of their gain from memory. Steering helped older models most: prompting Sonnet 4.6 to read its memory files and test its theories nearly doubled its score, while Sonnet 5 read memory before answering 9 times out of 10 unprompted (Sonnet 4.6: under half). A background "dreaming" pass that rereads transcripts fixed a wrong note in a Pokémon run. It also built wikis over about 350,000 files (about 420M tokens) from the Stainless corpus. Over 128 dreaming sessions, scores on 25 questions rose from 13% to 48%. Search plus wiki beat search alone (61% vs 53%) and saved about 20,000 tokens per question. On a merger-document task, one page per contract that quoted terms verbatim lifted fact coverage from 41/57 to 57/57. One wiki grew larger than the documents it summarised. This is Anthropic's own evaluation of its own models, so the numbers are the company's, but the failure cases are reported openly. It is also the practical context-engineering item of the day (see section 3). Author: Lance Martin, Anthropic · Format: deep dive · Length: about 20 min read. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/components-of-agent-memory

- vLLM: zero-mismatch asynchronous RL — Asynchronous RL trains on samples from older weights ("weight lag"), and the post argues the real instability comes from a second source: the rollout engine and the trainer compute different numbers for the same weights ("kernel mismatch"). Align the kernels bit for bit and plain, unclipped importance sampling is enough. In the NVIDIA Research and Inferact paper "REINFORCE Zero", the peak-to-median importance-weight deviation is 1.50x with aligned kernels and 442.54x with standard kernels. Reported results: stable for 20,000 updates, learning at policy lag 64, and 2.40x the training-step throughput of synchronous training. Zero-mismatch recipes are published for DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash, Kimi K3.1 and AMD MI355X, with code in the Inferact/zero-mismatch-rl repository. The numbers come from the vLLM team and its paper authors, not an independent replication. Author: vLLM team (Inferact, NVIDIA Research authors) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 11 min read. Source: https://blog.vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-06-zero-mismatch-async-rl

- GitHub: inside "Rubicon", the Git storage rebuilt for coding agents — GitHub engineers explain why agents change what a busy repository looks like and how they rebuilt storage for it. Their internal tests are 5,000 concurrent agents sending 7,200 pushes per second to one repository while reads stay fast; today's busiest repositories see at most dozens of writes per second. The design insight is that only reference updates need agreement. Packs live in Azure Blob Storage, references and the pack manifest live in a replicated compare-and-swap log (Ref-CAS), disposable "virtual replicas" serve traffic, and separate compaction nodes merge packs. Git clients see no change. The load figures come from GitHub's internal testing, not production. This is the infrastructure side of the argument that agents change how software teams and platforms work. Author: Karthik Raman and Brian Celenza, GitHub engineering · Format: deep dive · Length: 16 min read. Source: https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/inside-githubs-new-git-storage-architecture/

- Hightouch: a cheap "decision model" that asks Opus for help matches Opus at lower cost — Hightouch tested whether fast decision models (Jev, GPT-6 Luna) can grade identity-resolution results, meaning whether merged customer records belong to one real person. Out of the box they scored 95.6% (Jev) and 97.3% (Luna) against 99.0% for Claude Opus 5.5, at $0.05-$0.10 per 1,000 identities against $3.86 for Opus. The useful finding is calibration. Jev's mistakes cluster near 50% confidence, so sending only its uncertain cases (30-90%, 22% of volume) to Opus gave 98.8% accuracy at $0.88 per 1,000. Luna's errors came with high confidence, so routing barely helped it. The "4x cheaper" headline holds on the easy set. On the harder set the same hybrid scored 95.3% vs Opus alone at 95.1%, at $2.94 vs $5.39, which is nearer 1.8x. The test uses synthetic records for one imaginary town, and the post does not publish the data. Author: Albert Jin, Neil Desai and Delaney Mackenzie, Hightouch · Format: benchmark investigation · Length: about 15 min read. Source: https://hightouch.com/blog/llm-judges-identity-resolution

- Epoch AI: what Huawei's Horinger data center says about China's compute gap — Using satellite imagery, a cooling-equipment model and public filings, Epoch estimates the Horinger campus at about 286 MW of IT capacity in four dense buildings. The chip allocation rests on stated assumptions (76% rack occupancy, a four-month lag from sale to deployment, no export-controlled Nvidia chips) and the post shows its calculations. ## Prompting and context techniques

- Reddit r/PromptEngineering: which prompt lines survive an A/B test — A practitioner follow-up collects answers to "which line in your prompts have you never tested?", including three that came with tests. In one test, "avoid clichés" was run on 15 identical copywriting briefs and the cliches stayed. What removed them was a banned-phrase list of the exact words seen ("elevate", "seamless") plus one example paragraph in the target voice. "Be concise" turned out to cut only the closing summary, so "no closing summary" does the same job. "Think step by step" in an image-prompt generator added a paragraph of reasoning that the user then deleted by hand. The proposed rule is that a line naming a quality gets an A/B test on 10 or more inputs before it stays, while a line naming a concrete thing (a word, a length, a format) usually survives. These are single practitioners' tests, not controlled studies, but the rule is cheap to try. Author: Reddit user (post author), reporting tests by u/According-Stable4487 and others · Format: community thread with worked examples · Length: about 3 min read. Source: https://old.reddit.com/r/PromptEngineering/comments/1xohlji/7_prompt_lines_nobody_tests_3_got_measured_3/

- Also see the Anthropic agent-memory post in section 1: its prompting findings are that frontier models do better when told simply to save notes when asked or when something is clearly worth remembering and left to judge salience, whereas a fixed rule such as "record only failures" removed much of the gain.
