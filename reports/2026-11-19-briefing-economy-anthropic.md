## 1. Jobs, skills and professions

- Indeed Hiring Lab: "There is no AI jobpocalypse", but AI mentions in job postings doubled in a year — The Indeed AI Tracker (share of Indeed postings containing AI terms) rose from 3.6% in September 2025 to 7.2% in September 2026, and almost two-thirds of postings with AI in the job title are now outside traditional tech (truck drivers, retail sales, physical therapists). Against that, labor productivity has grown 2.1% a year since late 2019, in line with its long-run average, and 31% of workers say their employer has low-to-zero AI adoption. Only 45% of AI mentions in Glassdoor reviews are positive, down from 82% in 2019. The post is part of Indeed and Glassdoor's 2027 US Hiring and Worklife Trends Report, with a published methodology. Who is affected: US workers across occupations, employers adopting AI · Evidence: job-posting data and a worker survey (Indeed Hiring Lab, a vendor of the data) · Tension: AI language in postings doubled, yet no productivity lift and no clear job losses in the data. Source: https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/11/18/there-is-no-ai-jobpocalypse/

- US legal services: AI is shifting who does the work, not yet who is employed (arXiv, 17 Nov) — A difference-in-differences study across 89 federal districts finds a 6.6-percentage-point rise in non-prisoner self-representation relative to prisoner petitions by fiscal 2025, while lawyer employment shows no clear post-2022 shift toward client firms. The largest law firms reported 13% revenue growth in 2025 on nearly flat billable hours, and lower-priced firms saw stronger demand growth. The authors note that much of the reallocation predates generative AI, and legal-AI benchmarks still show failures on multi-step work. A first-version preprint, not peer reviewed. Who is affected: lawyers and law firms, clients choosing between self-help and counsel · Evidence: working paper with a stated design and court data · Tension: clients substitute AI for lawyers (self-representation up) while premium firms grow revenue on flat hours. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23354

## 2. How teams work with agents

- KPMG UK ran 100+ AI agents through an audit of a FTSE 250 company; they sometimes asked better questions than the human team (European Business Magazine citing the Financial Times, 18 Nov) — "Project Exponential" took about four months to build; agents gathered information, analysed and documented data, drafted conclusions and coordinated with each other. An audit partner signed off the exercise, but it was not used for the company's formal accounts, and an auditor had to step in when one agent mis-matched regulatory requirements to documents. KPMG plans to extend it to as many as 20 larger audits and says the technology is not mature enough for thousands of engagements. The article is a secondary report of the FT story, which sits behind a paywall; the findings are KPMG's own account. Who is affected: auditors and the accountancy profession · Evidence: company account via tier-1 report (secondhand) · Tension: head of audit says agents went deeper than humans, yet human intervention was still needed and the work was not used for the real audit. Source: https://europeanbusinessmagazine.com/ai-agents-better-questions-kpmg/

- GitHub Copilot's AI reviewer raised maintainers' workload while speeding review of human pull requests (arXiv, 17 Nov) — Using the 2025 introduction of Copilot Reviewer as a natural experiment on open-source repositories, the authors find an initial sharp increase in pull-request submissions and review workload; over time human contributions fall while bot-generated PRs keep rising. Survival analysis shows faster review of human PRs but slower review and merge of bot PRs. Their conclusion is that AI review raises review efficiency yet worsens the maintenance burden. A first-version preprint on open-source projects, not company teams. Who is affected: open-source maintainers and teams adopting AI code review · Evidence: working paper (natural experiment, survival analysis) · Tension: review gets faster per human PR while total maintenance load grows. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22793
