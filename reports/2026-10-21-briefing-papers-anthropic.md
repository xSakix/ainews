## Papers worth a deep dive

- Testing the Planning Claims of LLM-Based Agents against Formal Semantics — Each task is posed in natural language but checked against a hidden PDDL model that decides whether every action is legal, whether the goal is reached and whether a plan exists. Across five domains, four models, six prompting strategies and three agent architectures, the best setup solves 78% of solvable tasks offline but only 40% online, where agents mostly act without inspecting the state. Unsolvable tasks are recognised in under 10% of episodes, and heavy prompting strategies (CoT, reflection, critics) cost 5 to 22 times the tokens with no clear gain; the model matters more than the strategy or architecture. A direct test of "agents can plan" against a verifier rather than an LLM judge, and a result the prompting-technique crowd should read. Authors: not stated in the API record (see abstract page) · First submitted: 2026-10-15 · Artefacts: two new domains released with the paper · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22469

- Beyond Calibration: Temporal LLM Confidence Assessment Across Languages and Local Settings — Borrowing meta-memory theory, the paper separates pre-solve confidence (feeling of knowing) from post-solve confidence (judgement of learning) in two frontier LLMs. The cheap pre-solve signal is weakly calibrated; post-solve confidence is better; calibration transfers from English to translated Marathi but collapses on a new benchmark of genuinely local Marathi facts, and hallucinated answers keep high confidence after generation. Metacognition tested with human-psychology categories, and a reminder that translated benchmarks overstate non-English reliability. Authors: not stated in API record · First submitted: 2026-10-20 · Artefacts: new Marathi local-knowledge benchmark (release not confirmed) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27702

- Are Linguistic and Arithmetic Circuits Dissociated in LLMs? — Following neurolinguistic evidence that language and arithmetic dissociate in human brains, the authors extract circuits for grammatical and arithmetic tasks in five LLMs and measure overlap and causal effects. Circuit similarity is explained mostly by task domain, not by shared vocabulary or syntax, suggesting largely separate circuits for knowing language and doing maths. Interpretability set against neuroscience, with controls for the obvious confounds. Authors: not stated in API record · First submitted: 2026-10-20 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27790

- Do Knowledge and Reasoning Oriented Benchmarks Induce Stable Representation Signatures in LLMs? — A careful negative result. Linear probes separate a "knowledge" from a "reasoning" MMLU subject split in five 7B–31B models (macro-F1 0.78–0.81), but prompt text predicts the split almost as well (activation advantage only 0.017–0.070, not significant). After lexical adjustment no transferable knowledge-versus-reasoning signature remains, and two base-training trajectories show no common direction. Useful caution for anyone building internal monitors or "reasoning detectors". Authors: not stated in API record · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: project page https://parsa-ra.github.io/lmbehave/ · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24029


- Selling in the Wild: Benchmark and Randomized Field Evidence for a Sales Agent Trained on Commercial Conversations — A randomized trial on 100 live websites (1 May to 30 September 2026) with 14.8 million visitors. A sales agent fine-tuned on 4.6 million consented conversations raised revenue per visitor by 10.0% (95% CI +5.1 to +14.9) against no agent, and engaged conversion by 21.6% over a frontier-model agent with the same tools. Against human associates it was non-inferior (11.26% vs 11.20% conversion) at 1.8% of the cost per conversation. Rare field-experiment evidence on what agents do to a profession, though the authors are the agent's developers. Authors: not stated in API record (the Javo agent's developers) · First submitted: 2026-10-18 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25461

- Explore or Confirm? LLM Agents Misallocate Measurements Under Noise — In a simulated assay campaign built on scrambled real protein datasets (GB1, PhoQ) so models cannot answer from memory, five LLM agents misallocate a fixed budget in both directions: three over-confirm by re-testing, one under-confirms and trusts single noisy readings and ends below a basic scripted strategy. A list of untried candidates reduces over-confirmation; summaries or landscape descriptions barely help. Gives a concrete measure of how agents reason under uncertainty before they are trusted with lab budgets. Authors: not stated in API record · First submitted: 2026-10-18 · Artefacts: Wellocate benchmark and RL environment released · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25111

- Give the Model a Finger: Developmentally Aligned Evaluation of Counting in Vision-Language Models — Counting resembles children's development: exact on small sets, estimation on large ones. The authors give nine VLMs child-like tools (pointing, tagging) on 682 images. The exact-counting range varies from 3 to 150 dots by model; tagging mostly fails to raise accuracy, but it exposes an auditable trace showing that precise pointers read answers off the tags and that models rarely revise after seeing their own tags. Developmental psychology used as an evaluation method. Authors: not stated in API record · First submitted: 2026-10-20 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27756

- From Stories to Features: Scaling Concept Vectors for AI Alignment & Interpretability — Instead of recovering the whole feature space with sparse autoencoders, the authors build 1,036 alignment-relevant concept directions (honesty, deception and so on) as differences of mean activations on 2M synthetic concept/antagonist story pairs, which they release. Vectors generalise to chat and web text across six models from three families, share a geometry across models, and steer behaviour; they are applied to jailbreaks and reasoning. A practical alternative to SAEs with data the reader can use. Authors: not stated in API record · First submitted: 2026-10-17 · Artefacts: 2M stories released · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24556

## Also notable

- LLM Evolution in a Simple Simulated Market — Populations of 20 LLM agents differing only in system prompt are bankrupted and replaced by mutated copies of winners; a market paying for accuracy yields 94% accuracy, a per-token charge collapses accuracy to 10% though only tokens were priced. (First submitted 2026-10-16; code not stated.) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24033
- The One-Pixel of Reasoning: One Truthful Relation Is Enough to Mislead LLM Agents — One source-grounded sentence qualifying an implicit warrant flips initially correct answers in 54.9% (proprietary) and 80.0% (open-weight) of cases, best-of-10, across 18 models; multi-agent debate raises team error from 50.0% to 59.5%. (2026-10-17.) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24873
- Verbalising Model's Computation for Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness Detection (VERITAS) — Layer-wise Jacobian projections track how reasoning anchors emerge during a CoT; a classifier on them beats an LLM judge by 28.4% balanced accuracy on FaithCoT-Bench and BonaFide. (2026-10-16.) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23275
- LEV: Thinking Fast and Deep with Looped Decision Models — Reruns a looped model's layers to think deeper without generating tokens; a 2.6B model rises from 63.2% to 76.2% with four loops. Code at https://github.com/OpenLAIR/lev; high on Hugging Face Daily Papers (454 upvotes). (2026-10-14.) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20951
- After the Attacker Leaves: Benchmarking Carryover Attacks on LLM Agents (CarryoverBench) — A one-time prompt injection planted in long-term memory files or scheduled tasks re-fires across sessions; 101 attack tasks, five frontier models. (2026-10-20.) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28425
- Learning Perception to Action Rules through Reinforcement Learning for Theory-of-Mind Reasoning — GRPO-trained if-then rules linking perception, belief and action beat prompting and SFT on four ToM benchmarks, and rules from models of 3B or more transfer to GPT-4o-mini. (2026-10-14.) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19796
