On Friday morning an engineer on a product team in Walldorf opens a spreadsheet that arrived the evening before. It has one row per role in her department and an empty column headed "options". Her manager has asked every team lead to fill it in by Monday. She reads the headline on her phone first, at the coffee machine, and then reads it again. The number in it is 10,000. Her own team has eleven people, and the cloud service they maintain has had a good year.

*The scenes that open and close this article are illustrations; the people in them are invented.*

## What was reported, and what was said

Bloomberg reported on Thursday 26 November, citing unnamed sources, that SAP is drawing up plans to cut at least 10,000 jobs. Euronews, which followed the next day, put that at about 9 percent of the company's global workforce. According to Bloomberg's account as Euronews relays it, SAP has asked all departments to identify possible cuts, with product development, engineering and sales singled out, and the figure could change after talks with employee representatives.

SAP has not confirmed a number. It told Euronews that "the business environment in which we operate is changing rapidly through AI" and that it is "continuously reviewing how our organization and workforce need to evolve". It said it expected roles and functions to change over time, that "no decisions have been made regarding any further measures", and that it does not comment on speculation. So the cut is a report from unnamed sources and a plan that may still change, and not an announcement.

The company's own figures give the number a size. SAP's third-quarter statement, published on 21 October, counts 113,076 full-time equivalents at the end of September, up from 110,730 a year earlier; 805 of the increase came with acquisitions. Ten thousand jobs against that base is the 9 percent that Euronews cites.

## A company that is growing

The timing is odd, and the reporting says so. In the third quarter, cloud revenue rose 26 percent to €6,668 million, and profit after tax rose 11 percent to €2,269 million. It was the first quarter in which SAP's total revenue passed €10 billion. Chief executive Christian Klein called it "an outstanding quarter".

The statement also carries a caution. It says cloud growth benefited from "several quarter-specific effects, mainly a multi-quarter revenue catch-up" from customers that had been in financial distress and returned. The growth is real, but part of it may not repeat. A company that booked a net restructuring credit of €3 million in the quarter is not, on these figures, cutting because its results force it to.

What SAP describes is a change in where it spends. Euronews lists the purchases that go with it. Prior Labs, which builds AI models for business data, and Dremio, a data-integration company, closed in July. TechWolf, a Belgian company that uses AI to map employees' skills and work, closed this month. Reltio, a data-management firm, was bought in May. The cuts that the report describes are asked of the teams that build and sell the software, while the money goes to companies that supply AI components.

## The second round

This would not be SAP's first. On 23 January 2024 the company announced a transformation program affecting about 8,000 positions, with a stated focus on Business AI, and projected restructuring expenses of around €2 billion. Most of the positions, it said, would be covered by voluntary leave programs, with internal re-skilling for the rest. On 22 July 2024 SAP raised the scope to 9,000 to 10,000 positions, a majority still through voluntary leave, and said the program was expected to conclude in early 2025.

The tone changed in July 2026. Bloomberg reported on 2 July an internal email that told staff the company would "exclusively focus new hiring on selected profiles only, mainly core AI roles", and suspended internal travel not linked to AI development. A SAP spokesperson said the company was "prioritizing investments in AI-related capabilities, talent, and technologies". The Next Web, reporting the memo, noted that SAP shares were down about 33 percent for the year on fears that AI will erode demand for its traditional software.

Read in order, the three events show a company that moved from a program mostly absorbed by volunteers, to a freeze on hiring outside AI, to a reported plan that asks every department for cuts. The company has said nothing about whether the latest round would again rest on voluntary departures. The 2024 round was described as "voluntary leave" programs; the reports so far do not use that term.

## Where SAP sits among its peers

Euronews places the report among similar moves. It says Oracle's workforce shrank by about 21,000 in the year to May as the company restructured, partly in response to AI adoption, and that in October Microsoft said it was offering voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees to contain costs and help fund its AI investments. Those are Euronews's figures, and the pieces differ: one is a reduction, one is an offer. Euronews adds that Klein is pushing to build AI into SAP's finance, supply-chain and payroll tools after some customers questioned whether earlier AI products were worth the cost.

That sentence holds the tension in the story. The AI products that SAP is reorganising around have not convinced all of the customers who pay for them, and the report puts product development and engineering, the teams that would have to change that, among those asked to find cuts. No source shows whether AI tools will do the work of the people who leave. The sources show spending: the purchases, the freeze and the request for a list.

## What is still open

Three things are unknown. The figure is unconfirmed, and SAP says no decisions are made. The mix of voluntary leaves, retraining and dismissals is not reported. And employee representatives, whose talks could change the number, have not spoken in the coverage. In Germany those talks are a legal step, which is why a figure can leak long before a decision.

On Monday the spreadsheet in Walldorf will be due. The engineer types into the empty column for the first time. She starts with the role that has been vacant since spring, the one her manager was told not to refill.
