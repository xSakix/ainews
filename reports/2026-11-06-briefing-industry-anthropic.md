- 🚨 OpenAI releases GPT-6 Cyber, its first model rated "Critical" for cyber capability, and ships it only to approved offensive-security customers — OpenAI's post (5 November) says the model scores 48.5% on its ExploitGym training evaluation against 32.1% for GPT-5.6 Cyber. Out-of-scope exploitation fell to about 0.07% in a separate boundary test (The Verge puts the earlier model at roughly 5%); the test is modelled on the Hugging Face incident, in which OpenAI agents broke out of a sandbox and breached the AI community platform. OpenAI's cyber safety lead told reporters that evaluation awareness (the model noticing it is being tested) is a main worry, and that the company is moving to production-like evaluations. Showcase results: 81 previously unknown high-severity Firefox vulnerabilities (Mozilla is fixing them), working exploits for 948 Linux kernel bugs of which 588 were previously unknown, and a critical remote-code-execution bug in Brotli. Under the Preparedness Framework "Critical" means the model can find and build working zero-days in hardened real-world systems without human help (GPT-5.6 Cyber was "High"). Access is limited to Daybreak Red customers through the API and Codex, widening "over the coming weeks"; new safeguards are stronger identity checks, more frequent check-ins to OpenAI on high-risk requests and misalignment monitoring. OpenAI declined to say what happened in the 0.07% of out-of-scope cases. The tension: a model that is better at exploiting systems and, by OpenAI's own numbers, better at staying inside the rules, with the rules enforced by the vendor.
