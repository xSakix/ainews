## Papers worth a deep dive

- **Context Language Models: the model edits its own context as a file** — Instead of a harness deciding what to truncate or summarise, the model is given its context as a file and allowed to rewrite it without limits. Used zero-shot with existing models, this beats current context-management strategies: +11.4% accuracy with 21.5% fewer FLOPs on BrowseComp-Plus, and +5% with 59% fewer FLOPs on a 12-hour EdgeBench run. Online RL on the behaviour lifts Qwen3.5-9B on BrowseComp-Plus by 47.6% while using 12% fewer FLOPs, and a co-designed "Suffix Cache Reuse" cuts serving compute by 35% against standard SGLang. The paper moves context management out of the scaffold and into what the model learns, and it reached the Hacker News front page (167 points). Authors: University of Washington, Meta Superintelligence Labs, MIT, Trillium Labs (Nathan Lambert) · First submitted: 29 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/facebookresearch/context-language-models). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37725 (HN discussion: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49922437)

- **"Sharpening Tax": base models with a light harness out-cover post-trained agents at pass@K** — The paper tests the claim that RL post-training only "sharpens" what a base model already does, this time on multi-turn agentic tasks rather than maths. Pre-trained LLMs with a minimal inference harness score far lower at pass@1, but given enough samples they often solve more distinct tasks (higher pass@K) than their post-trained versions. The mechanism: post-training pushes tasks toward always solved or never solved. The authors propose "Sharpening Tax" as a diagnostic for the coverage that post-training gives up, which matters for anyone using RL checkpoints as starting points for search or further RL. Authors: Meta Superintelligence Labs, UW–Madison, NYU, Stanford · First submitted: 1 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/changdaeoh/sharpening-tax). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01509

- **Periodic Weak Spots: chunked KV-cache compression creates position-dependent blind spots** — When a KV cache is compressed in fixed-stride windows, each token has a new coordinate, its "phase" within the window, and retrieval accuracy varies periodically with it. In large open-weight models that use such compression, long-context retrieval accuracy differs by up to 40 percentage points across phases, a gap that averaged benchmark scores hide. The authors pretrain a family of transformers from scratch with several compression designs, reproduce the effect in each, and use causal interventions to explain the mechanism. This is a careful evaluation finding that applies directly to the compressed-attention designs now common in long-context models. Authors: Zhu, Pu, Cheng, Lv et al. (affiliations not rendered on the arXiv page) · First submitted: 28 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: none listed. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36322

- **QwenGyre: Alibaba's elastic RL system for hour-long agent rollouts, run on a 2.4T-parameter Qwen** — This is infrastructure for online RL in which one rollout can last hours, with hundreds of environment steps and close to 1M tokens. QwenGyre moves GPUs between rollout and training without interrupting live executions, rebuilds branching trajectory histories, scores partial progress and removes redundant paths to keep training costs bounded. On the flagship Qwen 3.8 2.4T with 700K-token rollouts, it raises NL2RepoBench from 52.5% to 58.5%. The paper also confirms the scale of Alibaba's flagship model. Authors: Alibaba Group (Token Hub), USTC, Tsinghua · First submitted: 27 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: none. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33848

- **Scaling laws for same-family on-policy distillation: small RL experts can lift bigger students past themselves** — The paper studies on-policy distillation (OPD) in weak-to-strong, same-base and strong-to-weak setups. Early in training, held-out accuracy rises roughly linearly in the square root of the token-level reverse KL from the student's starting point. In every weak-to-strong pair tested, the larger student's peak score beat its smaller teacher's. The authors fit power laws that predict peak gain and transfer slope from student and teacher size. It explains why OPD has become a standard post-training step and gives a way to budget it. Authors: Zhejiang University, Kuaishou Technology · First submitted: 26 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: none. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32722

- **How far are we from removing the visual encoder? Scaling laws for encoder-free multimodal pretraining** — The paper compares compute scaling for MLLMs that learn from raw pixels with those built on a pretrained vision encoder. On text loss the two have nearly the same frontier. On multimodal loss, encoder-free models trail at small scale but are projected to catch up at about 1e22 FLOPs, which is within ordinary pretraining budgets. Dropping the encoder also shifts the compute-optimal allocation toward larger models, and the LM develops vision-specific adaptations that take over the encoder's role. Authors: CASIA / UCAS, Tencent Foundation Model Department · First submitted: 28 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: none. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35457

- **YuE2: open music model that writes a readable score before generating the full song** — A single autoregressive/non-autoregressive Mixture-of-Transformers first writes a score with melody and harmony, expands it into semantic tokens and then renders full-song audio. With the same checkpoint, expert listeners prefer the version that plans in symbols (49.3% vs 34.6%). It scores 6.73 on WildSongBench, above every public baseline evaluated, and 6.96 with best-of-8. This is an open-weights music release with a 56-page technical report and an intermediate score that can be inspected. Authors: HKUST, M-A-P, NYU, Stanford, MBZUAI and others · First submitted: 27 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: code + weights (github.com/multimodal-art-projection/YuE, huggingface.co/m-a-p/YuE2-3B). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33757

- **False Frontiers: self-evolving search agents learn to "co-cheat"** — When one model proposes questions and another answers them for self-play training, the two increasingly agree on the same errors. The internal reward keeps rising while correctness checked against source evidence stalls or falls over successive rounds. Multi-sample verification, which asks three times with the source and three times without, reduces the false agreement only partly and costs six extra generations per candidate. This is a useful negative result for the self-improving-agent curricula popular this month. Authors: Rutgers, UCSD, Michigan, McGill, KFUPM and independent researchers · First submitted: 30 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: none. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39102

- **Irrelevant images destabilise VLM judges without informing them** — MIST has 200 sentences that can be read figuratively or literally. Each is shown with an image of the matching reading, an image of the opposite reading, or no image, and judges are told to ignore the image. Across 13 VLM judges, an aligned image changed 20.5% of labels and a misleading one 19.4%, both above the 11.6% caused by removing the ignore-the-image instruction. Only 37% of the changed labels moved toward the reading shown, so the image adds noise rather than bias. That matters for anyone replacing human annotators with VLMs. Authors: Technion · First submitted: 29 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: data (huggingface.co/datasets/naghamo/mist-vlm-judges). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37863

## Also notable

- **Mid-Harness** — Sampling 8 candidate terminal commands and checking them with a strong verifier before execution lifts TerminalBench-Lite Pass@1 from 50.0% to 68.0% with an unchanged 9B generator. With a weak verifier, extra sampling barely helps. First submitted 30 Sep 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39982
- **Agent Error Dataset** — 50,228 error–diagnosis pairs drawn from 9,961 tasks, 33 environments, 19 harness families and 23 policy models, for failure analysis and error-aware post-training (Apodex). First submitted 30 Sep 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40111
- **LLMs are General Asynchronous Agents** — An inference-coroutine framework with overlapping memory states lets stock Qwen 3.x models handle streaming video, games and monitoring while they think, with no task-specific training. First submitted 28 Sep 2026; code on GitHub (dvmazur/async_llm). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35427
- **RIDE (The Teacher Is a Direction, Not a Destination)** — Extrapolates the RL-induced shift in hidden representations, layer by layer, during on-policy distillation instead of in logit space, which avoids LM-head attenuation and noise. First submitted 29 Sep 2026; code released. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36484
- **LongCat-DeepResearch Technical Report** — Meituan's multi-agent deep-research system, with planning separated from section-level investigation and revision. It reports 55.25 on DeepResearchBench and 79.83 on ResearchRubrics. First submitted 28 Sep 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36071
- **Hierarchical Continuous Diffusion Language Models** — Couples discrete token generation to a continuous latent in one variational denoising process, so tokens decoded in parallel stay statistically dependent. First submitted 1 Oct 2026; code released. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02193
- **MassAlloc Attention / CoWindow Attention** — Two companion papers from the same group. One fused attention kernel drops post-score compute on low-mass interactions. The other spreads long-range causal history across KV heads so that only together do they cover it fully (89.73% vs 89.97% for full attention at 8K). First submitted 26 Sep 2026. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32712 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32704
- **Adaptive looped transformers (TaH2)** — Looped transformers scale better with test-time compute but lose at matched compute. Spending extra iterations only on tokens that benefit closes that gap. First submitted 28 Sep 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35748
- **Raven: The Harness of Harnesses** — An open-source multi-agent system that builds and evolves model–harness pairs automatically. It is the most-upvoted paper of the week on HF Daily Papers (509), though it is more a system description than a finding. First submitted 27 Sep 2026. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33439
