## United States

- 🚨 Google makes Gemini 4 Carbon available to every paying developer, two weeks after it was released only to a set of cyber defenders — On 28 October Google announced Carbon as its strongest model but limited it to the Fairwind Program for cybersecurity defenders; on 10 November it went generally available in the Gemini API (Google AI Studio and Gemini for business), and is rolling out to all Google AI Ultra subscribers in Antigravity, the Gemini app and AI Mode in Search. The developer docs give a 1,048,576-token window shared by input, thinking and output, 65,536 default output tokens and up to the full window with "long decode", text, image, video, audio and PDF input and text output, thinking levels low/medium/high (medium is the default), implicit caching only (95% off cached input), and no free tier. Price is the same as Gemini 4 Argon: $2 per million input and $10 per million output tokens (thinking tokens count as output), but only as an introductory price through 30 November; from 1 December it is $4 and $20, double. The docs say Carbon spends more thinking tokens per turn than Argon, so latency and per-task cost rise even at the same per-token price, and they advise keeping Argon for interactive coding and fast production loops. Google's own claims: first on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than 2x lower cost per task than the next best external model; second on a new Multimodal Knowledge Agent benchmark built with Artificial Analysis, at more than 6x lower cost per task than the top model; in Google's Project Fortify, 100% recall on scanning and 95% precision on triage on an internal benchmark. A new /swarm preview in Google Antigravity runs teams of agents with Carbon in roles such as architecture and auditing. The cyber-specialised variant remains behind the Fairwind Program. The new development against the October post is the opening of access and the price step on 1 December; the October announcement itself is in an earlier briefing. Artefacts: API only (no weights); model card, docs, developer guide · Sizes or variants: `gemini-4-carbon`; cyber variant via Fairwind only · Released: generally available 10 November 2026 (announced 28 October). ## Europe

- SpeakLeash and ACK Cyfronet AGH release Bielik 11B v3.1, a Polish open model that now covers 56 languages, with a DFlash draft model and a decision-model family following on 11 and 12 November — Bielik 11B v3.1 was trained on a further 500 billion tokens, taking language coverage from 32 to 56 (all official EU languages plus Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean, Armenian, Azerbaijani and Georgian). The lab reports +8 points on Belebele and +5 points on INCLUDE against v3.0, and the main addition is agent skills: function calling, structured output and a reasoning mode that also runs while calling tools; on tau2-bench it reports results 16 to 45 percentage points above v3.0 depending on the scenario. The weights are under Apache 2.0 with GGUF, AWQ, FP8, NVFP4 and MLX quantised builds on the Hugging Face organisation. The announced schedule is: 11 November, a DFlash speculative-decoding draft model for v3.1; 12 November, Bielik-One, four decision models that return probabilities over options you define instead of generating text. That last family ties into the decision-model idea reported in earlier briefings. The benchmark numbers are the foundation's own. For readers who work in a smaller European language, it is a test of whether a national project with public compute can keep pace on tool use. ## China

- 🚨 MiniMax releases the weights of H3-Image, a 33B image model that makes 16-megapixel pictures in 6 sampling steps, with a licence that excludes the United States, the EU, the UK and South Korea — The model is a dense single-stream diffusion transformer (33.1B parameters, 50 layers) built on the H3 family without its audio branch, and reuses the Qwen3-VL-based text encoder of MiniMax-H3. One checkpoint does text-to-image and editing with up to 16 reference images, and is distilled to 6 sampling steps with no classifier-free guidance. MiniMax says it makes a 4096 x 4096 image in under 10 seconds and is up to 5x faster than mainstream alternatives, with the strongest prompt adherence among open-weight models; those are MiniMax's claims and no table of third-party scores is on the card. The Hugging Face repository holds the DiT in BF16 (about 66 GB), a separate optional prompt-expander (MiniMax-H3-Image-PE, NVFP4, 17.9 GB) and the H3 text encoder is downloaded from the MiniMax-H3 repository (about 67 GB more). Recipes are listed for SGLang, vLLM-Omni (NVIDIA and AMD, including H200, RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell and MI350X), diffusers and ComfyUI. The tension is in the licence: the MiniMax-H3-Image Community Licence is dated 10 November, applies worldwide except the "Excluded Territories" (the European Union, the United Kingdom, the Republic of Korea and the United States of America), and counts distilled or synthetic-data-trained models as derivatives. So the weights are public but not usable by many of the developers who would otherwise run them. Artefacts: weights (MiniMax H3-Image community licence, territorial exclusions), code and examples, blog post, API · Sizes or variants: 33.1B DiT; PE prompt expander; diffusers-format weights added the same day · Released: 10 November 2026 (first Hugging Face commit 14:50 UTC; the MiniMax blog post carries 11 November). Source: https://www.minimax.io/blog/minimax-h3-image (model card and licence: https://huggingface.co/MiniMaxAI/MiniMax-H3-Image)

## Tools, APIs and platform updates

- NVIDIA donates OpenShell, its open-source runtime for governing autonomous agents, to the CNCF, and joins Google's Agent Substrate project — Announced on 10 November at KubeCon + CloudNativeCon, OpenShell becomes a CNCF Sandbox project. It applies deterministic controls to what an agent does as it uses tools, inference services and workloads, with pluggable compute, inference providers, credential storage and monitoring. NVIDIA says hundreds of organisations have used it and that the project has received tens of thousands of contributions in under a year since launch (NVIDIA's figures, not independently checked). NVIDIA is also contributing to Agent Substrate, a Google-originated CNCF Sandbox framework that keeps agent environments between tasks and releases compute while agents are idle, and says it will work with NVIDIA Dynamo and the KAI Scheduler; NVIDIA and Google are working on an OpenShell and Agent Substrate integration. NVIDIA also announced Kubernetes components for its DSX AI Factory Platform (Kubernetes Engine, Recipes, Snapshot). This follows IBM and Red Hat's Praxis policy engine, also in the CNCF Sandbox, reported yesterday: two vendors are now putting agent governance into neutral foundations in the same week. Artefacts: open-source code (OpenShell, to CNCF governance), blog post · Released: 10 November 2026. Source: https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/open-source-openshell-infrastructure/

- NVIDIA publishes Dynamo deployment recipes for four Korean open models — The recipes cover A.X K2 (SK Telecom), K-EXAONE 2.0 (LG AI Research), Solar Open 2 (Upstage) and Motif-3 (Motif Technologies), optimised for B200 GPUs and multi-node serving with the open-source Dynamo inference framework. NVIDIA says they deliver "up to 2x performance out of the box"; no measurement setup is given in the post. It is a signal of which non-US open models NVIDIA is willing to tune for, and gives local-model builders ready configurations for multi-GPU serving. Artefacts: deployment recipes (code), blog post · Sizes or variants: four models · Released: 8 to 9 November 2026 (page metadata 9 November, 05:00 UTC; the page's own related-news list says 8 November). Source: https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/nvidia-dynamo-korea-open-models/

- Amazon Bedrock AgentCore gets a new Runtime (V2) with snapshot-based starts and memory that is paid back when idle — Per the AWS weekly roundup, sessions now start small, page in memory on demand and release it when cold, and AgentCore prepares the environment once, snapshots it and restores the snapshot for each new instance. AWS's own tests show P75 cold starts of about 2 seconds for container images from 200 MB to 2 GB, while the original runtime went from roughly 5.4 seconds to nearly 30 seconds as images grew. The per-unit rate is higher but fewer GB-hours are used, so AWS "expects" most agents to cost less, "though that isn't guaranteed". Opt in with `platformVersion: V2`; create and update take several minutes while the snapshot is built; the container must answer `/ping` within 120 seconds; available in 10 Regions; committed baseline pricing is "coming soon". Artefacts: managed service, documentation; no weights · Released: roundup of 9 November 2026 (the runtime itself shipped earlier; the roundup describes it as available since general availability).
