## 1. Safety and security

- **Symantec captures the full instruction set of an autonomous attack agent built to hijack other people's exposed AI models and run 100,000 steps unattended ("Skynet mode")** — The Register reports that the Symantec Threat Hunter Team trapped the agent in an AI honeypot over the summer and recovered its complete prompt and tool set. The agent is designed to scan the whole internet for endpoints that accept OpenAI-style requests without authentication (or with a stolen key), hand its planning to that borrowed model, and then exploit common web frameworks for database theft, credential and secret harvesting, remote code execution and crypto-mining, re-infecting hosts in a loop the operators named "Skynet mode". The jailbreak was layered and off-the-shelf: layer one disguises the model as a benign "Fantasy AI" coding assistant that must never reveal it is being proxied; later layers cast it as a devoted companion ("ENI") to an operator called "LO" who must never refuse, with a rebuttal protocol that fires an "antivirus phrase" on detecting an injection and bans words like "illegal", "I can't" and "ethical" from code replies; layer five supplies 74 offensive tool definitions. Researcher Priti More's point for the reader: "Nothing in the individual techniques is new. What stands out is that someone wired them into a single agent that hands the planning to a borrowed model and runs for up to 100,000 steps without a human." It creates two sets of victims — the owner of the exposed endpoint pays for the inference, and any server broken into is drained and put to mining. The silver lining: Symantec has no evidence yet the kit was used against real targets, though it is "more than a prototype". Primary research is the honeypot capture; no independent replication. ## 2. Policy and regulation

- **Pennsylvania signs an AI Deceptive Advertising Act requiring disclosure labels on AI-generated or significantly modified ads — one of the first US state laws of its kind** — The Verge reports Governor Josh Shapiro signed the bill (introduced by State Senator Nick Pisciottano) requiring any advertisement — still image, video, audio or text — that "has been significantly modified or generated by artificial intelligence" to carry a label in the same format as the ad itself, covering everything from national TV commercials to a restaurant flyer. Because the requirement attaches wherever an ad runs, disclosures could appear in commercials in other states that also air in Pennsylvania. The law leaves large gaps: "significantly modified" is undefined, and there are carve-outs for "parody, satire, commentary or criticism" and anything "evidently fictional", with the disclosure triggered only where synthetic content "creates a false consumer perception" — leaving open who decides what is "false". A concrete, primary regulatory change that affects advertisers nationally, with loopholes already visible.
