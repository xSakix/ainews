- Andrew Ng on building AI agents (Stanford CS329Z fireside chat with Diyi Yang) — Ng (DeepLearning.AI, ex-Google Brain) says strong agent teams are separated from weaker ones by disciplined error analysis and evals-driven development, including knowing when to move from quick prototyping to a golden set with regression tests. - How to evaluate AI agents: benchmark design (Stanford CS329Z, Lecture 12) — John Yang, co-creator of SWE-bench, lays out a four-part framework for agent evaluation (request, environment, stopping criteria, scorer) and the properties of a good benchmark: challenging, discriminative, realistic, easy to run, easy to verify, interpretable, durable. His central caution is that scores depend on protocol choices such as harness, turn and cost limits and temperature, so the same model can score differently under different harnesses and these must be held constant to compare models fairly; making tasks harder can also make them less natural. A lecture, so it carries method rather than a new result. Channel: Stanford Online · Language: English · Length: 1:21 · Uploaded: 26 Nov 2026 · Signal: about 14.1K views. - µP and Muon: scaling optimizers to infinite width (Stanford CS312, Lecture 4) — Tatsunori Hashimoto derives µP through abc-parameterization so the optimal learning rate transfers across width, explains why the neural tangent kernel limit is stable but learns no features (the description says it scores about 0% on a word-analogy example), then reframes µP as steepest descent in an operator-norm ball, which leads to Muon and its Newton-Schulz iteration. He discusses evidence Muon works at scale and suggests its benefit may come from boosting small singular values. A first-principles lecture for readers who tune training runs.
