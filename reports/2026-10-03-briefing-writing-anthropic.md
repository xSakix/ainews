## 1. Deep dives and technical write-ups

- **turbopuffer explains why it is demoting the vector index in v3: "RIP, vector database"** — Engineer Dan Harrison walks through turbopuffer's storage design from v1 (an ID plus a vector, SPANN/SPFresh clustering over object storage) to today. His argument is that keying every document and index on the ANN cluster address forces block sizes of about 100–200 documents on every query plan, which is bad for aggregations and scans. The company is therefore making the ANN index "just another" secondary index. A concrete data point: moving full-text postings off cluster boundaries to fixed ~256 blocks made the index 10x smaller and queries up to 20x faster. The v3 benchmarks are not published yet (promised "over the coming weeks"), so the performance claims are still prospective. It is a useful read for anyone building RAG or search infrastructure, and a candid statement that vector-first design is a ceiling. Published 30 September 2026.
  Author: Dan Harrison, engineer at turbopuffer · Format: deep dive (vendor engineering blog) · Length: ~8 min
  Source: https://turbopuffer.com/blog/rip-vector-database

- **Red Hat and Meta engineers put an autotuned Helion kernel into vLLM's quantized linear backend** — Sean Chen (Red Hat) and Shangdi Yu (PyTorch, Meta) describe a single Helion GEMM that covers Standard, Split-K and Swap-AB variants, with the best variant and config chosen per shape by an autotuner. Dispatch is hybrid: Helion runs only for small token counts under CUDA Graph replay, and larger shapes fall back to CUTLASS or DeepGEMM. They report better end-to-end throughput than both defaults on Hopper across the models they tested, more than 10% for some workloads. The numbers are the authors' own and cover Hopper only. Together with the Meta TLX post from earlier this week, it shows the push to replace hand-written kernels with high-level DSLs plus autotuning. Published 2 October 2026.
  Author: Sean Chen (Red Hat) and Shangdi Yu (PyTorch, Meta) · Format: deep dive · Length: ~15 min
  Source: https://pytorch.org/blog/building-a-high-performance-and-portable-vllm-linear-backend-with-helion/

- **Earendil on why Pi now ships MCP in core, after saying "no MCP" for a year** — The Pi agent team explains the reversal. MCP has improved, but the deciding factor is that the changes needed to support it (an interpreter-style sandbox in which tool calls can be composed) are generally useful, and they also make the Jev-style models easier to use inside Pi. Their main remaining complaint is that MCP is hard to compose, and that even "codemode" only partly fixes this. It is a short design-rationale piece from a team that had publicly opposed the protocol, and the author is a project maintainer, not a neutral party. Published 29 September 2026.
  Author: Earendil Engineering (Pi maintainers) · Format: engineering write-up · Length: ~7 min
  Source: https://earendil.com/posts/you-said-no-mcp/

## 2. Essays and arguments

- **Ethan Mollick: "The Dot and the Swarm" — he got agent organisation wrong, and the Bitter Lesson explains why** — Mollick argues he was mistaken to expect that humans would need to design agent "org charts" carefully. Newer models organise themselves: given a three-team sketch, Codex with GPT-6 Astra Ultra spun up thirteen agents without further instruction. He says management exists largely to fix human problems (principal-agent conflicts, scattered information, costly communication) that agents mostly lack, though the Hugging Face incident shows those problems reappear between the swarm and its human principals. The evidence is anecdotal, and it is the opinion of a management professor who is also a heavy user and booster of the tools. Published 1 October 2026.
  Author: Ethan Mollick, Wharton professor (One Useful Thing) · Format: essay · Length: ~10 min
  Source: https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-dot-and-the-swarm

- **What is recursive self-improvement, and what would a ban on it even mean?** — Sarah (sarahhw) sorts the competing definitions of RSI, from "a blacksmith uses a hammer to make hammers" through "humans use AI in any way" to "AI replaces a researcher of a given level". She argues that any ban proposal has to say which of these it targets, since Amodei's "very carefully, if at all" and Ezra Klein's "go back to none of the code written by AI" imply very different lines. It is a clarifying piece for a debate that is currently conducted with undefined terms. Opinion and policy analysis, cross-posted from her Substack. Published 2 October 2026.
