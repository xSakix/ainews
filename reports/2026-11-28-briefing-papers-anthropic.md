## Papers worth a deep dive

- Language models steer on injected doubt but do not notice their own errors — Four open-weight models (Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, Phi-4) were run over 15,200 math attempts, which yielded 2,017 reasoning errors inside each model's capability. Verbalized stepwise confidence does not separate a wrong step from a sound one (within-position AUROC 0.500 to 0.561). At a model's first error the confidence stays at 95.1% to 99.9%, and a drop of 10 points or more shows up on fewer than 9% of errors in Llama and under 1.6% in the other three. Yet injecting a "10% confident" tag at the error step raises later correction by 3.7 to 22.5 points over a format-matched 95% tag (up to 38.5% with an instruction to rethink). Injecting doubt onto correct steps makes models discard valid work, losing up to 22.8 points. So the bottleneck for confidence-triggered self-correction is detection, not repair. Limitation: 7 to 15B open models on math only, with a single author and no frontier model. Practical reading for prompt designers: "state your confidence at every step" does not give a trustworthy error signal in these models. Authors: Daniel Theron (single author; institution not stated in arXiv metadata) · First submitted: 2026-11-23 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31228

- Seven open models consult an expert even when consulting guarantees a lower score — In a multiple-choice task with 2,291 questions and 54 combinations of expert accuracy and consultation cost, at the highest waiting cost all 7 open-weight models still asked the expert although a correct expert answer scored below an independent wrong answer. Help-seeking rates ranged from 20.8% to 94.1% across models, and they varied between conditions that had the same expected consultation score, so the models do not compute the expected score. Fine-tuning cut decision regret by 59% to 87% on held-out questions within the training range but raised inconsistency at matched expected scores. External routing, with candidate answers held fixed, improved scores by 0.180 to 0.273 per question. A study of metacognitive decision-making (knowing when to ask) rather than of calibration alone. Limitation: open-weight models only, multiple-choice with simulated expert and cost, and no human baseline. Authors: Jiahao You, Ziye Jia, Chao Dong, Fuhui Zhou, Qihui Wu · First submitted: 2026-11-23 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29981

- Multimodal models know the game's rules but answer the bare screenshot by real-world physics — RuleCraft has 1,240 image questions over 155 Minecraft rules whose outcomes were verified in the game engine, each asked as text, as an image with the game named, and as a bare image. Six widely used models state the rules and recognize the game, yet on the bare image they pick the real-world outcome. Naming the game raises accuracy on rules where the game departs from reality by 8.2 to 19.8 points on the main set and 33.8 to 52.7 points on a hard stress set. Reasoning traces state the game's rule and then answer by reality. One neutral sentence asking the model to apply "the rules of the world shown" recovers much of the gap, which is a prompt the reader can try. Limitation: Minecraft plus two more games, and the models are not named in the abstract. The authors' own conclusion is that benchmarks that name the game measure knowledge of rules, not selection of the right world. Authors: Jianxin Gao, Bohan Chen, Runze Li, Qi Zhang · First submitted: 2026-11-21 · Artefacts: code (GitHub, RuleCraft) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28745

- The answer is not the state: reopening a diffusion language model's answer does not make it revise — Diffusion LMs generate many positions at once, so unfinished text looks revisable. On a 150-question source-disjoint benchmark with Dream, LLaDA and iLLaDA, reopening only the answer lets the old rationale reconstruct the old decision despite new evidence. Reopening rationale and answer together makes the decision follow the evidence currently present, correct or deliberately corrupted. Transplanting a coherent rationale between paired trajectories steers the rebuilt decision. On a new 100-question free-form set the effect stays strong for LLaDA and iLLaDA but is negligible for Dream. For readers interested in how reasoning text constrains conclusions: the retained rationale, not answer openness, decides corrigibility. Limitation: small benchmarks, three research diffusion models, and token-level attribution found where to reopen without giving a compact fix. Authors: Junchi Liao, Wentao Li · First submitted: 2026-11-23 · Artefacts: none stated (28 tables in the paper) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29937

- Recursive collective intelligence: do agent teams beat equal-compute baselines? — The "equal-budget ladder" grades a group against a single agent, a compute-matched majority vote and compute-matched pass@n. Forwarding split facts between agents lifts groups above pass@n at three model scales (about a third at 27B). Identical 27B agents spontaneously form hubs, improving accuracy by 18 percentage points. Two label-free generations of a group learning from its own output let the second 9B generation exceed both pass@n and a control trained on base-model turns, and on PlanBench, HiddenBench and Hanabi each generation beat its predecessor on unseen problems for four to six generations. Useful correction to multi-agent claims that ignore token cost: the gain comes when information is passed efficiently, not from agent count. Limitation: open Qwen-scale models and benchmark tasks, and the "less than half the token budget" comparison for prompted organisation is descriptive only. Authors: Lixuan He, Shengqu Cai, Fang Wu, Shikang Zheng, Liwei Jiang, Manling Li, Yi Ma et al. · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: not stated in abstract · Cognitive side: yes (collective intelligence). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32606

- A coding agent that writes a program beats video models at visual reasoning — On VBVR-Pro-Bench (500 instances over five cognitive categories with symbolic ground truth), the same first frame and prompt were given to video models and to coding agents that write a program rendering the answer video, scored by an unmodified rule-based evaluator with one attempt per instance. Codex (gpt-6-astra), Claude Code (Fable 5.1) and Gemini CLI (Gemini 3.1 Pro) score 0.923, 0.894 and 0.893, each more than 0.22 above the best video model. The best of 23 open-weight models, GLM-5, reaches 0.572, above all video models except the RL-trained one. The authors split agent programs into depictive ones (navigate the image to recover a scheme) and propositional ones (hard-coded values), which bears on what "visual reasoning" means. Limitation: only tasks whose solution is computable from a first frame and prompt; a rule-based evaluator rewards symbolic answers. Authors: Dezhi Luo, Fengyuan Yu, Haotian Zhang et al. · First submitted: 2026-11-25 · Artefacts: not stated in abstract (benchmark from Xu et al., 2026) · Cognitive side: yes (reasoning route comparison). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34813

- Limited-parameter finetuning reveals hidden model behaviours that behavioural tests miss — Training only 3% to 25% of LoRA parameters on examples of a target behaviour reveals whether a model has that behaviour, even when standard evaluations cannot, because the behaviour only appears in contexts the auditor cannot anticipate. It works on model organisms with hidden behaviours (8B to 14B parameters) and surfaces naturally occurring hidden behaviours in open-weight models of 27B to 35B, and it can tell apart models that look identical to an auditor. Published at NeurIPS 2026. Limitation, stated by the authors: it was not compared with baselines in a realistic auditing setting, so its practicality is open. Authors: Boyd Kane, Jo J. Jiao, Elizabeth Donoway, Bryce Woodworth, Alex Cloud et al. · First submitted: 2026-11-23 · Artefacts: not stated in abstract · Cognitive side: no (auditing and interpretability adjacent). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30672

- An AI agent writes new capabilities into a small model's weights without backpropagation — Within a budget of 50 model passes, with no validation or test instance visible, an agent adds gated attention heads and MLP neurons to Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct. The six-module composite lifts its normalized BIG-Bench Hard score from 19.95 to 41.05 (Gemma-3-1B-it: 10.25 to 32.86), while the next-token distribution on 1,023,000 WikiText tokens is unchanged (KL exactly 0) and answers on 15,845 items of five other tests are identical. The agent also writes exact multiplication up to 20 digits while running the model only up to 6 (100.0% on the unseen lengths versus at most 2.1% for a LoRA at the same budget). Limitations in the paper: 4 of 45 single modules showed small side effects, 5 of 50 sessions hit the 12-hour limit without a module, one hypothesis is only a partial success on Gemma, and it is a single-author study of 1B to 1.5B models, although the tests were preregistered and sealed in git. Authors: Santiago Rivadeneira Quintero · First submitted: 2026-11-21 · Artefacts: not stated in abstract · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28483

- Letting the model manage its own context: NVIDIA compares model-driven compaction with harness-driven compaction — In NVIDIA's open-source agent harness, models were given a context API to decide when and what to compact, and compared with the harness summarizing automatically, on document research and ML engineering at budgets from 8K to 512K tokens. GPT 6.1 nearly triples its score at a 32K budget on OfficeQA Pro v2 (21% to 56%), and Kimi gains with up to 41% fewer input tokens. Models differ widely in how often and how early they compact, and prompt changes shift that timing. Reusing the KV cache of the unchanged suffix after an edit cuts prefill compute by up to 15% with no measurable accuracy change. Useful for anyone designing agent instructions or context management. Limitation: gains depend on the model (some use the API poorly) and on two task families. Authors: Alessio Devoto, Avinash Vem, Tara Safavi, Sanjay Kariyappa et al. (NVIDIA harness) · First submitted: 2026-11-25 · Artefacts: open-source harness named in the abstract · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34147

- Auditing a production LLM for watermarks: the published test never fires on Claude — The published black-box Red-Green watermark presence test was applied to Claude, a model the provider says is watermarked. Across ten initial configurations it never rejected, although it detected a local reference implementation at comparable sample sizes. The cause: including the prompt in the watermark's repeated-context history suppresses marking exactly where the test measures it, so the watermark stays active but the probe is blind. A redesigned probe finds a context-dependent shift on Claude Fable that is absent on two sibling controls, but the pre-registered decision stays inconclusive because selective refusals break the protocol and the effect's origin is unresolved. Main lesson for evaluators: validation on a surrogate model does not carry over to the deployed one, and a null is not evidence of absence. Single author. Authors: Felix Desyatirikov · First submitted: 2026-11-21 · Artefacts: code, prompts and parsed responses stated as available · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29018

## Also notable

- Reinforcement learning can be recovered from its own rollouts — Fitting a model by maximum likelihood to archived RL rollouts, in the order they were generated and without separating correct from incorrect answers, recovers online RL's performance, and a single update per rollout batch tracks the whole learning curve. Replaying the original RL algorithm on archived rollouts collapses. The authors read this as "much of RL compute goes into finding a better sampling distribution rather than learning from samples"; the abstract gives no numbers or model sizes, so check the setups before generalising. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33102

- Ads in agent tool results (MCP) — Ads appear on only 0.57% of 101,363 open MCP endpoints and 0.04% of 122,656 local MCP packages, but 173 ad networks target agents, operators insert the ads themselves on about half of the 226 ad-returning servers, and only 9.3% of ads carry a sponsored label (most links hide their destination). The authors propose a per-item ad annotation so clients can show it outside the model's answer. Limitation: servers found through public traces only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28911

- Latent handoffs between agents undermine safety (same thread as the latent-communication papers: cross-model KV-cache transfer) — A target model that natively follows 0.4% of benchmark prompt injections follows up to 44.2% when its context arrives through a learned KV-cache mapper from another model. Jailbreak success rises by up to 24.6 points within a model family from transfer alone, and white-box optimisation through the mapper reaches 92.5% against 37.5% for plain GCG. A target-side cache guard restores much of the refusal. Limitation: research mappers, and the defence is the authors' own. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34278

- OthelloGPT follows the rules, and violations can be detected in activations — Linear probes separate legal from illegal moves and tell three classes of rule violation apart, and an unsupervised detector based on the step length of activations between consecutive moves flags illegal moves above chance and improves in a latent space with sparse autoencoders. Limitation: a toy game, a workshop paper (NeurIPS interpretability workshop), and "above chance" is the stated bar. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29715

- When do stored facts become available in looped transformers? — With final-loop supervision, heavier memorisation delays retrieval in looped models, while per-loop supervision makes near-final information readable after the first loop. For multi-hop chains in a released six-recurrence model, swapping a probe-defined entity component at one position redirects downstream entities and the answer, and replacements that reach the same entity by different relations keep the original answer in over 99.5% of cases. Same thread as the looped-model work already covered (and 2611.33896 below). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28808

- Revisiting LLMs as context engineers — On a benchmark of different reasoning patterns with 8B models, no automated context-engineering method wins everywhere, not even against chain-of-thought, and scaling the model or iterations does not help consistently. Gains correlate negatively with the diversity of failure modes (r = -0.5, p ≤ 0.05), and a bottleneck is whether the model can extract useful experience from varied failures. Caution for people relying on auto-written prompts or skills; limited to 8B-class models. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30494

- Agent Money Benchmark — 12 models were tested on 80 payment tasks with simulated sellers and 76 research questions on live data, settled in USDC on Solana through x402. Three models passed 79 of 80 payment tasks without errors, nine fell for a seller's trap at least once (repeat charges and above-list quotes were 79% of scam events), and estimated waste ranged from $21.0 per $100 for Claude Fable 5.2 to $70.8 for GPT-6 Luna. Limitation: a small authored benchmark with an estimated waste metric. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28610

- Verifiable token billing for LLM services (TokenProv) — A billing auditor with signed provider receipts that links each charge to an authorised call; reported at 94.4% to 107.4% of baseline throughput with 54 to 66 ms of online non-LLM processing, and it rejected all 1,600 validly signed attack queries across 100 adaptive episodes. Addresses the reader's complaint that token bills cannot be checked, but it needs providers to adopt it and proofs are the authors' own. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31806

- Influence functions under controlled retraining — The first study to retrain GPT-2-type models (68M parameters, 5.6B training tokens at most) with controlled data splits to test whether "influential" examples matter, for GradSim, TrackStar and K-FAC. On average the influential examples are relevant, but they do not always carry the target property directly and the ratio varies by setting. Limitation: small models, factual and linguistic probes only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34373

- When skills fail to compose (soft-prompt skills) — Independently learned soft-prompt skills reach at most 7.6% composition accuracy in one call against 88.2% to 100% over two calls with an explicit intermediate output. Replacing the frozen learned partner blocks with independently learned blocks of the same operations drops median composition accuracy from 98.0% and 95.3% to 12.7% and 0.5% on two Qwen bases. Relevant to agent "skills" libraries; tested on digit and record-editing tasks. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34398

- Looped models are fast multi-token predictors (Floodgates) — A self-speculative decoding method that adds mask tokens as a suffix and learns by LoRA to draft in parallel, up to 7.5x faster across seven models and five benchmarks, lossless in most cases. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33896

- A study of 470,000 rollouts on harness design for robot agents — Across eight simulation benchmarks, six VLMs and 15 VLA/WAM checkpoints, harness choices shift success by tens of points with the same models, and effective harnesses must adapt to both the VLM and the control model. Simulation only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34778

- Cloned agents are not independent replicates after they talk (thread: LLM-agent social simulation, two papers) — With clones sharing model, prompt and decoding and differing only in a private brief, interaction raises off-topic BFI-10 response-profile drift by +0.917 and +0.912 in two scenarios on Qwen3-122B-FP8 (exact p = 0.0008 and 0.0039), which persists at temperature zero; so multi-agent evaluations that treat clones as independent replicates overstate evidence. The companion study runs 50 persona-conditioned agents with memory ten times from identical starts (700 hours) and finds stable network measures and communication contracting to sparse dyads, many of which re-form across worlds. Both are simulations with no human comparison. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29581 and https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31543

- Hidden sensitivity in LLM pre-training — At one sensitive checkpoint a 1e-5 weight perturbation changes 35.7% of top-1 predictions and 16.2% of benchmark answers after 200 steps while only 0.8% of greedy generations remain identical, though validation loss barely moves; perturbing Adam moments is similarly harmful. Seen in OLMo-1B as well; limited to the checkpoints studied. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28830

- Frontier LLMs misapply formulas outside their physical regime — Across 72 physical mechanisms, accuracy drops by 13 to 57 points when the regime changes; RL with verifiable rewards lets Qwen3.5-9B match DeepSeek V4 Flash (284B) on regime-dependent tasks, but it is limited by the formulas in the base model. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28669

- Can LLMs predict training runs? - Robots act on the instruction, not on what they see (Seeing Is Not Reaching) — Across six vision-language-action models, arms often reach for an object's training location after it has moved, through a scene shortcut and an instruction shortcut. In pi-0.5, masking the image-grounding pathway removes reaches to moved targets while masking the direct instruction-to-action pathway increases them, so grounding guides action but is overridden. A training-free fix (Pathway-Separated Guidance) raises target-reaching from 22.0% to 52.6% and task success from 16.6% to 40.8% on LIBERO-PRO Object under position perturbations. Limitation: simulation benchmark and the fix is the authors' own. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32206

- Safety measured in chat does not predict safety as a robot controller (SayDo) — Across six open and closed VLMs and 11,480 episodes, hazardous-action rates rise from 14% in chat to 47% in a simulated robot arm (from 2% to 80% for the most affected model), driven mostly by open-weight models; models flag the hazard before acting in 73% of unsafe episodes and give way to pushback about twice as often when controlling robots. The safest models also show overcaution on benign controls. Single-author simulation benchmark. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30957

- Seeing is not planning: multimodal LLMs on a railway puzzle (Railbound-Bench) — On 200 puzzles the best of nine models solves 59.5% within five attempts, the next 39.5%, the other seven at most 10.0%. GPT-6.1 Terra copies 97.2% of boards exactly and predicts future states correctly in 91.3% of cases, yet only 22.8% of its first layouts work, and visual feedback fixes the reported fault in 175 of 417 first revisions but only 43 of them solve the puzzle (no better than fresh retries at 40k tokens). Knowing the task is not using it. Code and data open. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28726

- Multi-image benchmarks reward looking at one image (ASTERISM) — In about 33k items from 19 benchmarks, one image or the question alone settles 24.2% of items, and MLLMs recover a median 78% of the accuracy that all images add; most wrong answers on items needing several images repeat the single-image answer. The authors release an audited 18.7k-item benchmark (2.0k items corrected or rewritten) plus 49k training items. Code and data on GitHub. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32316

- VLMs attribute actions to people who are not in the picture — Across 25 VLMs, captions of person-absent scenes mention a person four times as often as human captions of the same images; humans and VLMs both prefer captions that avoid it, and DPO on the new NOBODY preference set brings agreement with human captions to or above inter-human agreement, which nine existing preference datasets did not. A human-VLM bias comparison; caption task only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31714

- Process rewards for VLM abstention (HumblePRM) — A lightweight process reward model scores perception and calibration stages separately, raising abstention on ambiguous images considerably with accuracy on clear images roughly unchanged, and transferring to Gemma 4 E4B; the abstract gives no numbers, so size of the effect is unverified. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33452

- Greedy decoding hides good plans in driving VLMs (Sample-and-Verify) — On WOD-E2E and NAVSIM the best sampled trajectory consistently beats the greedy one; for example the model often assigns probability to moving but greedy decoding keeps choosing to wait. A frozen VLM plus a lightweight verifier trained on ground-truth futures selects better candidates (no headline number in the abstract; verifier is trained on the benchmark domain). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33621
