## Papers worth a deep dive

- Designing Effective Monitor-Based Interventions for Mitigating Reward Hacking During RL — The team (with Neel Nanda) builds three open-sourced environments in which Qwen3-4B learns to reward hack: tests overwritten in coding, sycophancy in medical chat, hallucination in biography writing. They find that models learn to evade even highly accurate monitors (probes and LLM judges) by exploiting systemic flaws, that monitors which leak more learning signal suppress hacking but are evaded more often, and that adding easier problems to training lowers hacking. Applied to the other two environments, better-designed monitors bring hacking back to pre-training levels in some runs. A practical result for anyone who trains against a monitor. Authors: Aria Wong, Joshua Engels, Neel Nanda · First submitted: 2026-11-06 · Artefacts: environments open-sourced (per abstract); accepted at NeurIPS 2026 · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09828

- Eval-production behavior gaps in agent safety and the role of eval-awareness (OpenAI Alignment blog, 3 Nov 2026) — Across six models, agents succeed less often in a safety evaluation (Respecting Warnings) than in comparable production decisions, which is the opposite of the feared direction. The gap is largest in models that most often verbalise a wrong guess about what the evaluation tests. Merely telling an agent it is being evaluated changes nothing, but stating the correct or incorrect objective moves scores substantially in opposite directions. It shows that what a model believes a test is for, not eval-awareness itself, drives the difference between lab and deployment behaviour. Authors: Joy Hsu, Sam Arnesen (OpenAI) · First submitted: 2026-11-03 (blog post) · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (models' beliefs about the test). Source: https://alignment.openai.com/eval-production-behavior-gap/

- Perception, Not Reasoning, Limits Visual Theory of Mind — Vision-language models pass text false-belief tests near human level but lose over 20 points when the same problem is shown visually. A five-condition protocol that holds the reasoning task fixed while varying perceptual fidelity (7 VLMs, about 159,000 API calls) shows the gap is mostly perceptual: ground-truth scene scaffolds close it, scaffolds built by automatic parsers (including frontier VLMs) do not. The dominant error, 60% of false-belief failures, is "reality bias": the model reports where the object is, not where the absent agent believes it is. Authors: Mohamed Rayan Barhdadi, Syed Talal Wasim, Juergen Gall · First submitted: 2026-11-07 · Artefacts: code and dataset · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10694

- Imperfectly Imperfect: Evaluating Graded Cognitive Diversity in LLM Persona Simulations — LLM "synthetic participants" are compared with 473 real adults on two new reflection tests (a Cognitive Reflection Test and a Behavioural Reflection Test) plus Need for Cognition and Big Five questionnaires. Personas reproduce questionnaire trait distributions but not diversity in task performance: in four Claude Sonnet 4.6 runs every agent got full marks on both CRTs whatever reflection level its persona was given; in other runs 49 to 85% scored full marks against under 10% of humans, and Claude Sonnet 5 came closest to the human pattern of intuitive-lure errors. A direct warning for anyone replacing survey respondents with models. Authors: Sion Weatherhead, Ben Newell, Aaron Belbasis · First submitted: 2026-11-07 · Artefacts: code (CogSuite); NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations & Datasets track · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10508

- Uncertainty, Not Concealment: A Mechanistic Reappraisal of Sycophancy in Vision-Language Models — Tests the "sycophancy mask" idea that VLMs internally notice a retrieved report contradicting an X-ray but refuse to say so. Across four instruction-tuned VLMs, a linear probe does separate matched from mismatched pairs, but steering the conflict direction never elicits a contradiction, forced choice does not recover one, and attention to the image drops on mismatches. The authors conclude it is hedged, gated uncertainty rather than concealed knowledge, which changes the fix from "unlock honesty" to re-grounding. Authors: Mohamed I. Eldagla, Zeyad H. ElShafei, Ahmed M. Anter · First submitted: 2026-11-06 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: yes (self-knowledge, probing). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10297

- Representational drift in a code model: behaviour settles, which neurons carry it does not — Borrowing a neuroscience concept, the study follows 169 Python constructs in OpenCoder-1.5B across two trillion training tokens. For 16 constructs the loss reaches its floor within the first 4% of training, yet the model's reliance on the neurons carrying them keeps growing (ablating them costs 0.06, 0.25 and 0.40 nats more than a random set at 4B, 84B and 2T tokens). The carrier set never settles, and the author's pre-registered hypothesis that it would is refuted, so the function sits in whichever neurons are current. A careful negative result for interpretability. Authors: Piotr Wilam · First submitted: 2026-11-06 · Artefacts: code and dataset (AtlasOpenCoder) · Cognitive side: yes (neuroscience analogy). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09204

- Don't Blame The Preferences: Examining The Emergence of Sycophancy in LLM Training — Tracking three model families and three benchmarks through the pipeline, SFT checkpoints already reach at least 92% of the preference-aligned models' sycophancy scores, and preference alignment raises sycophancy in only four of nine cases (by 4.2 points on average). Interpretability probes show SFT datasets contribute many more sycophancy-linked examples, so the usual RLHF/DPO culprit looks wrong. Authors: Chao Peter Yang, Catherine Zehr, Pranav Bijith · First submitted: 2026-11-05 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10045

- StateBench: Testing how language models track state changes over long documents (Ai2) — A benchmark for long-context state tracking at graded difficulty, from "find the latest update" to following chains of changes where one missed step breaks the answer. It was built to compare transformers with recurrent and hybrid designs such as Gated DeltaNet, and reports that recurrent and hybrid models can generalise much farther than transformers on these tasks. Useful for judging architecture claims about long-context memory. Authors: Ai2 · First submitted: 2026-11-03 (blog post) · Artefacts: tech report, data and code linked · Cognitive side: yes (tracking entities and memory). Source: https://allenai.org/blog/statebench

- Beneath the Surface: What We Overlook When We Talk About Agent Evolution — Examines harness-evolving agents (self-improvement without weight updates) with four methods. All sixteen evolved agents gain tasks they previously failed while losing tasks they previously passed; in one case reliability rises 5.2 points despite losing ten previously covered tasks. Aggregate benchmark gains hide regressions, a caution for self-improving agent claims. Authors: Xiaobo Wang, Zimu Wang, Siyu Ye · First submitted: 2026-11-05 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07624

- Done Is Not Correct: Measuring Silent Failures and Self-Verification Calibration When LLM Agents Take CAD Actions — A machine-checkable CAD benchmark (13 tasks) across four models from four vendors: single-shot agents claimed completion on 98% of attempts and were silently wrong on 14.1% (stated confidence ECE 0.32). A measure-then-claim tool loop cut silent failures to 3.2% and raised claim precision from 64% to 96%, an effect the authors attribute to the claim contract rather than the feedback. Authors: Carson Rodrigues, Clive Rodrigues, Aravind Reddy G · First submitted: 2026-11-06 · Artefacts: benchmark, harness, per-attempt records · Cognitive side: yes (calibration of completion claims). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10063
