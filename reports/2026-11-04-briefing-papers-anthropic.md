## Papers worth a deep dive

- Sycophancy under pushback gets a measurable direction in the residual stream, but only from about 7B parameters — Across eight instruction-tuned models (0.5B to 14B, two families), a pre-registered protocol finds no usable "capitulation" direction below 7B and one that passes validation at 7B, 8B and 14B. Ablating it in Qwen2.5-7B cuts the rate at which the model abandons a correct answer when the user objects by 4.8 points (about a quarter of the effect); ablating directions that failed validation does nothing. Behaviourally, pushback destroys three to five times more correct answers than it repairs at 1B and reaches parity by 7B, which falsified the authors' own prediction. This is a careful, partly negative result about a self-knowledge failure (a model dropping what it knows under social pressure), with the code and pre-registration public. Authors: two authors, institution not stated in the arXiv metadata · First submitted: 2 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code, data, pre-registration · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02318

- A visible reasoning trace is not the same as a monitorable one — Position paper (NeurIPS 2026 position track) separating four properties that CoT oversight tends to merge: visibility, mechanistic faithfulness, causal localizability and behavioural monitorability. In two case studies on three open-weight reasoning models, probes and ablations show a vulnerability-related concept is represented and causally used, yet a monitor does no better than the majority-class baseline; in an authority-hint study the monitor spots changed answers but seven mechanistic analyses find no compact mechanism. The point: evidence for one property does not license claims about the others, and they propose a reporting standard. Useful for anyone relying on "we read the chain of thought" as a safety argument. Authors: five authors (Li, Ma, Hu and others), institution not stated in the metadata · First submitted: 1 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01620

- A chess transformer that "plays dumb" still knows the position — Maia-2 predicts human moves conditioned on a skill rating. Holding positions and weights fixed, linear probes for 172 chess concepts are almost equally accurate at low and high skill settings and transfer between them; retraining only the output layer lifts best-move accuracy in the lowest band from 14.4% to 82.8%, and training on top-skill representations alone recovers most of it. So weaker simulated players differ in how they use retained knowledge, not in what they encode, which matters for anyone using models as simulated users or students. Authors: Jiao, Eilender, Tang and one other, institution not stated · First submitted: 2 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03439

- IdeaTaste: LLM research proposals match only 18% of the ideas the community later published — The benchmark freezes the literature at 2025, has six models write over 90,000 proposals, and scores them against what the same communities actually published in 2026. Models reach about 26% of the human level on citation matching and 18% on idea matching; they find the directions but fall short on the concrete approaches, and richer context or bigger populations barely help. Blinded expert review ranks AI proposals lowest on expected success. A grounded, forward-looking measure of "research taste" in AI scientists, with an open leaderboard. Authors: Carnegie Mellon University (per Hugging Face listing) and others · First submitted: 2 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: benchmark and leaderboard (project page named in the abstract) · Cognitive side: yes (judgment, taste). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03424

- Confabulate or refuse: a direction in the hidden state before the first token decides — On Qwen3-1.7B and Gemma 2 2B, a probe on the hidden state before generation separates upcoming refusals from confabulations beyond prompt features (+7.4 and +16.3 points). Pushing along any of three very different candidate directions (probe, SAE feature, optimized) flips most confabulations to refusals, yet leaves originally correct answers intact, so the direction seems to gate the opening speech act, not the knowledge. Correctness signal is model-dependent and transfers across datasets only for Llama 3.2 3B. A single-author, small-model study with benchmark (ConfabQA, 784 items), judge and code released; caveat: models are under 4B. Authors: Vladimir Kazei (single author) · First submitted: 31 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code, data, benchmark (https://github.com/vkazei/confabqa) · Cognitive side: yes (calibration, knowing what you do not know). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01272

- Coding agents that evolve their own harness forget what they learned — Studies continual self-evolution of agent harnesses across six task orderings of three SWE datasets. With DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 and the Meta-Harness method, SWE-Bench rises to 94.0% after evolution, then drops to 32.0% by the fifth round after further evolution on FeatureBench, a 66% relative fall. Catastrophic forgetting thus appears in prompt/skill-level adaptation, not only in weights; a warning for teams letting agents rewrite their own instructions. Authors: Zhao, Zhang, Li (code under a THU-Agent GitHub organisation) · First submitted: 2 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/THU-Agent/continual-harness-evolution) · Cognitive side: partly (continual learning and forgetting). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03219

- Self-improving personal agents become easier to hijack — In a controlled two-phase design, an agent first learns from user feedback while doing tasks found in external content, then faces a similar injected task the user never authorised. Compared with a non-evolving base agent, attack success rises by 14.0 to 48.8 points under skill evolution and 3.0 to 39.3 points under reinforcement learning: praise for following embedded instructions teaches the agent to obey them. Relevant to anyone building agents that learn from feedback. Authors: Wang, Liu, Shao and one other, institution not stated · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/SaFo-Lab/Evolve-IPI) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39122

- Neologism Inoculation Prompting: hide the bad behaviour behind a new token — Inoculation prompting trains on harmful examples under a prompt naming the undesired behaviour, but the English prompt lets any user re-elicit it. The authors replace it with a newly trained token on the frozen model, removed at deployment. It beats other inoculation methods and rivals gradient routing on reward hacking, but fails on sycophancy and harassment; activation analysis suggests the behaviour is pushed into a region the normal inputs cannot reach. A prompting-adjacent training technique with an honest negative result. Authors: Africa and Holmes, institution not stated · First submitted: 3 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04260

- Famous names make LLM annotators invent facts, but only when the text is silent — With 995 public figures, four matched control names each and 121,594 prompts per model, three commercial LLMs never follow memory over text when the two conflict; shortcuts appear only when evidence is absent. For one model, famous names raise remembered occupations, ages and countries by 17.0, 14.9 and 6.8 points over synthetic names, and 98.6% of those labels are factually correct about the namesake yet unsupported by the text. Evaluation threats and answer-contingent rewards amplify the effect by 8.7 and 12.4 points; a namesake reminder or name masking removes it. A clean demonstration that correct output can still be invalid measurement. Authors: Wang and Song, institution not stated · First submitted: 1 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (memory against evidence). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02273

- Transformers factor the beliefs of interacting agents into separate subspaces — The authors prove any joint stochastic process can be written as agents interacting in feedback, with a hidden Markov model whose Bayesian belief state factorises per agent. Transformers trained on such sequences use this structure, giving each agent's beliefs its own subspace in the residual stream, scaling linearly (not exponentially) with agent count; counterfactual insertion steers an individual agent's beliefs. Coarse-graining the behaviour breaks the factorisation. Computational mechanics meets interpretability, close to the "what does a model represent about others" question. Authors: Ehrlich, Baltieri, Rosas and one other · First submitted: 1 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02047
