## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- **Epoch AI: "Can AI automate Epoch?"** — Epoch gave six models real tasks from its own work (graphics, research design, tool use in Figma, Drive and satellite-imagery sites), ran them at the highest reasoning settings without intervention and graded them against staff standards. Its finding: Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra lead and are reliable on well-defined tasks, but miss implicit house conventions, pick promising research directions yet design experiments that do not measure what they claim, and converge on similar ideas; open-weight models lag further. A useful counterweight to verifiable-task benchmarks. Published 8 Oct 2026. Author: Epoch AI staff (research organisation) · Format: deep dive · Length: ~16 min read. Source: https://epoch.ai/publications/can-ai-automate-epoch
- **Epoch AI: "EBR-bench update"** — Epoch's long-horizon learning-from-experience benchmark (the board game Earthborne Rangers) was saturated by GPT-6 Astra, which scored 100% in over half of its attempts. Epoch traced every top run to one card that bypasses the game's time constraint, which its best human also used; it found no evidence of memorisation, and is banning the card. Multi-agent scaffolds had no clear effect on scores but led models to try more diverse decks. A careful case study of a benchmark broken by a legitimate exploit. Published 7 Oct 2026. Author: Epoch AI staff · Format: deep dive · Length: ~8 min read. Source: https://epoch.ai/publications/ebr-bench-update
- **Epoch AI: "How many AI agents could we run?"** — Epoch estimates from HBM shipments that chips shipped through 2027 could run roughly 30–170 million concurrent frontier-model agents, equal in weekly hours to about 140–720 million full-time workers. Applying DeepSeek V4 Pro serving benchmarks gives about 1.9 billion agents. It also measures agent cost from traces: roughly $16–18 per hour for Codex workloads and $24–50 for Claude Code. These are Epoch's modelled estimates with stated uncertainty. Published 2 Oct 2026 (at the edge of the 7-day window). Author: Epoch AI staff · Format: deep dive · Length: ~25 min read. Source: https://epoch.ai/publications/estimating-the-agent-population
- **Session-aware agentic inference with NVIDIA Dynamo** — The authors argue that agent sessions (huge prefill, repeated calls, subagents, KV cache idle while tools run) break request-level serving. Dynamo carries one session identifier through the stack to enable session-aware routing, a shared KV-cache index and programmatic cache movement across vLLM and SGLang. Read it for the serving-side view of why agent context is expensive; the performance claims are the vendor's own. Published 8 Oct 2026. Author: Ishan Dhanani, Jamie Li, Karen Chung (NVIDIA Dynamo team; PyTorch blog) · Format: deep dive · Length: ~15 min read. Source: https://pytorch.org/blog/session-aware-agentic-inference-with-nvidia-dynamo/
- **J++ Lens: Jacobian filtering for more faithful workspace lenses** — Builds on Anthropic's "global workspace" work (a J-Lens that reads verbalisable representations from activations). Filtering noisy gradients before the averaged Jacobian lifts success on latent-variable extraction to 55% (J-Lens 36%, R-Lens 38%) across 9–284B models, strongest in early layers. The authors say it could help monitor eval awareness and unverbalised scheming; code, a demo and lenses are released. Published 8 Oct 2026 on LessWrong. Author: Kola Ayonrinde (Anthropic Fellow) with Jack Lindsey (Anthropic) · Format: deep dive (research write-up) · Length: ~20 min read. ## Prompting and context techniques

- **How to prompt Hugging Face's ML Intern: seven worked prompts** — Yuvraj Sharma built six small models in days by describing each in one message to the ML Intern agent; his first prompts ran ~450 words, later ~2,000. The pattern: the idea and why, the exact dataset/base model/training script, a "Verified facts, do not re-derive" section, a required zero-shot baseline before training, a 50-step smoke test with a check that weights changed, defined model-card deliverables and a spend cap ("Cap total spend at USD 12 and ask me before exceeding it"). Results he reports: a 0.8B prompt-rewriter on CPU (99.7% valid output, ~$16) and a 2B vision model going from 14.9% to 52.8% on 335 test photos (~$1.90). All seven prompts are published at github.com/yvrjsharma/ml-intern-prompts. Published 8 Oct 2026. Author: Yuvraj Sharma (Hugging Face) · Format: deep dive / how-to · Length: ~8 min read. Source: https://huggingface.co/blog/building-with-ml-intern
