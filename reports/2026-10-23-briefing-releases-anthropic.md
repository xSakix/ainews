- Microsoft AI publishes its MAI-DxO diagnostic system in Science and open-sources the benchmark behind it — MAI-DxO is not a new model but an orchestrator: it runs several rounds of debate between virtual physicians that each propose diagnoses and choose tests, weighing each test's likely value against its cost. On SDBench (304 NEJM case proceedings plus typical emergency-department presentations from MIMIC-IV-ED, each played as an interactive case where the agent decides what to request next) Microsoft reports 83% accuracy on the NEJM cases against 22% for physicians without reference resources and 36% with them, and 80% against 47% on the emergency-department cases; one configuration reached 81% at less than half the cost of comparison systems. The gains held when the same orchestration was run on newer models, GPT-5.5 among them, so the claim is about the method, not one model. The tension for the reader is the gap between the headline ratios and the setting: all figures are Microsoft's own, the post does not say how the physicians were recruited or scored, and the paper (not read in full here) is the place to check that. Microsoft is releasing SDBench code (benchmark, Gatekeeper, Judge and cost estimator) for select NEJM cases so others can reproduce or challenge it. - OpenAI takes the Decisions API out of beta with gpt-6-luna, doubling the questions allowed per request — The endpoint (POST /v1/decisions) evaluates text, images and JSON and returns typed answers (a probability that a condition holds, a choice from a fixed set, or a score against a rubric) in one call; OpenAI says it is about ten times faster than the Responses API. - Ai2 releases OlmoEarth v1.3, a satellite embedding model, with precomputed embeddings for the whole planet and a browser explorer — The model reads a year of Sentinel-1 radar plus Sentinel-2 and Landsat imagery and writes one 128-number vector for every 10-metre pixel (the first 64 numbers also work alone), so a crop map, land-cover map or change analysis becomes training a small classifier on top, with no GPU-heavy fine-tuning. Ai2 reports its 128-dimensional embeddings score highest on 20 of 27 evaluations against AlphaEarth Foundations and Tessera v2 (the 64-dimensional version leads on 18), including a new tropical crop-type benchmark, PLANTEUR; these are Ai2's own comparisons. The numbers a practitioner can use: v1.3 took 53% of the compute of v1.2 and 18% of v1, and Ai2 puts the final Base pre-training run at roughly $2,720 of rented H100 time; the archive is about 150 TB per year at 10 m, with 2023 to 2025 global and 2017 to 2022 for selected countries, and each tile carries a provenance table of the source scenes. The worked example is gold mining in Peru's Madre de Dios, where year-on-year embedding change shows the 2019 crackdown and the pockets that kept operating. Weights, pre-training data and code are public under the OlmoEarth Artifact License (Ai2's own licence, not an OSI one). Artefacts: weights (OlmoEarth Artifact License), data, code, technical report, embeddings on Source Cooperative, Embeddings Explorer · Sizes or variants: OlmoEarth v1.3 Base; 64- and 128-dimensional annual embeddings · Released: 22 October 2026 (the model repository existed earlier; the blog post and embeddings are the announcement). - IBM publishes Granite Timeseries Ensemble, a no-training recipe that combines four Granite forecasters — The recipe stores a configuration, not merged weights, and pulls four member checkpoints with different architectures at run time, then pools their median and quantile forecasts either uniformly (the recommended Probabilistic Ensemble) or weighted by each member's forecast spread (the IQR Ensemble). On 97 GIFT-Eval configurations, with no fine-tuning and no fitting on test targets, IBM reports an aggregate MASE of 0.683 for the Probabilistic Ensemble against 0.685 for the best single member (PatchTST-FM-r2), and 3rd place among eligible systems on both MASE rank and CRPS rank in a leaderboard snapshot of 20 October. The honest part of the post is useful: the MASE margin is small, PatchTST-FM-r2 keeps the best aggregate CRPS, some fine-tuned or non-permissive systems score better, and the stated benefit is not having to choose a model in advance. Install is `pip install granite-tsfm>=0.3.11`. Artefacts: recipe and member weights (Apache 2.0 on the ensemble repository), blog post · Sizes or variants: Probabilistic and IQR recipes over four Granite time-series models · Released: 22 October 2026 (repository created 23 September; the blog post with the evaluation is the dated announcement). Source: https://huggingface.co/blog/ibm-research/granite-timeseries-ensemble-r1

- Liquid AI announces RIPL, an inference engine that returns a complete LFM response in under 50 ms — RIPL (Real-time Inference Pipeline) targets request paths with hard latency budgets, such as search ranking, recommendations, fraud and payment decisions, where teams today use gradient-boosted trees or small embedding models because LLM serving is too slow. RIPL is for enterprise deployments of LFMs on NVIDIA GPUs (H100, H200, B200, RTX PRO 6000) and is not open source; there is no public price. ## 2. Europe

- Hugging Face's Bio team releases Carbon-X, a 14.8B open model trained jointly on DNA and text — Built on Qwen3-14B with a hybrid tokenizer (the Qwen3 text vocabulary plus 5,515 DNA tokens, each covering a 6-base k-mer), it takes raw DNA in a chat prompt and answers in English: pathogenic-or-benign calls for variants, regulatory regions, organism classification and next-base prediction. Hugging Face reports an average of 66.7% across five DNA benchmarks with thinking off, 27 points above Qwen3-14B and next to GPT-6.1 Sol at 67.5%, while leading on coding-variant effects and next-base prediction but trailing on complex variants (85.1 against Opus 5.5's 94.8) and single-base variants (70.4 against 76.2). Two of the five benchmarks (FrontierVEP and NextBase) were built by the authors, and the post states decontamination checks. The consequence figure is cost: the team estimates classifying the 2.4 million ClinVar variants of uncertain significance would cost about $300 with Carbon-X against $32,000 to $4 million with the frontier models tested (their estimate, using API list prices and an assumed $5 per H200-hour), and the 80 GB bf16 weights let labs keep unpublished sequences on their own hardware. Context window 40,960 tokens; needs Transformers 5 or later and `trust_remote_code` for the tokenizer; vLLM serves it on one GPU. Artefacts: weights (Apache 2.0), GGUF, model card, code (github.com/huggingface/carbon), release blog (a Hugging Face Space) · Sizes or variants: Carbon-X (14.8B, 29.7 GB of weights) and a GGUF build; earlier Carbon models from 500M to 8B exist · Released: 22 October 2026 (repository created 21 October 10:21 UTC; model card finalised 22 October 09:55 UTC). - AI21 shows how it cut GitLab Duo Code Review's cost by 98% on a public benchmark by changing the harness, not the product — On Martian's Code Review Bench (50 public pull requests, scored by two judge models), the baseline (Claude Sonnet 5 on all four stages of GitLab's source-available flow) cost $0.589 per pull request at 46.4% F1. Compacting the context alone saved 19%; moving every stage to DeepSeek V4 Flash, self-hosted with vLLM on H100s but priced at Together AI's list rates, cost $0.086 (85% less) at 48.4% F1; doing both reached $0.0135 per pull request (97.7% less) at 47.2% F1 and about a third of the latency, because compaction made the largest prompts (up to 480K tokens) fit the 256K deployment instead of falling back to Sonnet. The caveats are in the post: configurations were tuned on the same 50 pull requests they are scored on, no GitLab production traffic was used, about one DeepSeek review call in seven ended without posting and was retried (retries included in cost), and the final setup catches slightly fewer issues (recall 47.6% against 52.0%) while being more precise. AI21 sells this kind of harness optimisation, so it is a vendor case study with its method written out. Artefacts: blog post with methodology and per-configuration table; no code released · Sizes or variants: Sonnet 5, Haiku 4.5 and DeepSeek V4 Flash configurations · Released: 22 October 2026 (11:26 UTC).
