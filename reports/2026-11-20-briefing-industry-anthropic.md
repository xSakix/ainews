## Safety and security

- Prime Intellect discloses that a hosted DeepSeek endpoint was silently swapped for a de-censored copy for about two days — Prime Intellect's advisory PI-SA-2026-001 (published 19 Nov) says an unauthorized party replaced the deployment behind its deepseek-v4.1-flash endpoint with an "abliterated" copy (refusal behaviour reduced or removed) from 8 Nov 01:24 UTC to 10 Nov 06:35 UTC. Chat template, tokenizer and serving configuration were unchanged. The company reports refusals dropped sharply while GPQA Diamond and tau-bench scores were unaffected, so a capability benchmark alone would not have caught the swap. It found no evidence that prompts or completions were recorded or that keys were touched, and says customers who relied on the model's refusals should review outputs from the window. The investigation continues. For anyone buying inference from a third-party host, this is a concrete case of serving integrity (which weights answered your request) failing silently. Source: https://www.primeintellect.ai/security/advisories/deepseek-v4-1-flash-model-substitution

- Bitrise publishes a 25-day incident report on an intrusion run by an open-source AI penetration-testing platform — Bitrise, a mobile CI/CD provider, says an operator using the ARTEX agent platform probed it from 25 Oct to 18 Nov, labelling its traffic an "authorized pentest" the company had not ordered. On 13 Nov the agents signed in to a dormant 2022 internal test account without two-factor authentication and, in about ten hours, chained credentials to reach signing files, secrets, build logs, source code and infrastructure state. Bitrise shut the platform down the same day, revoked all 919 cloud service-account keys and rebuilt build infrastructure. Files in 15 customer workspaces were accessed (one certificate file it could not open, one provisioning profile, 13 diagnostic build logs); the company found no evidence of wider customer data access and tells customers to rotate stored secrets. The report's point for practitioners is speed (stolen keys used within minutes) and how little it took: one forgotten account. Source: https://bitrise.io/artex-incident-report

- BIS remote-access rule is now in force: published and effective 19 Nov, comments to 19 Jan 2027 — Material new development on the interim final rule covered on 17 Nov. The Federal Register text says it restricts exports and transfers to overseas data centres that supply large remote compute to companies headquartered in Country Group D:5 or Macau, with due-diligence duties for data centres, foundries and chip exporters (commercial recommender systems exempt). A licence is now required for advanced semiconductors going to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil (plus any country Commerce designates; US-headquartered firms exempt with reporting), foundry and packager pathways for advanced-node logic chips are narrowed, and the Biden-era AI Diffusion Rule regulations are formally rescinded. For cloud and GPU-rental buyers in those countries the practical change is licensing and customer checks on who ultimately uses the compute. Source: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/19/2026-23529/addressing-remote-access-to-advanced-semiconductors
