## Safety and security

- 🚨 Uber says driver licences were stolen through the AI vendor Fireworks — Uber told several state attorneys general that attackers who compromised a service provider's internal credentials on 9 October used them to reach Uber data held there. An Uber spokesperson named the vendor as Fireworks.ai. State filings show 3,430 affected people in South Carolina, 2,567 in Massachusetts and 1,873 in Rhode Island (7,870 in those three states, with other states and totals not yet given), and the data includes driver's licence details. Fireworks has attributed its breach to a financially motivated group that sells stolen LLM access tokens. This is the third named customer fallout from the same vendor breach after Cursor and Notion, and the first with a count of affected individuals. Why it matters: it shows how an inference or fine-tuning vendor becomes a single point of failure for the data of its customers' customers. Source: https://www.engadget.com/2316881/uber-drivers-information-stolen-in-a-third-party-hack/ (also https://www.cyberkendra.com/2026/11/uber-data-breach-fireworks-ai-driver-licenses.html, which reproduces the state-filing counts and the notice letter dated 10 November)

- AI is pushing vulnerability volume past what maintainers can absorb — A Telegraph feature carried by Yahoo, dated 14 November, quotes Ondřej Surý of the Internet Systems Consortium: the ISC disclosed 1,415 flaws in October, a 1,000 percent rise year on year. The October PHP flaw that led the UK NCSC to tell site owners to unplug was found in August by two separate teams using advanced ChatGPT and Gemini models. Cisco Talos says weaponisation used to take months or years and no longer does, and the NCSC annual report warns of a race to shorten patch timelines. Opinion-heavy, but the ISC figure is a concrete count from a maintainer. Source: https://tech.yahoo.com/cybersecurity/articles/overwhelmed-techies-hold-back-wave-100000907.html

- AI-assisted ransomware: a rise in claimed attacks, but humans still run the successful groups — Wired's Kernel Panic newsletter (14 November) reports researchers counting nearly 3,000 claimed ransomware attacks on leak sites over the summer, 20 to 40 percent more than in spring, with growth concentrated outside the US. Former FBI cyber deputy Cynthia Kaiser (Halcyon) attributes part of the rise to AI. Recorded Future's Allan Liska says LLMs are used for phishing and negotiation, and that the successful groups remain largely human-operated; fully AI-driven ransomware has not appeared. Claimed attacks are an inflated and uncertain measure, as the article itself notes. Source: https://www.wired.com/story/beware-the-new-ai-fueled-ransomware-boom/

## Policy and regulation

- Bessent: enforce the existing chip rules, not new ones, and hold Nvidia staff accountable — The Treasury Secretary told CNBC (reported by Tom's Hardware, 14 November) that there is "a lot of leakage in the system" and the real question is whether Nvidia knew its customers and whether it sold to obvious pass-through shell companies. This sets the Treasury line against the four bills (AI Overwatch Act, Chip Security Act, Match Act, Cloud Security Act) being pushed in Congress.
