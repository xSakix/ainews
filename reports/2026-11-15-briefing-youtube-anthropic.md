## AI engineering and research

- **All Code Is Slop (Except Mine): how the Grok Bot team keeps agent-written code trustworthy** — Lauren Tan (poteto), engineer on the Grok Bot team at SpaceXSI, says the team merged about 11,000 pull requests in one month with a revert rate around 0.5%, and that the cause is the codebase, not the model. The talk covers enforceable architecture rules, lint rules that replace code comments, runtime verification with feature maps, formal verification with TLA+ and Lean, UI testing, and deliberately spending tokens on verification, built on Cursor agents and the "pstack" tooling. The figures are the speaker's own, from one team. Worth the time for anyone running agents on a real codebase: it is a concrete list of what to build before letting agents merge. - **Your PR Says the Tests Passed. Prove It.** — Stephen Chin and Cassandra Chin start from a narrow failure: a pull-request description can claim checks passed while the recorded commands do not show that the submitted change was tested. Cassandra built Tracetoon to recover the context behind a branch; the next version uses Relate, an embedded graph database written in Rust, to assemble an evidence brief (rationale, commands that ran, timing, code version) before the writer model starts, with a strong writer and cheaper reviewers checking claims against the brief. They report better description quality at roughly half the token use on their own "PR Bench" comparisons, and say that this describes their project, not other codebases. - **Your Picture of AI Is Out of Date** — Local Optima reruns the questions people still use to catch models out (r's in "strawberry", the car-wash question, the surgeon riddle) on 21 models from 2023 to 2026, plus 16 new questions written the week of the video so none could be in training data. Each model got each question five times in fresh API conversations with no search or calculator; exact answers were checked directly, the rest graded by a second model (Claude Sonnet 5.5, not among those tested), and the author graded 144 answers blind and reports the judge agreed on 143. His finding: 2026 models pass nearly all of the old tests when they think first; what still fails is counting letters in a paragraph, a hand with six fingers and whether the model chooses to think at all. A small independent evaluation with its method and sources in the description; one person's test, not a benchmark. - **Every Trick to Make Local AI Faster** — Alex Grankin measures the same Qwen 3.8 27B model on his M5 Max MacBook Pro going from 25 to 97 tokens a second by combining MLX, multi-token prediction (MTP), MTPLX, DFlash and Splash, then covers the PC side (keep the model in GPU memory, CUDA over Vulkan). He also covers the caveats: gains are smaller for writing than for code, a random draft model can make decoding slower, dense versus mixture-of-experts models behave differently, and he says speculative decoding does not lower answer quality. Single-machine measurements by the author, who sells a local-AI guide in the same video and description.
