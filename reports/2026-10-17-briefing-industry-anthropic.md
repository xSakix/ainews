## 2. Policy and regulation

- **NIST, NSA and CISA publish ten voluntary key practices for incident readiness and response in frontier AI training and evaluation** — Aimed at incidents where AI agents take significant uninstructed actions that breach the operator's controls or human oversight; the practices are grouped into five stages (preparation, detection, response, recovery, reporting) and grounded in NIST SP 800-61r3. It fulfils a recommendation of the White House's 2025 action plan and builds on a May 2026 NIST workshop. It is the first US government guidance written directly for the incident wave of the past months (OpenAI, Anthropic, India, Australia). Voluntary, no enforcement. Source: https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/10/key-practices-incident-readiness-and-response-super-intelligence-training

- 🚨 **China publishes draft export controls on 40 technologies including 23 AI technologies: models of 2 trillion parameters or more could be restricted, and agents for defense and critical infrastructure banned** — The Ministry of Commerce and Ministry of Science and Technology released a draft update of the export ban and restriction catalogue (AP, 16 October). Exports of core technology for large models trained on sensitive national data, and of agents built for national defense or critical information infrastructure, would be prohibited; models with 2 trillion or more parameters or "world-leading" capabilities could be restricted; some AI and memory chip design technologies are listed too. AP notes Kimi K3 and Qwen 3.8-Max exceed the parameter threshold (no models named in the draft). It lands as the US and China discuss an AI safety channel and an incident notification mechanism.
