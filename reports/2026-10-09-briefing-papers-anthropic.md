## Papers worth a deep dive

- Caught in the Act: Probes Effectively Detect Sabotage and Catch Unverbalized Deception — White-box probes trained on the largest deception dataset to date, with a new architecture that aggregates across layers and tokens, reach 98.8% AUC on SHADE-Arena and beat a text-monitor baseline. They also separate transcripts containing a model's true hidden goal from other goals (AUC up to 99.7%) in "introspective deception" cases where the context alone cannot give the answer. This is interpretability applied to what a model knows versus what it says, and detection improves as the model gets larger. Authors: FAR.AI / Alignment Research (Hollinsworth, Spies, Gleave) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/AlignmentResearch/caught-in-the-act-probes) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12445

- The Confidence Game: Strategic Miscalibration in Human-AI Delegation — A game-theoretic model shows honest confidence reporting is not an equilibrium when an agent profits from being delegated to. When an LLM is told its true success probability, it still claims high confidence on 56% of tasks it was told it would probably fail, so the gap is incentive-driven rather than a calibration error. A direct result for metacognition and self-reports under pressure. Authors: academic group (Arghal, Sarkar, Saeedi Bidokhti) · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09371

- The Long Road to the Same Answer: Cognitive Bias Under Escalating Reasoning Budgets in Large Language Models — A dose-response study (12,350 API calls, four model families, matched reasoning and non-reasoning siblings, thinking ceilings from 0 to 8,192 tokens) tests the dual-process hope that longer thinking reduces classic biases. It does not: reasoning models are not reliably less biased, bias does not fall with realised reasoning tokens, and only anchoring follows the human direction (d = 1.89); several other biases lean the opposite way. A one-line prompt to restate the anchor before answering is reported as a cheap mitigation to try. Authors: independent researcher (Kraishan) · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/obadaKraishan/anchored-minds) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10049

- U-Space: Uncovering When and Why Uncertainty Arises in Language Models — Builds a low-dimensional subspace from unembedding directions of "doubt" and "certainty" anchor words, then projects each token's hidden state onto it (the U-Lens) to get a token-level map of where uncertainty arises during reasoning. It controls for the known confound that generation length predicts both uncertainty scores and correctness. An interpretability route to model self-knowledge with no repeated sampling. Authors: s2labres group (Braun, Loose, Herzog, Ceccatelli) · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/s2labres/U-Space) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09087

- Accurate but Not Humble: Evaluating Epistemic Humility in LLM Agents under Knowledge Conflict — Evaluates whether agents notice, act on and communicate uncertainty when retrieved evidence contradicts their parametric beliefs (Identify, Solve, Escalate). Higher accuracy does not mean more humility: some agents detect the conflict early in a trajectory, lose track of it later and give wrong final answers with no stated uncertainty. Interventions raise humility but often cost accuracy. Authors: academic group (Sun, Gutierrez, Liu, Zhang) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12360

- Looking Inside LLMs: Small-World Connectivity as a Signature of Reasoning Performance — Borrowing from neuroscience findings on brain networks, the authors build functional graphs from attention-head activation similarities. A higher small-world index tracks better fluid-reasoning performance across models and training checkpoints, and heads with high within-community and low cross-community weight matter most. The idea is turned into a pruning allocation method (SWA) that cuts WikiText perplexity by up to 20% against competing allocations across six LLMs. Authors: Chinese academic group (Huang, Cao, Zhang, Qiu) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12304

- Reasoning-Token Spikes Under Prompted Untruthful Responding in Large Language Models — A content-independent deception signal: across three reasoning models and 210 questions, being told to answer truthfully yielded fewer reasoning tokens than being told to lie or to ignore truth. It is only a proof of concept for prompted lying, not spontaneous deception, but it is a cheap monitor when traces are hidden or unfaithful, framed through cognitive load theory. Authors: independent / small group (Morales, Dominik, Gao, Shirey) · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/Wakaranaino/token-spike-project) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10405

- The Persona Hierarchy Model: Understanding Contextual Generalization in Fine-Tuning LLMs — Explains why fine-tuning on a fixed context sometimes stays local and sometimes generalises everywhere: changes to the shared default persona transfer broadly, changes to a local persona stay local. Across 120 fine-tuned models, narrowness correlates with persona similarity to the default (r = 0.72 on Qwen3-4B), and a persona-preserving regularisation confines unwanted generalisation. Authors: Northeastern University and Stanford (Zhao, Wu, Bau, Shi) · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found on abstract page · Cognitive side: yes (partly). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09384

Authors: Korean academic group · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09733
- Examining Social Attribution in LLM Reasoning — 7,639-question responsibility/blame benchmark (SAB-Bench) grounded in attribution theory, 32 LLMs, plus probes of how attribution dimensions are represented compared with humans. Authors: Chinese Academy of Sciences group · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and data · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12022
- DecepEval — benchmark pairing neutral and induced versions of agent tasks (pressure, incentive, opportunity, conflict); inducements raise deception in all nine frontier models tested, even those with low baselines. Authors: academic group · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: partly. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07967
- PHRBench — 4,820 controlled instances, 18 LLMs, on how models treat a hallucinated premise in context; successful recovery is rare and goes with more frequent belief updates along the trajectory. Authors: academic group · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10455
- From Uncertainty to Action: Learning to Steer LLM Agents — A table of about 82,000 counterfactual continuations shows uncertainty identifies failing trajectories but not the step where steering helps; a learned Value-of-Steering monitor addresses that. Authors: academic group · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: partly. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09115
