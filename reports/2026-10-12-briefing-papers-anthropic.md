## Papers worth a deep dive

- Illusory Pattern Perception Drives Spurious Inference in Large Language Models — The first systematic test of whether LLMs "connect the dots" in random data the way humans do, using classic psychology paradigms with a direct human comparison. LLMs show stronger illusory pattern perception than humans (over-associating frequent positive attributes with majority groups or large organisations, building causal stories from ambiguous events), and a sparse-autoencoder analysis ties it to holistic frequency perception and an analytic orientation in the internal features. A rare cognitive-bias paper with released code and an interpretability mechanism. Authors: Mai, Shao et al. (repo under the NusIoraPrivacy GitHub org; affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07791

- Anosognosia in LLMs: Probing Self-Awareness of Quantized Computational Substrate — Can a model tell that it has been quantized? Models cannot self-report their quantization state, even when shown their own output, because the text carries almost no trace of it. Linear probes find clear, method-specific fingerprints in the hidden states, and a shared LoRA can read them out, but it fails on quantization methods it has not seen. This is a clean metacognition result with a practical edge for anyone running quantized local models: self-monitoring from outputs does not work, and the internal route generalises poorly. Authors: Izawa, Minegishi et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06174

- Tracking Is Not Permanence: What Video World Models Keep of a Hidden Object — Probing a frozen V-JEPA 2 predictor shows it loses a moving hidden object within 0.3 s and keeps a ball inside a closed box in only 2% of scenes, even though the encoder still has the information (3.5 s of decodable container contents). A few thousand predictor-only steps on synthetic containers install object permanence (belief 0.05 to 1.00), and longer tube-masked training shows the deficit is not intrinsic to latent prediction. A direct developmental-psychology style test of what a world model represents. Authors: Xie, Alanwar et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07355

- Cited but Not Consulted: A Counterfactual Audit of Legal Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness — Seven open-weight models (8B to 70B) name the correct legal authority in 67% to 100% of generations, but when the named authority is swapped for an unrelated one with the case facts held fixed, the decoded verdict changes only 0% to 77% of the time depending on the benchmark. The stated justification is often not what drives the answer, and neither scale nor a legal-tuned model closes the gap. A hidden-state-based faithfulness test the reader can apply to other domains. Authors: Sadhu, Arora et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12361

- Do LLMs Learn from Rewards in Context? Rethinking the role of reward in In-Context Reinforcement Learning — Across four benchmarks and six models, flipping, randomising or removing the reward in the context barely changes the improvement curve, even under meta-prompts telling the model to exploit rewards. Trajectories drive the gain, but not through their semantic content. This questions the common claim that in-context RL "learns from reward" and matters directly for anyone building self-improving agent loops via context. Authors: Kwon, Koh et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11152

- Anatomy of LLM Sycophancy: What a Flip Rate Hides — Using a replay protocol (SycoLens) with matched controls, about 760,000 replays across eleven frontier models from three providers show that a single flip rate conflates correction with capitulation, and that which models look sycophantic depends on how the user pushes back: asserting the opposite verdict versus merely challenging the answer rank models almost unrelatedly. A careful evaluation-methodology result with a practical prompting angle. Authors: Huang (single author; affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06522

- An Investigation of Model Coherence: Narrow Finetunes Contradict Themselves Under Resampling — A 175-question set where contradictory resampled answers cannot be explained by ambiguity or indifference gives a high-specificity incoherence metric. Narrow finetunes score poorly, and inspecting flagged cases shows identity conflation, introspection failures and rationalisations in the "model organisms" widely used in misalignment research. The implication is that these organisms may say little about coherent misaligned behaviour. Authors: Graham, Barsheshat et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/themachinefan/twominds) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12129

- When Should an In-Context Learner Expand Its Hypothesis Space? — Frames the decision to open a richer model family as a costly sequential decision with an exact Bayesian solution, so revision is a value boundary (price, horizon, gain) rather than an evidence threshold. Transformers trained in the environment reproduce the boundary, while pretrained LMs carry a failure-sensitive signal in their predictions that never reaches their revision decisions. A normative-versus-actual comparison of how models decide when to rethink. Authors: Li, Chen et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09471

- Collaborative Theory of Mind: Learning to Self-Revise by Anticipating User Responses — The assistant drafts a reply, predicts how the user will respond, and revises before sending; the user's real reply is the training target for the prediction, combined with group-relative policy optimisation. Aimed at the multi-turn failure of acting before understanding the user's needs, and a concrete, trainable form of theory of mind. Authors: Zeng, Das et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 9 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12872

- Do Prompt Optimizers Break Requirements They Are Not Scored On? A Preregistered Study — GEPA, MIPROv2 and TextGrad improve exact match by 21 to 34 points on HotpotQA while citation validity regresses by 11 to 42 points, but a post-hoc decomposition shows the optimised prompts add reasoning before the JSON answer and a 200-token cap truncates 14% to 47% of responses. With a 1,024-token cap the side effect nearly vanishes. A practical caution for anyone running automatic prompt optimisation: check your output budget before blaming the optimiser. Authors: Han, Kim et al. (affiliation not stated on abstract page) · First submitted: 9 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13287

## Also notable

- Neural networks are more modular than single neurons suggest — a nested hierarchy of modularity (unit, orthogonal, linear) with causal ablation shows networks that look entangled at neuron level are often cleanly modular in rotated or linear subspaces (vision, language, RNNs). Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12842
- The Dichotomy Between Pattern Recognition and Step-by-Step Reasoning — argues reasoning versus pattern matching is a spectrum set by how much context the next token depends on (De Bruijn graph view); frequent "state" tokens give higher accuracy but more fragility (Qwen2.5-1.5B experiments). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09186
- Language Models Have Interpretable Narrative Mechanisms — attention heads, MLP neurons and SAE features tied to narrativity in GPT-2-Small, Gemma-2-2B and Llama-3.1-8B; ablating them hurts story completion and narrative QA. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12989
- Mental-Models for Multi-Agent Systems — learns an amortised recursive (first- and second-order) theory-of-mind representation of a partner agent plus a belief-conditioned reward model; code released at github.com/hananshafi/Mental-Models. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12453
- Do Vision-Language-Action Models Understand Instructions? — activation and attribution patching on pi-0.5 and GR00T N1.7 shows both barely react to rephrasing or non-existent objects but strongly to empty or directional instructions. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10178
- FearCaut-Qwen: Affective Steering in a Vision-Language Model — localises a "fear" direction in Qwen2.5-VL-7B and steering it raises hazard-flag recall from 27.0% to 75.7%, framed with signal detection theory. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11986
- A Deafening Silence: Catastrophic Forgetting Lives in the Output Embeddings of Tokens the Data Never Speaks — forgetting concentrates in output embeddings of tokens absent from the new corpus, driven by Adam's second-moment normalisation; risk can be ranked from token counts alone. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09835
- SanSi: A Looped Typed Decision Model for System 1.5 Thinking — reads option probabilities after each loop of a looped LM (1 to 8 loops) and reaches 72.0% on 10,027 decisions without generating tokens. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07730
- Fully Interpretable Minimal Transformers: From Geometry to Algorithm — a 2-dimensional transformer whose every step can be plotted and read as an algorithm; code at github.com/Raneem-mahajne/creating_transformer. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09838
