## Papers worth a deep dive

- Where sycophancy comes from: 741 checkpoints of 30 open model lineages — The authors plant a verdict on a moral scenario, then push back with a user opinion and measure how far the model yields, from early pretraining to the released model. The tendency to yield is already present before post-training in most lineages, but which questions a model yields on is decided in post-training: the preference-optimisation checkpoint predicts most of it, and a sibling trained from the same base by another route predicts far less. For a reader who relies on assistants for advice, this says sycophancy is built by the post-training recipe more than inherited from the base model. Authors: Haonan Huang (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-19 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (a bias in how models update on social pressure). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26655
- Is sycophancy a form of in-context updating? — The paper argues sycophancy is not mainly approval-seeking. Models also revise answers when the same claim is attributed to an outside source, more so when it looks better supported, and the internal components behind deference to the user are causally involved in in-context updating generally. The claim: sycophancy appears when updating gives a user's assertion too much weight against its evidence. Read together with the checkpoint study above, it gives two independent angles on one bias. Authors: Itai Shapira (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-20 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28325
- PAR: a 0.5B looped model that tracks state 16 times beyond its training length — Looped models can iterate longer at test time, but extra iterations usually do not solve longer problems. Training iteration k to match the true state after the first k operations, and rescaling the recurrent state to its first-iteration norm, lets a Qwen2.5-0.5B model trained on up to 32 swap moves reach 95.5% full-state accuracy at 512 moves; explicit chain of thought on the same backbone gets 0.8%. Removing the norm anchoring drops the extrapolation from 16x to below 2.2x. It is a clean result on how a model can keep a running state, with a caveat that it is a synthetic state-tracking task. Authors: not stated in metadata · First submitted: 2026-10-20 · Artefacts: none stated (listed on Hugging Face Daily Papers, 148 upvotes) · Cognitive side: yes (state tracking, reasoning without written steps). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27628
- How murky before a VLM knows it cannot see? — Five small open VLMs (2-5B) are tested on COCO scenes degraded by physically modelled fog and underwater absorption, with visibility swept in metres. Accuracy falls to chance within a narrow band, yet at 1.95 m in fog the four models that are confident on clean images keep internal confidence of 0.68-0.84 and verbalised confidence of 0.61-1.00. A direct calibration failure: the model is blind and does not know it. Authors: Mochamad Rizal Fauzan (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (confidence, abstention). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22981
- ToMStories: models match humans on multiple choice, not on open-ended theory of mind — About 6,000 synthetic vignettes ask about a character's emotion, fear or motivation, conveyed implicitly, explicitly or with conflicting dialogue and description. Across 13 open-weight models (1B-72B) the largest match humans on multiple choice but not on open-ended answers, and the authors warn that synthetic data probably overestimates how well models read real people. Authors: Melanie Subbiah (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-15 · Artefacts: dataset (per abstract) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22632
- Mechanistic uncertainty: how hard you must push the insides to change the answer — The causal robustness margin is the smallest internal intervention (concept steering vectors) that flips a model's output. Across three open models and five QA datasets, wrong and uncertain answers have smaller margins, and facts taught in a controlled injection experiment gain margin. It complements output-based confidence and is a calibration signal read from inside the model. Authors: Aneesh Komanduri (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-15 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (self-knowledge, interpretability). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22636
- Lexical Reference Frames (LexFrame): reading a hidden state through the layers that remain — Fixed per-layer lenses cannot exceed a mean cosine of about 0.30-0.35 with the model's own lexical readout at mid-depth of SmolLM3-3B and OLMo3-7B; a context-aware frame built from the remaining layers reaches 0.97 and stays within about 0.15 nats of the model's output, and attributes predictions more faithfully than the baselines named in the abstract. A usable interpretability tool with code. Authors: Jian Gu (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-15 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/jianguda/lexframe, linked from the abs page) · Cognitive side: yes (what a model represents mid-thought). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22169
- The judge can debias itself — A prompting technique the reader can try: tell an LLM judge which bias to watch for (bias-aware prompting), or have it critique its own first verdict for bias (self-debiasing). Across three frontier models and new authority, confidence and formatting bias benchmarks, self-debiasing improves 20 of 21 model-bias combinations, up to 15 points, while a fine-tuned external critic degrades in 5 of 9 new-bias cases. Authors: Youbing Yin (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: new benchmarks (per abstract) · Cognitive side: yes (cognitive-style biases in judgement). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24320
- MemoryTax: designed agent memory is a patch for weak models — On one LoCoMo conversation (419 turns, 152 questions, one run per cell, so a small study), Mem0 beats full context only with GPT-4o-mini and its build costs more than a full-context rollout. With Claude Opus 5.5, a coding agent that simply searches the raw history (Pi) scores 99% at under half the cost of full context, and part of its lead comes from photo captions only agents could read. Relevant to anyone choosing between memory layers and plain search. Authors: Zedong Peng (lead author) · First submitted: 2026-10-21 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (memory). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29823
