The lab itself warns that agentic benchmark scores shifted with the harness (Terminus 2 versus OpenCode), so the comparison is best-effort. The company's main business is AI chip design; the general model is a variant of the one behind that platform. On OpenRouter it is listed at $0.20 per million input and $0.60 per million output tokens (created 4 November 19:03 UTC). - 🚨 Moonshot's Kimi K3.1 Pro weights are on Hugging Face, with a licence that sets a revenue threshold for hosting companies — This is the follow-up to the 24 October API launch, when Moonshot promised weights by 4 November; the Hugging Face repository was created on 2 November and the community noticed it on 4 November (its Hacker News submission reached 404 points). The model card gives 2.8T total and 104B active parameters, 93 layers (69 linear-attention "KDA" and 24 gated DeepSeek-sparse-attention layers), 896 experts with 16 active, 1,048,576-token context, text and image input, and MXFP4 weights with MXFP8 activations from quantisation-aware training; the repository holds about 1.56 TB in 96 safetensors shards. The licence is custom and permissive for most users, but a company that runs a "Model as a Service" business and earns over $20M in aggregate revenue over 12 months must sign a separate agreement with Moonshot, and products above 100M monthly users or $20M monthly revenue must display "Kimi K3.1-Pro" in the interface; internal use and use through Moonshot's official products or certified partners is exempt. Moonshot's own table has Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7 against 66.4 for Claude Opus 5.5 and 58.2 for GPT-6 Astra, and is well behind both on ProgramBench (31.5 against 53.5 to 65.0). Hacker News commenters say third-party hosts (OpenRouter, Together, Modal) already serve it, report doubled throughput over Moonshot's endpoint, and argue about whether the model is "benchmaxxed"; one notes that running it locally needs roughly an 8x B300 node. Artefacts: weights (custom "kimi-k3.1-pro" licence) / model card / API · Sizes or variants: K3.1 Pro (a faster "Highspeed" variant is API only) · Released: weights on Hugging Face 2 November, publicly noticed 4 November 2026. - AI21 Labs (Israel) publishes two case studies of post-training NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Super to beat frontier models on narrow tasks at a fraction of the price — In the Deel study (4 November), a payroll-question model trained for three weeks with just 412 curated examples for supervised fine-tuning and 698 questions for self-distillation policy optimisation (SDPO) lifted criteria met on Deel's EmBench Payroll from 0.513 to 0.720, against 0.640 for Claude Opus 5.5, and AI21 says it costs about 50 times less per million tokens.
