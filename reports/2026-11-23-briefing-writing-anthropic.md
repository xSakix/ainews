## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- How Public Evals Work and Fail, and What Should Change — Eugene Yan reads twelve public agent evals (four coding/science, six knowledge work, two browsing/computer use) and finds evidence the grader failed correct work in eight of the twelve. Numbers: reviewers found 41 of 65 Terminal Bench 4.0 tasks graded on unstated requirements; 23 of 113 DeepSWE tasks marked correct solutions as failures; on BrowseComp trainers disagreed with the reference answer on 50 of 367 questions (14%); Artificial Analysis found material hallucinations in over 60% of Harvey LAB results that passed every criterion; 55% of AutomationBench assertions only check that a string is present. His argument: report pass^k (an agent that solves 70% of tasks every time is not the same as one that solves every task 70% of the time; over five trials 70% becomes 17%) and treat human review attention, not dollars per task, as the scarce cost (on AutomationBench, models declared success on 72-91% of analysed failures; GDPval: GPT-4o cost about a tenth of GPT-5 per task but was slower than an expert once review and redo time counted). Catch: the grader-failure counts come from public issue trackers and reviewers' reports that Yan collates, not from a re-grading of his own, and the "what should change" section is his opinion, which he labels partly "vibes". Worth reading before trusting the next leaderboard number. Author: Eugene Yan, applied ML practitioner and writer · Format: deep dive · Length: about 42 min read. Source: https://eugeneyan.com/writing/public-evals

- Inkling 2 Mini on vLLM: Day-0 Support for a Looped Model — The serving-side write-up of Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini (the release itself was covered in earlier briefings; what is new is the engineering explanation). It is a 26B-total, 3.5B-active multimodal MoE with 1M context whose middle 12 layers (6-17) run three times per token with shared weights; each pass keeps its own KV cache and receives gated "messages" from the previous pass at every sublayer, and all three passes start from the same prelude output rather than chaining. vLLM treats each pass's cache as an ordinary cache layer, fuses the message reads, uses a native DSpark speculator (7 speculative tokens in the example command), and also serves a research checkpoint (TML-d24-varloop-research) where the number of passes and whether passes share a KV cache are set at startup via --hf-overrides. Accuracy check: per-layer hidden states at all 48 layer visits differ from the reference by about as much as FlashAttention-4 and PyTorch attention differ from each other; end-to-end checks were needle-in-a-haystack to 1M tokens, MMMU-Pro, HLE and GSM8K. Catch: it is written by the serving team with the lab and NVIDIA, gives no throughput numbers in the text I read, and the accuracy statement is qualitative. Author: Inferact, Thinking Machines and NVIDIA (vLLM team) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 8 min read. Source: https://blog.vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-20-inkling2-mini

- Keeping Agents Fast: High-Interactivity Decode with FlyDSL MonoKernels in vLLM — How AMD-side engineers cut small-batch decode latency by fusing a decoder layer's dependent stages into one or two persistent GPU launches (weight prefetch, data-ready handoffs), because at concurrency 1 the gaps between kernels dominate and HIP graphs only remove CPU launch cost. Numbers on merged PRs: concurrency-1 speedup over stock vLLM 1.20x to 2.26x across six PRs; DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on one TP2 MI355X node goes from 318.1 to 429.1 tok/s/user (P90 interactivity); a standard decoder layer drops from 32 kernels in 320 microseconds to 2 kernels in 115 microseconds on MI325X; a TP8 decode step with DSpark drops from 8.59 to 5.36 ms (392 to 580 tok/s, using a synthetic acceptance length of 3.87). Catch, stated by the authors: gains shrink with concurrency (DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash 1.35x falls to 1.14x at concurrency 8; MiniMax-M3 reaches parity at 16), TP8 mono decode delivers 26% less throughput than two TP4 replicas at concurrency 4, MI300X/MI325X kernels keep their own weight copy (170 GiB versus 99 GiB per rank), accuracy for the TP8 configuration is still being evaluated, and numbers were measured by the PR authors. Author: AMD and vLLM contributors (Felix Li, Emad Barsoum and others) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 20 min read. Source: https://blog.vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-18-flydsl-monokernels

- Does a model's character affect how it judges other models? — A small, honest experiment on oversight: four small open models (Qwen3-1.7B and 8B, Llama-3.2-3B and Llama-3.1-8B), each LoRA-trained into five characters (optimistic, pessimistic, cautious, neutral, conformist) with five seeds, giving 120 judges that scored 360 short stories. Findings: a judge's character shifts scores on identical stories by 2.85 points (1-9 scale) between optimistic and pessimistic judges, but judges favour their own model family or character by only +0.11 points, and sharing a family does not help a judge identify the author's character (same-family advantage 0.02 in probability); judges project their own trait (optimistic judges call 48% of authors optimistic versus 25% for base judges). Catch: tiny models, 100-word stories and a non-verifiable moral-score task, a single-author LessWrong post without peer review; it says little about frontier monitors. ## Essays and arguments

- Perplexity and Intelligence — OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki argues that pretraining loss on held-out data (log-perplexity) is the metric that best tracks emergent capability, and publishes OpenAI's own series for the first time. His numbers: GPT-3.5 about 0.99, GPT-4 about 0.91, models below 0.8 show transformative cybersecurity and maths capability, a ~0.76 model began resolving open maths problems, and "further transformative capabilities" are expected in the 0.68-0.70 window; he proposes industry "common limits" on perplexity and other measures that labs commit not to cross until oversight tools (for example a successor to chain-of-thought monitors) reach a stated milestone. He notes that the data is OpenAI's internal codebase held-out set, that perplexity is hard to compare across labs, ignores gains from chain-of-thought, and that the 2026 acceleration is attributed mostly to research and compute, only partly to AI automation. Opinion of an interested party, with the chart as the only evidence. The Hacker News thread (506 points, 100 comments) objects that code perplexity need not map to general ability, and one commenter measured Inkling 2 Mini at perplexity 5.7 on his own text set, which would place it between GPT-6.2 Astra and OpenAI's internal model on the plotted scale, so the metric is not comparable across datasets. A LessWrong post adds that per-token perplexity is not comparable across labs because tokenizers differ: Anthropic uses more tokens per word, which would make its models look closer to any fixed bar; OpenAI staff replied that no specific limit was proposed. ## Prompting and context techniques

- Genie 4 prompt and controls guide (Google, Gemini API docs) — Google's tested patterns for steering its real-time world model (genie-4, 24 fps streaming, generally available 19 November). Techniques with enough detail to try: (1) bind "Rule:" cause-and-effect clauses to a concrete object or character, never a location ("Pulling the red lever..." works; "Entering the bridge" misfires), place the trigger "far away from me" or it fires during setup, and tell characters to wait; (2) give key-bound actions via client-side impulses (10 repeats at 150 ms, 2-second cooldown) when a guaranteed trigger is needed, since Rule clauses are probabilistic; (3) seed with a 16:9 reference image under 48 KB over WebRTC; (4) request broadband physical Foley (footsteps on wet cobblestone, leaves rustling), not music or narrowband drones, which the prompt planner strips or rewrites, and state the visible propulsion source at frame 0 (otherwise the model invents a boat hull or vehicle hood); (5) expect a rolling canvas memory (turn 180 degrees and back after 10 seconds and the scene is reinterpreted) and long-horizon drift near 90 seconds. Evidence is Google's own "live testing", with no published failure rates; the guide itself calls the phrasing rules empirical workarounds. Pricing in the docs: $1.50 per 1M input tokens, $9.00 per 1M text output tokens, $17.50 per 1M video output tokens (about $0.10 per second). Author: Google (Gemini API documentation) · Format: prompting guide · Length: about 15 min read. Source: https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/genie-prompting
