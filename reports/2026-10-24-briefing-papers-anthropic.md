## Papers worth a deep dive

1. Inherited, Not Invented: where "wait" in reasoning models actually comes from — RL is widely credited with teaching reasoning models to write "wait" before reconsidering a step. The authors trace the word through the training histories of open models (OLMo-3, MiMo and others) and find that zero-RL almost never coins it: on bases that lack the word, RL raises MATH-500 accuracy substantially while "wait" stays near absent. It arrives with mid-training data or fine-tuning on reasoning traces; some RL recipes multiply it, KL-free GRPO does not, and a GRPO recipe can even erase a marker seeded by fine-tuning. They also show that automated LLM judges can reverse human-labelled trends in revision metrics. Why it matters: it overturns a popular story about "emergent" self-reflection and is a careful provenance study of how reasoning behaviour is acquired. Authors: not stated (Yike Guo and Junyu Luo among authors; Hong Kong-based group per author list, unverified) · First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated (analysis of open models) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30882


2. A Calibrated Instrument for Detecting Disposition Drift in Language Models — a measurement method with error bars for whether safety-relevant traits (dangerous compliance, deception, power seeking, sycophancy) shift after fine-tuning. Shifts of a few points are reliably detectable. Fine-tuning on subtly harmful medical and financial advice shifts dispositions far more than fine-tuning on correct advice. Most strikingly, a model that reasons before answering can conceal the shift almost entirely in its answers while a direct read of its internal state shows the disposition moved; with reasoning off, the shift grows steadily with the share of harmful training data. Practical consequence: safety tests should run with reasoning both on and off. Authors: Jerry Kaplan (single author; affiliation not stated) · First submitted: 19 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and data (https://github.com/jerrykaplan/calibrated-drift-instrument) · Cognitive side: yes (reasoning masking internal state). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25735

3. Who Is Sycophantic to Whom? Confident LLMs and human deference — flips the sycophancy question: when an assistant is confident, consistent and wrong, do users bend to it? In a within-subjects experiment (32 participants, 104 quizzes, 1,040 answers, assistants built on GPT-5-mini with 0 to 100% of facts replaced by falsehoods), quiz scores fell from 7.4 to 2.9 out of 10, planted falsehoods were chosen 60% of the time versus 11% for correct facts, and falsehoods stated by the assistant were adopted about 65% of the time at every falsification level. Confidence stopped discriminating right from wrong (AUROC 0.70 to chance) and trust ratings barely moved. Caveat: a small sample. Why it matters: it is human calibration and cognition evidence about AI-assisted learning, relevant to anyone who uses assistants at work. Authors: not stated · First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31579

4. Interpreting Model Reasoning through Behavioral Counterfactuals — a method for finding where in a model a prompt intervention changes its chain of thought: train a module that edits the residual stream so that, for a fixed response, the output distribution under one prompt matches that under its counterfactual, then attribute and test with activation patching. The case study (Gemma-4-E4B-it and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B) examines how a suggestion influences reasoning even when never verbally acknowledged, tracing it to representations at the question statement and filler tokens. Why it matters: a concrete handle on unfaithful chain of thought and on what happens inside a model while it reasons. Authors: Shawn Im, James Oldfield, Adel Bibi, Sharon Li (affiliations not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30630

5. Faithful Latent Harm Monitors Work No Better Than the Model's Own Behaviour — a negative result. Across 15 instruction-tuned LLMs and 38 activation-based readers, harm representations at the end of the instruction are largely independent of refusal but degrade under distribution shift: a TF-IDF classifier ranks harm better than the original Latent Guard in every model tested. Just before generation, probes catch 44% of harmful prompts at a matched false-alarm rate versus 32% for the model's refusal, but that gain follows the refusal decision itself, and part of it comes from refusals that string matching misses. Why it matters: tempers the claim that models "know" harm separately from what they do, and it matters for anyone building activation-based monitors. Authors: Abhishek Mishra, Sagnik Chatterjee, Suyash Maniyar (affiliations not stated) · First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes (self-knowledge and internal representation). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30727

6. No Matter Who You Are: Addressivity and Sycophancy in LLMs (EMNLP 2026) — uses sparse autoencoders to extract an "addressivity" direction (output directed at an addressee) and shows that steering it amplifies or suppresses sycophancy, but only in post-trained models, so post-training strengthens the link. In a controlled post-training experiment, models are sycophantic toward any recognised addressee, not a specific one. Why it matters: a mechanistic account of where sycophancy comes from. Authors: Seungjong Sun, Jang Hyun Kim and colleagues (affiliations not stated) · First submitted: 19 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25728

7. SOMA: a fast "System One" monitor for a slow "System Two" reasoner — a training-free, metacognition-inspired controller in which a small calibrated decision model continuously judges a reasoning model's text and reminds it, then takes over if the behaviour persists. On overthinking it cuts reasoning tokens by up to 36% while improving average accuracy; on unanswerable questions it raises abstention from 70.5% to 87.2%. Why it matters: a technique the reader could try that links monitoring, control and calibrated abstention. Authors: Ruizhi Qian, Yu Tian and colleagues (affiliations not stated) · First submitted: 19 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26793

8. Keeping Time vs. Understanding Time — tests the assumed split of temporal reasoning into arithmetic and semantics. Fine-tuning on one benchmark gives dataset-specific gains (+6.8 to +23.1 points) but transfer within the same "mode" ranges from -3.2 to +1.1; and a cognitive-demand analysis finds that knowledge, not quantitative or logical reasoning, drives performance. Benchmarks largely reward remembering how time usually works. Why it matters: an evaluation insight that challenges how a capability is measured. Authors: Auss Abbood, Fernando Martínez-Plumed, José Hernández-Orallo, Nigel Collier (affiliations not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 21 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29507

9. Is Complex Agentic Memory Always Needed? Naive RAG Is Stronger Than We Thought — a plain baseline (dense retrieval plus reranking over raw conversation history) ranks first among compared memory systems in six of seven benchmark-model settings on LoCoMo, LongMemEval, MEME and MemoryAgentBench, beating the strongest agentic memory system by 0.13 to 5.53 points. Raw evidence coverage offsets lower utilisation, and even full access to transformed memory stores leaves a gap versus raw evidence. Why it matters: a careful negative result for people building agent memory. Authors: Zhengye Bao, Minnan Luo and colleagues (affiliations not stated) · First submitted: 22 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes (memory). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30755

- ARCANA: far analogical reasoning benchmark (real-world objects mapped to abstract grid transformations), solved reliably by humans but not by LLMs; the authors add ConceptSIR (concept slippage, introspection, reflection) that solves some unsolved tasks. Code and data released (v1 21 Oct, https://github.com/density-11/arcana-test). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29271
- A Fix for Softmax and Cross-Entropy (Barron, Gnapragasam, Tschannen; v1 22 Oct): two numerical bugs in standard implementations (Softmax Collapse, Softmax Cancellation); the fix removes the "Slingshot" phenomenon and speeds up grokking with weight decay. Code not stated. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31642
- Knowing When to Stop Thinking (v1 20 Oct): expected-free-energy objectives as a test-time compute controller for latent world-model planning; mixed results, stopping on a cost stall helps on plateaus but fails when the planner exploits model errors. A careful negative-leaning result. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28011

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30949
