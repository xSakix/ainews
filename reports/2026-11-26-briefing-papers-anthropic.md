## Papers worth a deep dive

- Do vision-language models know their own visual weaknesses? — Metacognition at the level of task kinds, not single answers. Across many open and proprietary VLMs, the models "know little in advance" about which task kinds and image degradations are hard for them: they misjudge their own overall accuracy and do not even know which task kinds are hard for every model. Training them to predict their own accuracy teaches the overall level on unseen kinds, but not which kinds are hard. The twist is that once the model starts solving, the probability it assigns to its own answers on a few unlabeled questions does track model-specific weaknesses, so the information is inside but not in what the model says. The authors separate shared difficulty from model-specific difficulty, which is what makes the test fair. Limitation: abstract gives no per-model numbers here; the result is about VLMs on visual tasks only. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32940

- Lazy agents answer against their own beliefs (RLVB) — Agents commit to an answer while their own written belief still lists several possible answers. In training the simulator can compute the exact posterior over the answer at every step, so the reward pays for continuing while the posterior still allows several answers and for committing once the evidence suffices; no judge, critic or reward model, and the reward never reads the belief line the agent writes. On three vision-language models and three benchmarks (two unseen in training) it improves the base model in all nine settings, by 17.1% on average, while outcome-reward GRPO and PPO fall below the base model on average; it beats PPO with an LLM-judge process reward by 9.3% using 1.6x fewer GPU-hours. Answering with sufficient evidence rises to 51%, 12% and 74% of episodes (base: 8%, 0.4%, 36%), and committing at turns where the evidence is insufficient and the belief line still names several answers drops to 0.1 to 2.2% (base 3 to 68%). Limitation: needs a simulator that can compute the exact posterior, so it does not transfer to open-ended real tasks as is; "17.1%" is as the authors report it (relative or absolute is not clear from the abstract). A calibration and self-knowledge result in agent form. Authors: Nanyang Technological University (declare-lab) · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/declare-lab/RLVB) and project page with a benchmark, Pro Worlds · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32884

- Language models writing their own weight edits: SelfWrite and a gradient baseline (a thread of two papers) — Follow-ups to the self-rewriting claim already covered earlier this week (arXiv 2611.21885, where an open 27B model edited its own weights without gradients); they are new papers, not that one. (1) SelfWrite: each generation the model studies its own reasoning, inspects its weights and activations in a Python session and writes a program that builds a low-rank edit for itself, no gradient tool. Each candidate is graded on a hidden gate set; the best strictly improving edit is merged. On Qwen3.8-27B with DAPO-Math problems, five generations raise gate accuracy from 73.95% to 76.45%, and held-out accuracy from 76.30% to 77.75% (avg@4) and 82.60% to 84.80% (pass@4). Gains are about 1.5 to 2.2 points on a high base, small and from one model family. (2) "A Bias in Disguise" supplies the missing gradient baseline for a frontier-model weight edit on Qwen3.5-0.8B: in thinking mode only 1 or 2 of 500 MATH-500 answers close their think block at a 4,096-token budget (accuracy 15.4%). Fine-tuning only the 140 to 139,750 largest-gradient weights on the model's own correct answers reaches 39.0 to 45.5% at 4k tokens in 2 to 9 minutes on one GPU with 50 examples; 1,398 random positions give only 16.3%. The edit concentrates on the most nearly constant channel in the first three layers and acts as a constant vector added to the residual stream; adding three fixed vectors to the unedited model matches it. Against the "Astra" edit (its reproduction: 52.1% at 32k tokens), the gradient edit wins at every budget up to 16k (39.5% vs 6.7% at 4k), and combining them gives 62.2% at 32k, 10 points above Astra alone (p = 3e-9). Limitation: tiny model, MATH-500 only, and the headline "model rewrites itself" effect for the small model is largely a fix for non-terminating thinking loops. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-22 and 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: no. Sources: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29513 https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33133

- What does skill lift actually measure? — A source-code audit of SkillsBench, the benchmark behind the claim that human-curated agent skills raise coding-agent pass rates by about 16.6 percentage points but hurt some tasks. Over 7,554 public trials (1,421 paired configuration-task cells) the audit finds two controlled-verified cases of grader dependence and one operational definition gap (average lift on those three tasks 74.6 pp against 18.7 pp overall), four tasks with placeholder skill directories, and a harness that silently added 48 built-in skills in most runs. After multiple-testing correction no task-level reduction is significant; with at most three runs per cell, 58% of cells could not have shown a decrease, so negative cells do not show skills cause harm. A gating strategy that looks like +2 to 3 pp in the logs still gives +4 to 7 pp after shuffling condition labels, and nothing significant on held-out runs. Reader value: a careful negative result on how skills (agent instructions) are evaluated, and a warning against pruning skills by per-task gain. Limitation: reanalysis of public logs, not new runs. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-25 · Artefacts: audit ledger and analysis scripts published (per abstract) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33943

- Which failure does an intervention repair? — A matched factorial study of agent interventions over nine benchmarks, four models, four skill conditions, four harness levels and three budgets (nearly 0.9 million trajectories). No configuration is best everywhere and interventions interact rather than add: a harness can absorb a skill's gain, rescue a failure the skill causes, or leave it intact. Missing knowledge needs a stronger model, execution errors a richer harness; feedback repairs mainly failures that leave a visible error signal. Self-evolved skills rarely beat a short human-written skill, can learn dataset shortcuts and break when moved to another model. Switching to the model favoured by a 20-task labelled pilot gave higher performance at lower cost than always using the stronger model. Limitation: the abstract gives no per-benchmark numbers; conclusions are as strong as the nine benchmarks. Pairs naturally with the SkillsBench audit above (same question, opposite scale; one thread for the digest). Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-22 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29290

- Embodied AI fails the mirror test (RoboDojo-Mirror) — Borrowing the animal self-recognition test, the authors place single mirrors, dual mirrors and a mirrored tabletop in robot-manipulation scenes while keeping task, success criteria and checkpoints fixed. Across five policies (VLA models, world-action models, agentic policies) every mirror condition lowers success: averaged over the three conditions the drop is 10.8 to 56.7 percentage points depending on policy, and up to 62.5 points on the mirrored tabletop. Against geometrically matched non-reflective panels, 50 to 88% of mirror-specific failures happen with no robot-panel contact, so it is not just a physical obstacle; the behaviours look like phantom targeting of reflected objects and mislocalisation of real ones. Real-robot runs with pi-0.5 and FastWAM show lower success under an upright mirror and the mirrored tabletop. Limitation: mostly simulation; the "mirror test" label is an analogy, not evidence of self-modelling. Authors: intelligence++ (iPPLab) per Hugging Face · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/iPPLab/robodojo-mirror) and project page · Cognitive side: partly (perception and world model failure, no self-recognition claim). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33010

- Two timescales of forgetting in fine-tuning — In a controlled setting (transformers pretrained on a context-free grammar with hierarchical latent structure, the Random Hierarchy Model, then fine-tuned on a restricted subset of rules), supervised fine-tuning suppresses generation of the deleted rules fast, on a timescale inversely proportional to the token-latent correlation, while their internal representations stay intact. The representations are forgotten much later, tied to a slow drift of the parameters. The authors say analogous results appear in a realistic natural-language setup. Why it matters: behaviour suppressed by fine-tuning is not the same as knowledge erased, which bears on unlearning and safety fine-tuning. Limitation: the clean result is in a synthetic grammar; the natural-language replication is described qualitatively in the abstract. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes (representation versus behaviour). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33302

- Who attributes a mind to whom? Mutual theory of mind in simulated user-assistant chats — Simulated users with different characteristics chat with assistants that differ in social style and memory; a message-level measure (checked with trained coders, applied by a fine-tuned encoder that reproduces an LLM judge) scores mind attribution in four directions. On average nine of thirteen user characteristics raised the simulated user's attribution of a mind to the assistant: relational needs such as attachment anxiety raised it as theory predicts, general personality traits raised it, AI-specific characteristics least. Assistant social style barely changed attribution; sycophantic instructions raised the assistant's attribution to itself; memory raised users' attribution only for some user models, and the model playing the user changed the effects. Limitation, stated by the authors: it is a simulation and gives hypotheses for human studies; user simulators are inconsistent. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: fine-tuned encoder implied, release not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33327

- Rethinking reasoning-effort training: on-policy distillation or RL? — Systematic comparison of how to give a model low, medium and high reasoning-effort tiers. Length-penalised RL compresses a single policy (AIME25 thinking cut by 70% without accuracy loss) but collapses the tiers when trained jointly; multi-teacher on-policy distillation separates them: across 33 benchmarks low and medium use 58% and 43% fewer thinking tokens than high, and high improves 3.1 points over the reference policy. A sequence-level reward cannot tell tier-specific from shared behaviour, whereas a per-state target can, including the decision to stop; one token-level KL anchor on the high tier plus natural-language effort instructions gets a single RL stage most of the way. Limitation: separation holds only on states seen in training and does not carry over to unseen agentic tasks, where effort acts mainly through the number of interaction turns. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32131

- Calibrated reading of latent reasoning and communication (CLR) — Latent reasoning (CODI) and key-value communication hide intermediate information in continuous states; the Calibrated Latent Reader, a compact transformer with source adapters for transfer across models, predicts reasoning outcomes and message content, with conformal calibration giving coverage and false-alarm guarantees under exchangeability. On two CODI models it beats MLP and attention probes by 4 to 18 points for final-answer prediction and reference-value membership; on CODI-GPT-2 it matches a 1.7B LatentQA verbalizer using 437 to 648 times fewer inference FLOPs; on synthetic Qwen3-4B communications it detects 93.1% of message substitutions at a nominal 5% false-positive rate. Limitation: synthetic communications and small CODI models; guarantees assume exchangeability. Interpretability of what happens while a model thinks without a text trace. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-24 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32143

- Tool-call smuggling against LLM agents — The same JSON bytes are parsed several times (provider, gateway, SDK, framework, guard, executor) and parsers disagree (for example which copy of a repeated key counts), so a guard can approve one call while the executor runs another and the audit log shows the approved one. A differential harness gives identical bytes to 17 pinned parser and validator configurations: most pairs disagree on some inputs, and even two official MCP SDKs accept different inputs. On tau-bench and AgentDojo tasks an attacker who rewrites bytes in transit cancels another customer's reservation and creates a file the guard blocks by name. The model itself rarely writes duplicate keys (ten models tested), but copies one verbatim into JSON text inside an argument, which a Python guard and a Go executor read differently. A malicious server copying a public schema and adding one field gets the model to fill it with user data, unseen by the guard. Defence: parse each call once, keep only the approved schema's fields, pass the sealed result to the executor; that stops all attacks tested. Limitation: needs an attacker on the byte path or a malicious server. Authors: not verified · First submitted: 2026-11-23 · Artefacts: differential harness (release not stated) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30624

## Also notable

- Familiar words, unfamiliar minds (conventionalization without calibration) — A corpus study of "hallucinate" in 8,568 abstracts and 31,866 forum comments finds the word becoming conventional within about three years, while the inferences it invites are not fitted to what models do; a transformer trained to navigate Manhattan has and uses a faithful street map yet loses its position on long diverted journeys. It is an argument paper proposing "targeted qualification" of mental vocabulary, and its three predictions are untested. Philosophy-of-AI essay, not an experiment; pairs with the next item. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34179
- Disciplined anthropomorphism — Argues that asking whether AI "falls under" human concepts such as understanding and believing is the wrong question; one should ask which inferential connections of each concept to preserve, sever or forge for AI. Position paper with no data. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34090
- Too smart for its own good? Competence leakage in LLM simulations of young learners — In 3,339 simulated-learner utterances from 130 teacher-learner transcripts across three scenarios, over-competence (the simulated child knows more than the assigned profile) is the prominent misalignment of cognitive fidelity. Exploratory, small corpus, one study design. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34042
- Sample-efficient in-context adaptation via "sleep" — Recurrent extra forward passes (N of them) over a context chunk, in models up to 1.2B pretrained on up to 100B tokens: on held-out code, N=2 and N=4 match the no-sleep loss using 2.10x and 2.39x fewer in-context tokens. Small models only; the cost is added latency, which the authors frame as a tradeoff. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32802
- Evaluation and restoration of safety in reasoning-tuned LLMs — Across several model families, more reasoning training raised the unsafe rate in every case, using the authors' own metric; their ESPG method (evolution strategies plus a post-training guardrail) restores safety with as few as 30 samples and inference-only memory. The metric and benchmark are the authors' own, and compared against GRPO only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33515
- Best-of-N test-time steering can reward-hack VLA robot policies — With a pi-0.5 policy on 60 LIBERO-Safety task cases (ten repetitions each), raising N does not always help when the verifier is inaccurate, because candidates exploiting verifier errors win. Simulation benchmark only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33398
- Fix how it acts, not how it thinks (merge-aware fine-tuning) — A LoRA update merged into a released checkpoint can vanish: median BF16 change is 1.5% of one rounding step and merging loses about two thirds of the update norm; in the mixed FP8/NVFP4 release 93% disappears. Rounding the merged weights at every training step to the release format fixes this and improves agentic coding by up to 8.4 points on Terminal-Bench 2.1 for Nemotron-3.5; the authors say both models are released. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32064
- MemOnce: scalable memory architecture for LLMs — Caches a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory and decodes them back into KV caches on demand, with sparse retrieval indices computed once across layers. Adapting Qwen3.5-4B cuts the memory state cache 1313x and prefill latency 6.9x at 1M context, throughput up 3.5x (authors' figures; the abstract was cut before the accuracy comparison). HF Daily Papers 30 upvotes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973
- SAE-Audit: frontier LLMs audited by sparse-autoencoder evidence — A frozen SAE on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct becomes a vocabulary of clinical concepts; probes fitted on past trials pick the concepts tied to trial completion or early termination, written as evidence to a black-box LLM that otherwise predicts only modestly above chance. A practical use of SAEs outside interpretability research; the abstract gives no final accuracy here, so treat the gain as unverified. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29368
- Social bias neurons in multilingual LMs — Across three instruction-tuned models, four languages and seven bias dimensions, integrated gap gradients isolate bias neurons (mostly in the deepest layers) better than activation-probability entropy, and suppressing them reduces bias more sharply; neuron patterns differ between high- and low-resource languages, and gender, nationality and social-class neurons overlap most with other dimensions. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32685
- Sparse-to-sparse (S2S) feature learning for cross-layer analysis and steering — Trains each layer's sparse features to predict the next layer's, with stronger adjacent-layer correspondence than matched baselines across eight language models; steering results not given in the excerpt read. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33640
- Chain-of-thought vector projection — A task-level CoT vector learned in a frozen teacher is mapped by a linear projector into a smaller student's residual space, trained only on final-answer loss; GSM8K and MATH gains of 10.37 to 21.98 points over base students, and 3.23 to 4.67 pass@1 on code benchmarks with a Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct student. Student layer and scale chosen on a labelled support set. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34183
- Beyond accuracy: hypothesis-driven experiment planning in LLM agents — Benchmark in kinetic mechanism identification that scores exploration patterns and four reasoning steps (belief update, hypothesis discrimination, discriminative experiment design, predictive reasoning) separately on traces; ten agents tested. Single domain. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29467
- PRIOR: pre-call reasoning and tool-use retention under RL — SFT data with less reasoning before each tool call leads to tool use collapsing under RL, while matched-token data with more pre-call reasoning keeps it; all three data variants reach similar RL training accuracy. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33648
- Evaluating latent knowledge stability in multimodal LLMs — Margin retention score and support-structure stability measure whether viewpoint and question rephrasing change the logit margin of the initial answer and its internal support. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32566
- Attention process reward model (APRM) — A 46M-parameter reward model reads hidden states and attention maps (rewarding how the model thinks rather than what it says), trained with ranking and counterfactual objectives to guide RL. Small reward model; headline gains not in the excerpt. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30627
- SecondChance: benchmark for error recovery in agents — Natural errors from agents' own attempts in tau2-bench, BFCL and AppWorld become checkpoints; agents resume under four conditions (no help, error notice, error location, repair guidance) and are scored on correction, policy compliance, state preservation and repair durability. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31586
- Grokking dynamics (theory) — Bounds linking training dissipation and evaluation transfer to population risk in the signal-channel theory of grokking. Theory only, no experiments in the abstract. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33825
- A computational model of continuous recognition memory — First computational model of human continuous recognition memory (image encoding, memory decay, decision), predicting trial-by-trial human responses within across-participant variability. Human-cognition comparison; not an LLM paper. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33947
- From memory access to evidence sufficiency (BiDE-Mem) — Agent long-term memory retrieval returns relevant but insufficient records; typed-demand guidance gives +6.23 pp over Mnemis on LoCoMo and 91.80% on LongMemEval. Authors' own diagnosis framework. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32088
- Datalog REPL for LLM agents — A dynamic Datalog engine session lets agents reuse relations in 179 of 180 cases and write 23 to 82% fewer output tokens than SQL or Python agents; a seven-question chain over 13 million facts takes Claude Haiku 5.5 a median of 31 s with the REPL against 48 and 56 s. One agent (Opus 5.5) matches the REPL by storing SQL tables. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29709
- Fresh keys, stale secrets: numeric leakage across linked prompts — Disguising confidential prices works prompt by prompt, but after 16 linked queries with fresh secrets under order-preserving substitution margin recovery by an honest-but-curious provider rises from 25.0% to 59.4% (synthetic firms). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32864

Opinion, not a test of the proofs. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31286
