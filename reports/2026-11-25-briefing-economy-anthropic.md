## 1. Jobs, skills and professions

- GitHub Copilot adoption cut quarterly technical hiring by 29% at the firms studied, concentrated in junior coding-heavy roles — A preprint (arXiv, first version 23 November) studies Copilot adoption at 5,414 multinational firms that account for 38% of the US programming workforce. It uses firms' predetermined software procurement cycles as a natural experiment. After one year, adoption reduces quarterly technical hiring by 29% among "review-induced" adopters, with no effect on separations, total technical headcount or vacancy duration. The slowdown sits in the junior roles with the highest share of coding work, and demand shifts to experienced workers and roles that pair some coding with design duties. The 29% applies to one subgroup of adopters, the paper is a preprint without peer review, and it measures hiring, not layoffs. Who is affected: junior software engineers at large multinationals · Evidence: study (natural experiment on firm-level data, preprint) · Tension: Copilot adoption lowers junior hiring by 29% while Indeed shows US software postings up 23.4% on the year (different measures, see next item). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31083

- Indeed Hiring Lab, November snapshot: postings recover overall, software development is up 23.4% on the year but still below its pre-pandemic level — The Indeed Job Postings Index reached 106.2 on 13 November (highest since April 2025, up 5.1% on the year). Software development postings are up 23.4% on the year, but the sector index stands at 82 against a February 2020 baseline of 100, while engineering sits at 149 and healthcare at 134. The share of postings that mention AI or generative AI is 7.8%, against a previous peak of 3.3% in 2022. Unemployment is 4.1% (October), posted wages are up 2.6% on the year against 3.7% CPI. The shares are keyword matches in Indeed's own postings data, not a measure of AI's effect on jobs. Who is affected: software developers and job seekers in data and analytics, media · Evidence: statistic (Indeed postings index, published method) · Tension: software postings up 23.4% on the year yet still 18% below February 2020. Source: https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/11/24/us-labor-market-snapshot-november-2026/

- Developers who learned to code before generative AI report the biggest change in how work feels — A preprint (first version 20 November) surveys engineers on how AI-assisted development differs from traditional development. Engineers report less talking with peers about technical problems, less decomposition, less documentation search and less debugger use, plus more task switching, less deep focus and less sense of control over code and design. Core code-production activities show only small mean differences, and the effects grow with years of experience. The data are self-reported perceptions; the abstract gives no sample size, so check the paper before quoting a figure. Who is affected: experienced software engineers and the training of new ones · Evidence: study (survey, preprint) · Tension: small differences in code-production tasks against larger changes in craft and control. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27947

- 1,118 developers at Microsoft, GitHub and Pfizer: 35 guidelines for where to use AI and where to resist it — A two-phase study (first version 21 November). Phase 1 surveyed 860 Microsoft developers; Phase 2, a year later, validated the 35 synthesised guidelines with 258 developers. Of those, 92% found them clearly worded, 91% relevant, 89% would adopt them. Developers describe delegating verifiable work while keeping judgment and accountability. Agreement rates measure endorsement, not outcomes, and all three employers are heavy AI adopters. Who is affected: developers on teams rolling out AI · Evidence: study (survey, preprint) · Tension: none. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28996

- Agentification model: replacing local work with foreign AI agents can lower domestic income — A theory paper (23 November) models a small open economy where exporters and local services use agents bought from abroad. Using Brazilian accounts as a reference, a supplier bargaining share above about 0.15 is enough for further agent adoption in local services to reduce value added even at negligible foreign cost (about 0.66 for tradables). Exporters can offset the loss by earning the foreign exchange to pay for agents. It is a calibrated model, not an observed result. Who is affected: services workers and exporters in middle-income economies · Evidence: study (theoretical model with Brazil and Mexico accounts, preprint) · Tension: output per paid hour rises while domestic income can fall. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31518

## 2. How teams work with agents

- iFood runs a coding agent as an API across 15,000 repositories at about 4 US cents a run — OpenAI's customer story describes iFood's Remote Agents Platform: a REST call launches a task in an isolated sandbox with deterministic steps (clone, branch, publish merge request) around an agentic step running GPT-5.6 Luna, and results are checked with Structured Outputs. The first user is an internal security product that scans for vulnerable dependencies and attempts fixes; the engineer quoted says the accuracy of fixes "more than doubled" after migration (the page summary says the number of vulnerabilities fixed more than doubled; no baseline given). The cost figure is the engineer's average per run. This is OpenAI marketing built on the customer's own account. Who is affected: platform and security engineers at large engineering organisations · Evidence: company account (vendor-published case study) · Tension: none. Source: https://openai.com/business/customer-stories/ifood

- Richpanel says it met a 50% support-automation guarantee in about 300 deployments in six months — From an October interview published by OpenAI: Richpanel sells AI customer support with a money-back guarantee of 50% automation in 30 days, says it met that in all roughly 300 deployments, and claims its agents beat human agents on customer satisfaction. It uses an "AI manager" (Boss AI) to write the procedures and tools its support agents need. Both figures are the CEO's claims, with no published method, in a vendor-published story. The quote worth keeping: "The intelligence is no longer the bottleneck. It's everything around it." Who is affected: support teams and outsourced contact centres · Evidence: company account (vendor-published) · Tension: none. Source: https://openai.com/business/customer-stories/richpanel-ai-customer-support

- sudo L7 benchmark: staff-level engineering judgment is where agents still fail — A preprint (20 November) builds 60 tasks from private production codebases, each based on a place where a frontier model failed, with 1,287 expert rubric criteria. An attempt passes only if it meets every high-severity criterion, and more than half of these test something other than functional correctness. Claude Fable 5.2 passes 52.2% of attempts, Claude Sonnet 5.5 50.6%, Claude Opus 5.5 46.1%; GPT-6.1 Sol, Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra pass 31 to 34%, and the other 13 models at most 18.9% (19 models, three attempts per task). Grading is by an agentic grader that agreed with engineers on 85 to 86% of criterion verdicts. Authors' own benchmark and task selection. Who is affected: engineering managers deciding how much review agents need · Evidence: study (benchmark, preprint) · Tension: patches that pass tests against truthful reporting and judgment on underspecified requests. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30517
