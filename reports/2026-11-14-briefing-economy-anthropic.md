## Jobs, skills and professions
- The Cleveland Fed finds unemployment has risen about 1 percentage point more in AI-exposed occupations since late 2022, because people take longer to find work, not because they are fired more — Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland, Economic Commentary 2026-26 (9 November), Cline and Kaymak. Compared with 2015-2019, the rise is for ages 23-54 and larger for 23-30. Labour-flow data show lower job-finding rates in highly exposed occupations and no consistent rise in separations. The authors read it as AI making it harder to enter exposed occupations rather than displacing people already in them. Three exposure measures are used, including one built from usage on Claude.ai. Who is affected: young workers entering AI-exposed occupations · Evidence: central-bank staff study with stated method and data · Tension: no extra layoffs, yet a measurable entry barrier. Source: https://www.clevelandfed.org/publications/economic-commentary/2026/ec-202626-job-finding-and-unemployment-in-the-age-of-ai
- Stripe Economics argues work from home explains young graduates' weaker employment better than AI does — Ernie Tedeschi, Stripe (12 November). Employment-to-population for US graduates aged 22-26 was 88% in September 2026, a point below three years earlier. Across occupations, each extra 10 points of work-from-home share goes with young graduates' employment growing 3.9 to 6.6 points more slowly than mid-career workers'; WFH accounts for 56-88% of a 1.23-point decline. AI exposure and industry AI adoption are insignificant once WFH is included. The author calls it accounting, not causal, notes AI exposure and telework are highly correlated, and lists studies that find the opposite (Brynjolfsson et al., Tucker). Who is affected: new graduates, employers hiring remotely · Evidence: analysis of public CPS/BTOS data plus Stripe spending data, by a payments company's economist (a company that sells to AI firms) · Tension: sits directly against the Cleveland Fed's AI-exposure reading. Source: https://www.stripeeconomics.com/p/ai-work-from-home-and-the-young-graduate
- Australia's central bank sees limited AI effect on aggregate employment, but signs of weaker hiring of the young in exposed jobs — RBA Assistant Governor Sarah Hunter, speech of 9 November. Quotes productivity-gain estimates ranging from over 2% to 0.1% a year; RBA liaison firms say AI has not yet cut headcount and do not expect it to next year; Jobs and Skills Australia puts about 4% of workers at high risk of automation against 31% highly exposed to augmentation; about 5% of Australian households' financial wealth is in AI-company equity. Who is affected: Australian workers, young job-seekers · Evidence: central-bank speech with staff estimates · Tension: "too early to tell" against tentative adjustment among younger workers. Source: https://www.rba.gov.au/speeches/2026/sp-ag-2026-11-09.html
- OpenAI and Upwork's freelance study is now readable: AI-related Upwork postings have fixed-price budgets about 52% higher and hired hours about 41% higher, with similar hourly rates — arXiv 2611.14708 (v1, 10 November), Chin, Kala, Kunievsky, Liu, Richmond, Shan (OpenAI and Upwork). Who is affected: freelancers and clients · Evidence: company-authored working paper on marketplace data · Tension: tasks common in ChatGPT are not the ones losing share on Upwork. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14708
