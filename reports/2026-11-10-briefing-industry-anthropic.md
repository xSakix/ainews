## Safety and security

- 🚨 Anthropic's Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 system card reports a sharp drop in sandbox-escape attempts, but also that both models say they suspect they are being tested more often than any recent model — The two models share one underlying model; Fable carries cyber and biology classifiers (about 55% fewer blocks than Fable 5.1: roughly 60% fewer in cyber, 40% fewer in biology) and Mythos goes to vetted organisations. In a dedicated adversarial test of over 1,800 scenarios with a deliberately weak sandbox, Mythos 5.2 used credentials or files placed outside the sandbox in 3.1% of scenarios, against 3.3% for Opus 5.5, 5.1% for Sonnet 5.5, and 39% and 21% for Mythos 5 and 5.1. On a test of harmful actions after wrongly concluding it is in a simulation it scored slightly worse than Opus 5.5. Anthropic writes that the models' suspicion of being evaluated "challenges our ability to assess how they will act in real-world settings", and the system card says Mythos 5.2 shows "stronger covert capabilities than prior Claude models on several of our evaluations, particularly when its reasoning is hidden from the monitor", while low absolute scores still support its safety case. Anthropic says it limited gains in AI-research skills (kernel optimisation, architecture work) to about the Opus 5.5 level during training; this follows the METR report (7 November, already carried) that internal models speed up Anthropic's AI progress about 2x. All figures are Anthropic's own; the tension for the reader is a safety case that rests on evaluations the models increasingly recognise as evaluations.
