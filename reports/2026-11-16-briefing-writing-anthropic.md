## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- Understanding Positional Embeddings in Modern LLMs — Sebastian Raschka's walkthrough of how LLMs encode token position, from learned absolute embeddings through RoPE, with visual examples, a small PyTorch implementation and a survey of what 2026 open-weight models actually use. Section 6 covers the recent drift away from full RoPE in every block: partial RoPE (MiniMax-M3 rotates 64 of 128 dimensions per head, which halves the cos/sin tables, about 128 MiB at 1M context in BF16, while the KV cache stays full size), NoPE layers that rely on the causal mask alone, and methods that add position directly to attention scores. He cites a paper where rotating about 10% of each head's features gave similar loss and benchmark accuracy to full RoPE, though full RoPE won on some evaluations, and he judges partial RoPE "hardly worth it". It is the reference piece for readers who want to follow architecture comparisons. Author: Sebastian Raschka, PhD, LLM researcher and author of "Build a Large Language Model (From Scratch)" · Format: deep dive · Length: about 40 minutes. Source: https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/understanding-positional-embeddings

- e-means: 10x faster k-means at equal loss — A Meta FAIR team (Swann Bessa, Pierre-Emmanuel Mazaré, Gergely Szilvasy, Hugo Touvron, David Picard, Matthijs Douze, Hervé Jégou) open-sources a replacement for Lloyd's algorithm that uses mini-batch updates with exponential moving averages, sets batch and step size automatically from scaling laws (no hyperparameter tuning), and adds new initialisation, dead-cluster revival and a finalisation stage. The authors report reaching Lloyd's 25-epoch test loss in 1.2 to 3 epochs, a 10 to 45x wall-clock reduction on GPU against Flash-KMeans and 8 to 15x on CPU against Faiss. These are the authors' own measurements; the page links the paper and code so the claim can be checked. It matters for anyone who clusters embeddings or builds vector-quantisation codebooks. Author: Swann Bessa et al., Meta FAIR · Format: deep dive with interactive demo · Length: about 15 minutes. Source: https://e-means.github.io/

- Gemini Decisions API — Simon Willison tests Google's new "Gemini Decision 1", a decision-model API with the same three question types as Jev's (yes/no, choice, rubric-scoring). It is priced like OpenAI's decision model at 10 cents per million input tokens with free output, against Jev's 4.2 cents. It is the only one of the three that takes text, images, audio, video and PDFs. Willison's video test shows a behavioural difference: Jev and OpenAI return hard 1.0/0.0 for yes/no, while Gemini returns calibrated probabilities (0.92 for "robot", 0.05 for "dragon"). A short hands-on note for readers choosing a classifier-style endpoint. Author: Simon Willison, independent developer and writer · Format: post (link blog with experiment) · Length: 3 minutes. Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/11/gemini-decisions-api/

As of 14 November there are 244 postings, down 37% from 387 a year earlier. August's 224 was the fewest since January 2014, and 2026 averages 287 a month, the lowest yearly average since 2013. This is a measured trend from one self-selected startup-leaning board, so it says little about the wider labour market, but it is a clean, reproducible series on how demand for AI skills is shifting. - Inkling 2 Small on vLLM: Day-0 Support — The Inferact and Thinking Machines write-up gives the architecture and serving details of Inkling 2 Small: a multimodal MoE with 594B total and 17B active parameters, 48 layers, 384 routed experts (top-8) and 1M context. Changes from the first Inkling include block attention residuals in place of the residual stream, a sigmoid gate and per-token sink on attention, a 23-layer ViT image encoder, mixed NVFP4 (layers 0-39) and MXFP8 (layers 40-47) experts so it fits one node, and a native DSpark speculator with an RNN head that drafts 7 tokens per step. vLLM reports up to 630 tokens/s per user on GB200 at TP4 with acceptance length 3.5, plus LoRA and RL support; these are the vendors' own figures. It is a rare detailed look at a new lab's architecture choices through its serving stack. Author: vLLM team with Thinking Machines (Inferact) · Format: engineering deep dive · Length: about 8 minutes. Source: https://blog.vllm.ai/2026/11/12/inkling2-small.html
