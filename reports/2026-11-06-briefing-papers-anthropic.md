## Papers worth a deep dive

- LLMs Don't Know What They Know: Benchmarking Temporal Self-Knowledge for Financial Backtesting — Metacognition with a measured cost. FinDendro tests 2,226 dated US facts (1999 to 2026: macro series, month-end stock prices, Fed decisions) and asks each model both to answer and to say whether it knows or is guessing. For most frontier models self-reports understate knowledge: GPT-5 answers 76% of controlled facts correctly but claims to know only 38%, and gets 61% right among the facts it says it does not know. Knowledge claims also persist up to seven quarters past the measured boundary, and telling the model a historical date removes most claims of prior knowledge without changing accuracy. The consequence for practitioners: a vendor's stated cutoff, asking the model, or "answer as of date X" cannot certify a backtest as leak-free. Authors: Lingfei Qian, Yan Wang, Xueqing Peng et al. (institution not stated in abstract) · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: benchmark (FinDendro) introduced, link not stated in abstract · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06808

- The Intrusion Pathway: How Non-Evidential Context Degrades Language Model Performance — Explains mechanistically why irrelevant text in a prompt (the situation in RAG, tool use and long contexts) lowers accuracy even with no competing answer. The authors trace a path: MLPs enrich both the query subject and the irrelevant tokens, "parroting" heads carry prefix content to the prediction position, and a sparse set of "intrusion heads" promotes distractor content. These heads transfer across tasks and distractor types and are mostly separate from the heads that integrate relevant evidence. Patching the clean last-token residual recovers 97.6% of the distractor-induced drop (an oracle that needs a clean pass), and a deployable head mask is also tested. Authors: Dikshant Kukreja, Gautam Gupta, Avni Mittal et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 2026-11-05 · Artefacts: code and project website (GitHub dk4248/the-intrusion-pathway) · Cognitive side: yes (distraction and attention in a model's internal computation). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07778

- Persona Perspectives: Adopting Viewpoints Reorganizes Representations in LLMs — Prompting a model to adopt a liberal or conservative persona changes the geometry of its latent space: projections of over 9,000 words onto 33 semantic axes, and the alignment among the axes, shift in ideology-consistent directions. Linear probes, SAE classifiers and word-representation classifiers trained without a persona then give politically slanted ratings of truth and morality when applied under a persona. The point for safety monitoring is that probes assume features keep a fixed representation across contexts, and this paper shows they do not. Compares neatly with human perspective-taking. Authors: not stated in abstract · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06848

- Shaping & Monitoring Models through their Internal Representations — "Mechanistic Shaping" trains a model so that it follows rules about which concepts it expresses and which it relies on (for example, not using gender when screening a CV), measured in internal representations. It works across vision, medical imaging and language, up to 32B parameters, with classification, SFT, DPO and GRPO. The key question is whether a concept trained against is gone or just hidden: the authors build a hierarchy of monitor classes and identify three ways a concept escapes a probe (drift, erasure from the layer, nonlinear encoding in higher-order statistics). In practice shaped concepts mostly stay recoverable by a refitted linear probe. A cautious, useful result for interpretability-based training. Authors: not stated in abstract · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: partly (interpretability). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07071

- Transforming Integer Grids to Spatial Intuition: Perception, Not Planning, Governs ARC-AGI-3 — An independent-researcher entry to the ARC Prize 2026 paper track. Frontier models reading raw integer grids lose spatial topology and waste actions rediscovering boundaries and symmetries. SARBLOH adds a zero-parameter perception engine (35 ms per frame: object inventory, temporal tracking, geometric briefing) and a code-as-action runtime. Across eight presentations the rubric score rises from 3/28 to 25/28 with 89.4% fewer tokens; geometric invariants generalise (100% on 23 held-out games) while single-frame affordance guesses do not (6 to 50%), so active probing is still needed. Hidden Kaggle score 16.53 on one GPU in nine hours. The claim that perception, not planning, is the bottleneck is testable and the code is public. Authors: Harsidak Singh Banwait (independent) · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: code (github.com/Harsidak/SARBLOH), open-weight agent · Cognitive side: yes (perception versus planning in fluid intelligence). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06793

- What Does Multi-Turn Reinforcement Learning Really Improve in LLM Agents? — Evaluating agents across sampling breadth K and interaction depth T (Pass@(K,T)), the authors find that RL gains depend on having enough turns and shrink when budgets tighten, and that RL does not raise single-step decision quality uniformly: its strength is error recovery and long-horizon resilience. They propose BudgetMix RL, which varies the interaction budget during training, for robustness across settings. Useful for anyone deciding what agentic RL actually buys. Authors: Kejian Zhu, Zhuoran Jin, Shangqing Tu et al. (Hugging Face lists the Chinese Academy of Sciences Institute of Automation) · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: project page (rethinking-agentic-rl.github.io), code per Hugging Face listing · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06483

- Stress Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitoring Against Covert Misalignment — TraceMRT is an automated red-team that reframes a hidden side task as a coherent sub-goal of the main task and searches for trace-shaping strategies that complete it while reading as natural execution. Black-box attacks reveal substantial weaknesses in strong monitors (named in the abstract: GPT-5.2 and Claude-4.5). The proposed Bayesian Trajectory Monitoring, combining global context with local segment evidence, reaches 0.91 AUC and 0.74 TPR at 5% FPR. Complements the CoT-faithfulness paper from earlier in the week with an adversarial number. Authors: not stated in abstract · First submitted: 2026-11-05 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07586

- When Bigger Becomes Brittle: Why Compression Hurts More as Language Models Scale — Decomposes the loss from ablating the attention heads that carry in-context retrieval into the part the rest of the network repairs and the part that reaches the output. For damage of the same size, small models restore most of the loss; large models retain most of it, since compensation offsets less as models grow. So quantisation or pruning that looks harmless on a small model can be costly on a large one, a counter-intuitive and practical point for local-model users. Authors: not stated in abstract · First submitted: 2026-11-05 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: partly (mechanistic). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07891

- Structured Machine Theory of Mind from Agent Trajectories — SMToM attributes explicit, separately supervised belief and desire representations (a Belief-Desire-Intention structure) from observed trajectories, so that interventions are direct: change the belief or the desire and watch the prediction change. In a controlled pedestrian-navigation domain with known ground truth, BDIBottleneck beats trajectory-only baselines and matches an auxiliary-supervised baseline while offering an editable mental-state interface. Substituting desires shifts predicted destinations coherently; marking a location unavailable in the belief state reduces its probability. Small, synthetic domain, but a clean take on machine theory of mind. Authors: not stated in abstract · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07208

- FrontierPhysics: Evaluating Agents for End-to-End Physics Research — 56 tasks written by 42 physicists from their own research, across nine domains; agents must review literature, plan, run research and write a manuscript, scored by deterministic verifiers plus expert rubrics. Across 22 models in 26 configurations no configuration passes more than 46 of its 168 rollouts. Manual review blames insufficiently rigorous reasoning and limited expert intuition, not missing physics knowledge or execution errors. Authors: Bingran You, Wenjun Ke, Ria Rosenauer et al. (Hugging Face lists BenchFlow) · First submitted: 2026-11-04 · Artefacts: benchmark; code or data link not stated in abstract · Cognitive side: partly (research reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06910
