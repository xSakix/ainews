## 3. China

- 🚨 Moonshot announces Kimi K3.1 Pro and K3.1 Pro Highspeed, a "3T-class" model with a 1M-token context window, live on the API and apps today with full weights promised by 4 November — K3.1 Pro is a post-trained K3 with much more reinforcement-learning compute across hundreds of domains; Highspeed is the same model at 2 to 3 times the speed. Moonshot's own numbers: Terminal-Bench 4.0 at 65.7% (GPT-6 Astra 58.2%, Claude Opus 5.5 66.4%), BenchCAD 92.7% with tools (Opus 5.5 96.2%), and on an internal stress test the claim-level hallucination rate falls from 23.4% (K3) to 8.3%. The distinctive part is the cyber section: Moonshot says vulnerability-research skill rose steadily as post-training scaled, traced it to cyber content in the mid-training corpus and in the RL environment mix, removed that data for the open-weight release, and reports that on its in-house xdev-bench-hard exploit benchmark the checkpoint without removal solves 90.5% (pass@3) against 81.0% for the final model. It states it aims not to release open weights that extend the absolute frontier of cyber capability while intending to raise what open weights offer progressively. Independent check: Artificial Analysis scores it 55 on its Intelligence Index (4th of 215), lists it at $2.00 input and $10.00 output per million tokens and 80.7 output tokens per second, and notes it used 150M tokens on the index against a median of 87M; its page currently labels the model proprietary because the weights are not out. Parameter count and licence are not stated, and the weights are a promise, not an artefact, until they appear. Artefacts: API only today (weights promised by 4 November) · Sizes or variants: K3.1 Pro, K3.1 Pro Highspeed; "3T-class", 1M context, text and image input · Released: 23 October (Hacker News submission 16:59 UTC; the blog page is undated). - Shanghai AI Laboratory releases Atria Dawn, a 753B-parameter agentic model built on the 744B GLM-5.2 mixture-of-experts, as MIT-licensed weights with a 512K context — The model card says it targets research and engineering loops (finding evidence, writing and running code, analysing results, recovering from failures) in four areas: discovery, creation, delivery of documents and spreadsheets, and cybersecurity in authorised environments. It is text-only, ships in BF16 and FP8, and runs on SGLang 0.5.13.post1 or newer and vLLM 0.23.0 or newer; the lab's hosted API is OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible, and the card gives setup recipes for Codex, Claude Code and Kimi Code. The tension is in the lab's own table, which compares it with Claude Opus 5, GPT 5.6 sol, Kimi K3, Qwen 3.8 Max, GLM 5.3 and DeepSeek V4 Pro: it leads on AutomationBench (54.7), CyberGym (86.8), SkillsBench (67.8), WideSearch (84.6), BFCL V4 (78.4), DeepSearchQA (96.5) and Harvey LAB (27.5), but trails Claude Opus 5 on SWE-bench Pro (61.7 against 76.5), Terminal-Bench 2.1 (81.0 against 90.2), NL2Repo (50.0 against 79.9) and DeepSWE (49.3 against 72.6). All figures are the lab's, with no independent run. A preview (256K context) has had a Hub page since 11 September, so this is the full release. The card cites arXiv 2609.15818, which I could not retrieve. - Tencent Hunyuan releases Hy-Nomas, an open interactive world-generation model that extends a video one segment at a time from text instructions and camera paths — It pairs a segment-autoregressive diffusion transformer (54 dual-stream blocks, hidden size 2,048, a persistent key/value cache carrying history between segments) with a Qwen2.5-VL-7B text encoder and a fine-tuned PixelUMM decoder; the distilled setting needs 8 denoising steps for 720 by 1280 video at 24 frames per second, and a separate INT8/FP8 configuration with CPU offload is described for a single RTX 4090. The repository ships a base and a distilled checkpoint, a cinematic LoRA, and a robotics LoRA trained on a real dual-arm robot dataset, and Tencent publishes Hy-Nomas-Bench (280 test cases for multi-turn control, camera navigation and spatial memory). The technical report is marked "coming soon", so quality claims rest on the demo.
