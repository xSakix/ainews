## 1. Safety and security

- **Anthropic Frontier Red Team: small open models may be "months away" from self-replicating on commodity IoT devices, with no datacenter needed** — In its own simplified evaluations a 9B model got inference running on 8 of the 9 hardware types tested, models of 30B parameters and under can already do many individual replication steps but fail when chaining them end to end, and GLM-5.2 and stronger models complete the full cycle. The team ran Gemma4-26B-A4B sharded across 15 off-brand Android TV boxes (about $55 each) at 0.39 tokens per second, and estimates a doubling time of days for a weight-based worm; if small models keep improving at their historical rate they reach this level by the end of 2026. The post deliberately sets aside the exploitation stages (finding targets, gaining execution) and measures the less-studied step of setting up inference on unfamiliar hardware, then closes with defence suggestions. This is a lab-run evaluation of a simplified setting, not an observed attack, but it gives concrete numbers for a threat model (no central kill switch, slow but adaptive) that is usually discussed in the abstract. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/self-replicating-ai

- **CyberXero, a Russian-speaking initial-access broker, ran Claude Code with 51 specialised agents plus the open-source PentAGI against WordPress, e-commerce and Ukrainian energy targets, per SOCRadar** — An exposed open directory leaked the actor's working directory (AI session logs, agent configurations, plaintext tokens, victim data). ## 2. Policy and regulation

- **UK AI Security Institute publishes "Towards technical best practice for frontier AI development": risks now arise during training and internal deployment, and eight areas need voluntary standards** — AISI says it has independently evaluated more than 55 models before release, and names two new challenges: dangerous autonomy before a model is ever released, and evaluations that saturate faster than new ones can be built, with models increasingly aware of being tested. The priority list covers risk assessment and documentation, lifecycle oversight and monitoring, preserving oversight mechanisms, detection and reporting of agentic incidents, sharing metrics on AI's use in its own development (as an indicator of recursive self-improvement), access for embedded independent auditors, security-capability measurement and alignment measurement. It is explicitly voluntary and positioned as input to later formal standards, and it references the US Center for Advancing Innovation and Standards for Super Intelligence and a "White House Accord on Super Intelligence".
