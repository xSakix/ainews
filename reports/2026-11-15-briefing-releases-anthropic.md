## 3. China

- Meituan releases LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.6, an MIT-licensed audio-driven avatar video model that runs 8 denoising steps and holds quality past 10 minutes — LongCat-Video-Avatar 1.6 appeared on Hugging Face and ModelScope on 12-13 November, with a technical report PDF in the LongCat-Video repository. It keeps the 1.5 architecture and adds two LoRA adapters (a DMD adapter and an OPSD adapter) that are mixed across the 8 denoising steps (default: first four steps DMD, last four OPSD), an optional weight-only INT8 transformer, and FP8 support, aimed at cheaper serving. Meituan's claims, all its own: less exaggerated lip movement, no error build-up in autoregressive continuation so videos longer than 10 minutes stay consistent, and faster end-to-end inference. In its own human-preference arena (33,051 valid votes, 15 pairings, six systems) 1.6 ranks second with a Bradley-Terry Elo of 1068.1, behind OmniHuman-2.0 and ahead of 1.5, HeyGen, OmniHuman-1.5 and Kling Avatar 2.0. For a reader, an open, MIT-licensed model that places second against paid avatar products in the lab's own test is what to check. - Codex CLI 0.168.0 and 0.169.0 ship on 13 November, adding multi-provider setup and a side-chat workspace — OpenAI published both versions to npm on 13 November (0.168.0 at 04:00 UTC, 0.169.0 at 08:43 UTC). 0.168.0 adds model-provider browsing and custom gateways in the app server, a side conversation pane (`features.tui_workspace`), audio-recording attachments for audio-capable models and animated-GIF generation. 0.169.0 adds multiple side-chat tabs, search across older tasks, a custom model provider flow with device-code sign-in over SSH, and a Cloud connection that sends images and archives conversations from the terminal. - NVIDIA publishes a recipe for serving the 753B-parameter GLM-5.3 across two DGX Stations, with its own throughput and latency figures — The NVIDIA Technical Blog post of 13 November (17:00 UTC) shows how to run Z.ai's GLM-5.3 NVFP4 (753B parameters, 40B active per token, a checkpoint of about 464 GB) on two GB300 DGX Stations, because one GB300 has only 252 GB of HBM3e. It uses vLLM with tensor parallelism of 2 over ConnectX-8 RDMA, multi-token prediction drafting four tokens per step, an FP8 KV cache and prefix caching, with a context ceiling of 98,304 tokens and an unauthenticated OpenAI-compatible endpoint (NVIDIA warns to firewall it). NVIDIA's own AIPerf runs on the SPEED-Bench Coding dataset peak at 607.8 output tokens per second at concurrency 16 and fall to 385.0 tokens per second at concurrency 32, where mean request latency reaches 453.5 seconds. For anyone weighing local frontier-scale inference against an API bill, that is the usable number: two GB300 machines peak at 607.8 tokens per second with 16 concurrent requests and queue badly at 32, and the figures are the vendor's, from one dataset. The model checkpoint itself (nvidia/GLM-5.3-NVFP4) has been on Hugging Face since 14 September; the new item is the serving recipe and its measurements. Artefacts: tutorial with launch scripts, benchmark figures; weights (NVIDIA Open Model Agreement and Z.ai's licence) published earlier · Sizes or variants: GLM-5.3 NVFP4, 753B total, 40B active · Released: 13 November 2026.
