## 1. United States

- **Google publishes DiarizationLM-Gemma-4-E4B-v1, a 4B Gemma 4 fine-tune that corrects speaker labels in ASR transcripts, with GGUF builds** — The model post-processes the output of a speech recogniser and a diarizer, fixing speaker-turn errors in the text. It is trained on "locality-preserving oracle" targets over four diarization corpora (Fisher, Callhome, ICSI, AMI), where the earlier 8B Llama 3 model saw only 2-speaker telephone data. The model card claims lower word diarization error rate (WDER) than the earlier 8B model on Fisher (2.99% against 3.28%) and Callhome (4.92% against 6.66%), and better than the baseline on all four corpora. Those figures are the authors' own. The card states it is "not an officially supported Google product"; it sits in the `google` Hugging Face organisation and the code lives in the `google/speaker-id` repository. It is a small, practical release for people running local transcription pipelines rather than a frontier launch.

## 3. China

- **bilibili's Index-Translate family gets a free public API, official quantised builds and four benchmarks; the 35B-A3B preview weights appeared on 2 October** — Index-Translate is an Apache 2.0 family of translation models built on Qwen3.5 (2B, 9B and a 35B-total, 3B-active MoE preview) covering 150 languages, with instruction-following constraints such as glossary enforcement, JSON/markdown preservation and style. The family also has Index-Echo (speech to subtitles or speech, in the speaker's voice), Index-Homura (translation to a target syllable count, for dubbing) and Index-NativeLong (whole documents). The technical report (arXiv 2609.40181, v1 dated 30 September) is the lab's own account; it says the 35B-A3B preview scores 0.8794 FLORES COMET-22 and 76.76 on the WMT26 judge, and that the 9B scores 75.35. Those comparisons use GPT-5.6-Sol as judge and are bilibili's own claims. Development inside the window (from the project's GitHub news log): 4 October, a free OpenAI-compatible public API for the 35B-A3B at index-translate.bilibili.com and four released benchmarks (instTrans, MEME, SandGlass, NativeLong); 3 October, official GGUF, FP8 and NVFP4 builds for every model. For a reader running local models, the GGUF builds make the family usable in llama.cpp.
  Date caveat: the family itself was announced on 30 September (README news log and arXiv v1), and the 2B and 9B repositories were created on Hugging Face on 28 September, both outside the 72-hour window.
