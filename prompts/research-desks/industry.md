# Desk: Safety, policy and industry

Desk name for the header: `Safety, policy and industry`
Output file: `reports/YYYY-MM-DD-briefing-industry-<suffix>.md`, where `<suffix>` is your system's suffix (see `prompts/research-run.md`)

## Scope

The events that keep the reader in the loop without being the core of the site (tiers 2 and 3 in `prompts/editorial-profile.md`):

- safety and security incidents, vulnerabilities and red-team results, with their technical detail;
- policy, regulation and court rulings that change what can be built, released or used — in the United States, the European Union, China and elsewhere;
- hardware and infrastructure, when there is technical detail (specifications, benchmarks, availability), not only orders and revenue;
- business, briefly: funding, acquisitions, IPOs, deals, earnings. Keep these to one line each, at most five, and only when they involve a lab, model or tool the site covers.

Flag any business item that meets the profile's exception — an open model relicensed or withdrawn, an open-source project shut down after an acquisition, a lab closing, an API discontinued or repriced — with `Exception:` and the consequence for users.

## Window

24 hours.

## Where to look

- Regulators' and governments' own pages (European Commission and AI Office, US federal agencies and state attorneys general, the Cyberspace Administration of China, the UK AI Security Institute), courts' dockets.
- Security advisories and incident reports: the affected vendor's own disclosure, CVE entries, security researchers' write-ups.
- Lab safety and policy posts.
- Tier-1 outlets for events with no primary page: Reuters, Financial Times, Bloomberg, The Information, TechCrunch, The Register, Ars Technica, 404 Media, Wired.

## Sections

1. Safety and security
2. Policy and regulation
3. Hardware and infrastructure
4. Business, briefly — one line each, at most five
