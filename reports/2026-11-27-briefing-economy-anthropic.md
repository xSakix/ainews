## Jobs, skills and professions

- Will AI Replace Juniors? Task complexity cuts managers' willingness to swap in AI — A pre-registered survey experiment with managers from 1,435 German firms (information economy and manufacturing) randomly gave them a standardised-task or a complex-task capacity need. Complex tasks lowered willingness to use AI instead of hiring an entry-level employee by 0.54 points on a five-point scale and raised the importance of human oversight by 0.15 points; managers also expected AI to cut hiring of entry-level workers more than of experienced ones. It measures stated intentions in a vignette, not actual hiring, and the preprint (v1, 25 Nov 2026) is not peer reviewed. Who is affected: entry-level hires in German knowledge work and manufacturing · Evidence: survey experiment, preprint · Tension: managers expect AI to cut junior hiring most, yet judgement-heavy tasks lowered their willingness to use AI in place of a hire. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34099

- AI, Automation, and Fiscal Pressure — A calibrated two-sector general-equilibrium model (Bastani, Skans and co-authors; v1 25 Nov 2026) shows how automation shifts income from labour to more lightly taxed capital in countries that fund health care and education from taxes. In the EU-27 benchmark, where automation is faster in the market sector, the deficit rises by nearly one percentage point of value added while the economy grows by 12% from automation. It is a model result under stated assumptions (public services follow consumer demand, tax rates unchanged), not an observed outcome. Who is affected: EU public budgets and tax-funded services · Evidence: calibrated model, preprint · Tension: output grows 12% yet the deficit widens by about 1 percentage point of value added. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34234

- How AI is reconfiguring work in India — A task-based framework (v1 25 Nov 2026) sorts Indian jobs into two regimes: jobs where AI automates most of the skill bundle and the human mostly validates, and jobs where the human drives and AI is a tool. It draws on cases from business process outsourcing, manufacturing, MSMEs, sanitation, finance, law and software engineering, and concludes that every job in its sample was reimagined rather than eliminated. The abstract gives no headcount or wage figures, so this is a taxonomy and policy argument, not a measurement. Who is affected: Indian BPO, software and MSME workers · Evidence: conceptual framework with case evidence, preprint · Tension: none. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34065
