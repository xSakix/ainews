## 4. Rest of the world

- AI21 Labs publishes its write-up of LIFT-Bench, an open instruction-following benchmark whose constraints apply to specific document sections, plus a 34,258-task training set — Most instruction-following benchmarks, such as IFEval, check simple constraints across a whole response, and those same checks are used as RL rewards, so models learn to satisfy the checks and skip the task. LIFT-Bench gives the model a topic and a nested outline to draft. Each constraint is pinned to one section ("section 2.1 in French", not "some French somewhere") and is either checked in code or judged by an LLM. A response that meets every format check but skips the content still fails every judged constraint. The accompanying paper (arXiv, first version 20 October; Goldstein, Belfer, Belinkov, Gissin) reports that frontier models struggle on the denser subsets, and that training on LIFT-Train was much less prone to reward hacking than training on the common IFTrain. These results are the lab's own. For practitioners, the release is a ready-made RL environment rather than a model: tasks ship in the format Prime Intellect's `verifiers` reads natively, together with the synthesis pipeline, so new domains and constraint densities can be generated. One caveat from the repository: the judge is fixed to Gemma-4-31B-it, and scores are comparable only between runs that use the same judge. The blog post is dated 25 October on AI21's blog index, but the post itself returned a Cloudflare block to this desk. This summary therefore comes from the dataset card, the repository README and the paper abstract. The dataset (15 October) and the paper (20 October) predate the window, and neither has appeared in an earlier briefing.
