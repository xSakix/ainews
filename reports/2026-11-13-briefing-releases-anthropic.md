- 🚨 Google releases Gemini 4 Hydrogen, a fast "everyday" tier of Gemini 4, to the Gemini API (free tier included), the Gemini app, Search worldwide and Antigravity at once — Google says Hydrogen scores 51 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index at its default "high" thinking level (above its larger Gemini 4 Argon at 50) and 54 at a new "xhigh" level. It is priced flat at $1.50 per million input and $7.50 per million output tokens across the whole 1M-token window, with up to 1M output tokens, and replaces Gemini 3.8 Flash in the upgrade path (Google reports Terminal-Bench 4.1 at 57% against 18.2% for 3.8 Flash, fabricated claims down more than 5x, and 2.5x faster completion at the default level). The safety section is unusually specific: 99.9% chain-of-thought legibility across more than 200,000 audited turns, 96.7% on an internal agent-honesty evaluation, and 2.3x more resistance on the Gray Swan indirect-prompt-injection benchmark, all Google's own measurements. For the reader it is the clearest price and speed move of the week: a model Google rates above its own Argon, at a price that makes it the default worker model in agent setups. - 🚨 Thinking Machines releases the Inkling 2 Small weights under Apache 2.0, one week after announcing the model closed — The 5 November launch said the weights would follow "next week" after external safety testing; on 12 November the lab added an update to its post and the Hugging Face repository went public. Inkling 2 Small is a 594B-parameter mixture-of-experts model with 17B active (48 layers, 8 of 384 experts per token, hybrid local and global attention, image input, 1M-token context), trained natively in 4-bit NVFP4. The card says it needs at least 500 GB of aggregate GPU memory (two B300s or four H200s) for the NVFP4 checkpoint, 800 GB for the optional MXFP8 one, and lists day-one recipes for SGLang, vLLM, Unsloth and TokenSpeed and hosted access at NVIDIA, Fireworks, Together, Baseten, Nebius, Modal and Cloudflare. It is the staged-access story the profile asks about playing out to its end: closed first, red-teamed, then open, in seven days. The lab's own table compares it with GLM-5.3-Flash, MiMo V2.6 Flash, MiniMax-M3.1-Flash and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017 and reports an Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index of 31. What is new today is the weights; the model itself was reported on 6 November. - Salesforce's Sovereign Intelligence group releases SOV-Transcribe-1, an Apache 2.0 speech-recognition model that it says is the best open-weight transcriber on the Artificial Analysis leaderboard — The model attaches a Whisper large-v3 audio encoder to Gemma 4 26B-A4B (26.6B parameters, about 4B active) and takes plain-language instructions about transcript style, plus a context list for names and jargon, for English, French, German, Spanish, Italian, Portuguese and Dutch, up to 30 minutes of audio. Salesforce reports 2.28% word error rate on the Artificial Analysis test (Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe 2.81%, Voxtral Small 2.77%), 25.69% on the hard HALAS earnings-call set and a 76.3% pass rate on BeSimple's VoiceCodeBench, where a recording passes only if every phone number, email and code symbol is right. The entity-level test is the useful part for anyone feeding transcripts to a voice agent: one dropped underscore breaks a tool call. A DFlash speculative-decoding draft model ships in a separate repository; vLLM support is currently a plugin pinned to vLLM 0.32.0. Artefacts: weights (Apache 2.0) / model card / launch blog / draft model · Sizes or variants: SOV-Transcribe-1 (26.6B total) and SOV-Transcribe-1-DFlash · Released: 12 November 2026 (repository created 10 November). - IBM releases Granite 4.1 3B and 8B "LLMON" previews, models trained to read prompts as typed, addressable blocks instead of a flat string, and reports near-zero success for indirect prompt injection on some benchmarks — LLMON ("lemon") marks instructions, data, requirements and outputs with six reserved single-token delimiters and two-token block IDs, and a final "exec" block states which instruction runs on which data. Because instructions and data are separated in the token stream, injected text in a retrieved document is mostly ignored; IBM reports that on the OPI benchmark the preview avoids 47% more attacks than the best frontier model it tested and 95% more than the best open model, with general capability on par with Granite 4.1 Instruct (static attacks only; multi-turn adaptive attacks are not yet covered, as the post says). The models post-train from the Granite 4.1 base checkpoints, ship their own chat template (you name message parts and mark each exec True or False), and come with a JSON-to-LLMON converter library and native support in IBM's Mellea framework. It is a concrete, testable defence against the attack class that makes agents risky to point at the open web. Artefacts: weights (Apache 2.0) / blog / code (toolkit and converters) · Sizes or variants: granite-4.1-3b-LLMON-preview, granite-4.1-8b-LLMON-preview (8.8B parameters), experimental previews · Released: 12 November 2026. Source (the model page; IBM's Hugging Face blog post of 12 November is the announcement): https://huggingface.co/ibm-granite/granite-4.1-8b-llmon-preview

- Meta makes MetaEncoder v1 public on Hugging Face, a 28B multimodal embedding model whose README adds a "decide" call that answers yes/no, choice and score questions with probabilities — The repository facebook/meta-encoder-v1 (a gated "meta-encoder" appeared on 30 September; this is the public v1) holds 28.4B parameters in bf16 in 11 shards, needs one 80 GB GPU and transformers 5.15 or later, and supports encode, retrieve and decide over text, images, video frames and document images with 6,656-dimension vectors. Probabilities are a softmax of cosine scores at temperature 0.02, and the card shows a calibrate() call to fit the temperature to labelled data. There is no announcement page, technical report or licence field, so what is checkable is the card and the code; the benchmark image is Meta's own. It joins Google's Gemini Decision (11 November) and the Bielik-One family below in a pattern worth watching: labs shipping models that return calibrated choices rather than text. ## Europe

- Translated, the Rome-based translation company behind Lara, publishes Lara-4-Mini-Base, a 7.2B mixture-of-experts base model with 0.6B active parameters, trained from scratch on 6T tokens in 45 languages — About a third of the data (2.18T tokens) is in 44 languages besides English, including all 24 official EU languages. The layers are a hybrid: 14 Gated DeltaNet, 13 sliding-window attention (window 512) and only 7 global attention layers, with 128 routed experts (top 4) plus one shared in each layer; context is 4,096 tokens through the first three stages and 128k (131,072) after a 100B-token long-context stage. Translated reports an average of 57.0 over English, multilingual, maths and code benchmarks, the highest of 36 open base models with up to 2B active parameters (granite-4.0-h-tiny 53.9, Qwen3-1.7B 53.8, both trained on several times more tokens), and about 1,100 characters per second for one user on an A100. It is a base model with no instruction tuning or safety tuning, under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (non-commercial) and gated behind a manual access request, so "open" here means research use only. The benchmark list and comparison set are the company's own. Artefacts: weights (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, gated) / model card / custom code · Sizes or variants: Lara-4-Mini-Base, 7.28B parameters in BF16 · Released: 11 November 2026 (repository created 14:38 UTC; surfaced late). ## Rest of the world

- Higurashi, a Japanese lab, releases Haru-4B, an Apache 2.0 full-duplex speech-to-speech model that listens and talks at the same time, with about 65 ms end-to-end latency on a MacBook — The base model is built on Qwen3.5-4B (32 layers, hidden size 2,560) and runs at 7.5 Hz, taking one 133 ms audio frame and producing one frame of audio and text per step. It generates continuous 64-dimensional audio latents with a small MLP head in one MeanFlow sampling step, so there are no discrete audio tokens and no second audio transformer. The lab says it was pretrained on 40 million hours of real conversation (not synthetic turn-based dialogue) covering more than 160 languages, with nine main ones (English, Spanish, Portuguese, German, French, Chinese, Japanese, Russian, Korean) and sessions up to 60 minutes. It is a pretrained research base, not a tuned assistant; code covers PyTorch with a web demo (about 12 GB of GPU memory), an MLX build with a macOS app, and a vLLM-Omni plugin. The latency and training figures are the lab's own. Artefacts: weights (Apache 2.0) / model card / technical blog (English and Japanese) / code · Sizes or variants: Haru-4B base, 5.27B parameters in the safetensors metadata · Released: 12 to 13 November 2026 (repository created 12 November 11:05 UTC; blog dated 13 November JST). - Edge0 releases Edge0-145B and Edge0-120B, mixture-of-experts models it says run on a single laptop or consumer GPU, and claims coding scores ahead of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 — The model is derived from Qwen3.8-Flash-Next ("latent" mixture of experts: 145B total, 10.12B active, 512 experts per layer with 10 routed plus one shared, 48 layers, 256K context, trained natively at 4 bits, with a 1.26B multi-token-prediction draft head). Edge0 reports 64.6 on DeepSWE 1.1 and 86.52 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 58.7 and 83.3), 90.4 on GPQA-Diamond (Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 91.7), 29 GB peak memory and 80+ tokens/s decode on a laptop, and on the model card 98.3 tokens/s agent decode on an RTX 5090 D 24 GB using 23.25 GiB of VRAM plus 38.27 GiB of host RAM, so "one consumer GPU" depends on a large system-RAM pool. The blog calls it "open-source", but the licence is the custom Edge0 Community Model licence 1.0, and Hacker News readers pointed out it is open weights; nothing here is independently reproduced yet, and commenters on that thread are sceptical that a 145B model matches the scores. ## Tools, APIs and platform updates

- Inception Labs is shutting down the Mercury API, its hosted diffusion-LLM service, on 28 December 2026 — A banner on the Inception docs site and a "Mercury API wind-down" page say the API and the platform dashboard stay fully available until 28 December; after that, requests stop being served and users lose access to the platform and to any data submitted to it, so logs, invoices and prompts must be exported before then. Customers received an email on 12 November and moved first on Hacker News, where users who had just built on Mercury 3 (launched 19 October) asked whether the models will be sold, released or open-sourced; the notice gives no reason and says nothing about the model weights, so those questions are unanswered. For anyone with a latency-sensitive pipeline on diffusion LLMs, the practical result is a six-week migration deadline and one fewer provider of that model type. Artefacts: none (API discontinued) · Sizes or variants: Mercury API (Mercury 3 and earlier) · Released: announced 12 November 2026, effective 28 December 2026. Source: https://docs.inceptionlabs.ai/support/wind-down ; https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50470335

- Anthropic launches self-hosted Claude Managed Agents: the agent service, not only the sandboxes, can now run on a customer's own infrastructure with a model provider of their choice — The Claude Platform release notes for 12 November say the whole Managed Agents service (API, agent loop, and storage for agents, sessions and session history) can run in a customer's cloud account or data centre. The server, called cma-server, needs PostgreSQL 16 or later, never runs an agent's shell or file tools (those run in customer-hosted sandboxes), and sends model requests to Anthropic by default or to Amazon Bedrock, Claude models on Google Cloud (Google managed), Microsoft Foundry or the customer's own gateway. Customers install each release themselves, some hosted-only features are missing (a "differences" page lists them), and access is by request through the Anthropic account team. It matters to teams that could not use hosted agents for data-residency reasons: the agent state stays inside their own network. Artefacts: product (access on request) / documentation · Sizes or variants: server plus self-hosted sandboxes · Released: 12 November 2026. Source: https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/managed-agents/self-hosted

- Google adds Gemini 4 Hydrogen and a new "Orchestrator" model option to Antigravity, its agentic coding product, and makes Hydrogen the default model for the Antigravity agent in Gemini Managed Agents — Orchestrator switches between Gemini 4 Carbon and Hydrogen inside one session, with Carbon as coordinator and Hydrogen as the worker and verifier agents in isolated workspaces. On Terminal-Bench 4.0 Google reports that Orchestrator reached near-Carbon success rates while using on average 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster; Hydrogen is open to paid Google AI Pro and Ultra subscribers in Antigravity. This is a pricing and quota story as much as a model story: the cheaper model does the typing and the expensive one does the judging, and the quota saving is the figure to test. Antigravity says tens of thousands of /swarm runs started within days of Carbon's release earlier this week. Artefacts: product feature (paid tiers) / blog · Sizes or variants: Orchestrator (Carbon), Hydrogen as standalone · Released: 12 November 2026. Source: https://antigravity.google/blog/gemini-4-hydrogen-in-google-antigravity ; https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/build-with-gemini-4-hydrogen/

- Claude Code 2.1.323 adds Code Mode, an experimental opt-in in which Claude writes JavaScript to call its own tools, and tightens a long list of Bash permission checks — Published to npm on 12 November at 20:29 UTC. Code Mode can be switched on by the user and switched off for an organisation (the documentation page linked from the changelog was not yet live when checked). The release also lets plugins declare which Claude Code versions they support, adds type checking of a plugin's TypeScript hooks to "claude plugin validate", and fixes permission-prompt gaps: allow rules with wildcards such as Bash(ls*) skipped the prompt in some cases, an rm judged from the wrong folder skipped the dangerous-removal prompt under bypass permissions or an allow rule, deny and ask rules missed commands run through sudo and env, and the Bash sandbox could switch off during a session.
