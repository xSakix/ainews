## Papers worth a deep dive

- Language models find a stated fact by where it was mentioned, not by its name — With a list of facts in context ("Alice eats an apple. Bob eats a pear."), the hidden state for "what does Bob eat?" differs from the one for "what does Alice eat?" along an "ordinal vector" that points to the fact by order of mention. Adding that vector to a question about the first fact makes the model answer with the second fact. The addresses are low-rank, shared across contexts, present from 1.5B to 32B parameters, form early in pretraining, and make the first-mentioned fact the easiest to reach, which the author compares to human recall. This is an interpretability result about how a model binds a question to its context, and the experiment is simple enough to re-run. Authors: Yufa Zhou (single author; affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 1 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/MasterZhou1/order-of-mention) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00910

- 4MT-VLM: vision-language models keep a place only from the viewpoint they studied — The benchmark borrows a four-mountains-style test used clinically to probe hippocampal function. It uses procedurally generated landscapes in five stimulus modes that strip out appearance cues while keeping layout fixed, tested on 16 open and closed models with 4-alternative forced choice. Models recognise a place from the studied viewpoint but fall below the 25% chance level at a 135 degree camera rotation, where the abstract reports a human observer scoring 85%. Recovery comes only when distractors are moved more than 30 m apart. The paper's reading is that VLMs have rudimentary cognitive maps that are too coarse to survive a viewpoint change. Authors: Markus Frey (single author; affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 30 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: dataset introduced; release link not found on the abstract page · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39238

- The geometry of knowledge accessibility — The paper shows that whether a model can recall what a query needs is visible in the query's representation before any generation. Accessible queries sit closer to a centre in representation space and inaccessible ones farther away. The distance ordering transfers across datasets and tracks accessibility more than reasoning difficulty. This gives a pre-generation "knowledge boundary" signal, a form of self-knowledge. The reported use is routing: query rewriting helps most for accessible queries, chain-of-thought near the boundary, and retrieval beyond it. Authors: Lihu Chen (single author; affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 2 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03052

- Stress-testing LLM lie detectors: probes follow the persona, not the truth — Probes that read a model's internal state to flag dishonest output are tested on 8,916 human-reviewed on-policy responses from three LLMs playing anti-factual personas (a conspiracy theorist, for example). Many of the eight probes from prior work fail, especially when right and wrong answers share the same persona prompt. Three confounder datasets, in which truth is anti-correlated with another concept, show that the probes partly track instruction compliance or response likelihood. The paper adds a simple linear probe that does best on both tests and argues for training data in which truth is decorrelated from confounders. A careful negative result on interpretability-based honesty monitoring. Authors: von Klinski, Lapuschkin, Samek (names as listed on arXiv; affiliations not on the abstract page) · First submitted: 30 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: dataset described; release link not found on the abstract page · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39807

- When History Fails to Become Experience: agents don't link past actions to their outcomes — Adding interaction history improves language-agent task completion, but most of the gain survives shuffling the past actions, so agents are not reliably connecting what they did with what happened. A simple fix is to label each returned observation explicitly as the outcome of the preceding action, which raises task success and reduces repeated actions without adding any new information. A learned "calibrator" that re-assesses past actions and records selected experience improves further. The labelling change can be tried in any agent loop today. Authors: Jingyu Liu et al. (affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 2 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (learning from experience). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02769

- MetaCtrl: a small metacognitive controller that decides when a frozen reasoner should keep thinking — A lightweight controller watches the evolving reasoning trace and chooses to continue, simplify, skip redundant steps or conclude. It is trained with RL on a reward for correctness that favours shorter correct solutions, with no per-problem budgets and no retraining of the reasoner. On DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B it adds 4.7 accuracy points average over seven benchmarks while cutting generation length by 53.3%. The same controller transferred to an unseen reasoner, Qwen3-14B, for +2.9 points and -50.3% length, per the abstract. Authors: Zhibin Wen et al. (affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 29 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/binbin2xs/MetaCtrl) · Cognitive side: yes (metacognitive control of reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.37304

- Do LLMs Really Forget? Unlearned information is still in the hidden states — Output-level unlearning metrics (TOFU, MUSE, WMDP) can read as success while generative probe decoders trained on hidden states across layers still recover substantial sensitive information. The authors prove that a decoder can be made insensitive to sensitive directions while the representations keep the information. They propose PARS, an unlearning objective that adversarially minimises extractable information from hidden states, and report stronger erasure under adversarial probing and relearning attacks than the baselines. Relevant to anyone using open-weight unlearning claims. Authors: Reisizadeh, Ruan, Liu, Hong (names as listed on arXiv; OptimAI-Lab per the code repository) · First submitted: 29 Sep 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/OptimAI-Lab/HiddenStateUnlearning) · Cognitive side: partly (what stays encoded after "forgetting"). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36612

- RealCompanion: ten real human-AI companion relationships with 27,218 messages over up to 120 days — Real long-term conversations, released with profile, persona, chat and question test items, each label linked to supporting messages. Only 3.4% of messages depend on something said earlier, and the needed message is usually far back (median 2,157 messages in the abstract's wording). Looking at the most recent messages finds the needed one 95.9% of the time overall but 2.2% when it is far back. Detectors for "when the past matters" barely beat chance, models bring up the past 10 to 14 points more often when earlier messages are labelled "memories", and agent systems add inferred detail beyond what the person revealed (persona F1 0.71). A rare real-data benchmark for long-term memory. Authors: Arman Behnam et al. (affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 1 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: data (stated as released; link not found on the abstract page) · Cognitive side: yes (memory, user modelling). It was also among the higher-upvoted papers on Hugging Face Daily Papers for the 5 Oct listing. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01780
