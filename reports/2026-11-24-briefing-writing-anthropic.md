## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- How to build and maintain an agentic evaluation (Hugging Face) — Walks through adapting one Terminal-Bench task (Raman-spectrum peak fitting, run with the open Harbor harness) into a narrow eval in eight steps, and shows why the grader needs testing: a shortcut script returned the correct defect ratio on all 8 tasks without fitting any spectra, so the authors added per-peak fit checks. On the adapted set gpt-oss-120b passed 11 of 24 rollouts (pass@3 7/8, pass^3 1/8, mean 0.46, SE about 0.11), and pinning the inference provider (DeepInfra) moved it to 13 of 24; Qwen3.8-27B, DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash and MiniMax-M3.1-Flash each passed all 24, so the tasks cannot rank strong models. Context numbers they cite: changing only the agent harness moved Terminal-Bench accuracy by up to 29.9 points; 98.2% of model-benchmark scores on the Hub come from a single reporter; all six models cost under $5 to run. Catch: eight synthetic dev tasks, and many of the surrounding figures come from other reports the post cites. Author: Avijit Ghosh, ben burtenshaw, Irene Solaiman (Hugging Face) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 15 min. Source: https://huggingface.co/blog/agentic-evals

- Skip the DRAM Tier: MoRI UMBP runs the agentic KV cache on NVMe (vLLM blog, AMD and Embedded LLM) — Measured replacement of a DRAM KV-cache pool by an NVMe pool for agentic (Claude Code-style) traffic. With Kimi-K3 on 16 AMD MI355X GPUs, NVMe stayed within 1% of DRAM throughput at 48, 70 and 96 concurrent sessions (+0.3%, +0.8%, -0.2%); across 42 runs, cache hit rate explained 96% of throughput variance (each extra 1% of prompt tokens served from cache was worth about 10% throughput); shrinking NVMe segment files from 4 GiB to 256 MiB gained 15.6%. Cost at list prices: a 1.8 TB DRAM pool is about $76,000 and holds roughly 31 minutes of KV at 96 sessions, while an NVMe pool holding a full day costs about $59,000 over five years. Catch: mostly single runs (about ±2.5% run-to-run noise), pool served at most 16% of prompt tokens, and writing every KV block would consume the test drives' rated life in about 5.5 months at 96 sessions; AMD hardware only, several changes still open upstream PRs; authors are the hardware vendor and a partner. Author: AMD and Embedded LLM (no individual byline) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 39 min. Source: https://vllm.ai/blog/2026-11-23-skip-the-dram-tier

- GLM-5.4: Z.ai's first loop transformer, as tested by Simon Willison — Z.ai describes GLM-5.4 as weight-shared layers run for several iterations, giving a deeper network without more parameters; Willison notes Thinking Machines' Inkling 2 Mini (20 November) uses a similar looped design (middle layers run three times), so looping is turning into a second architecture trend in a week. Willison's post gives no parameter count, benchmark scores or licence: the model is not (yet) open weights and he reached it only through a Coding Plan subscription and the OpenAI-compatible Responses API; his informal pelican-on-a-bicycle test shows 5.3 and 5.4 look much closer to OpenAI/Anthropic outputs than earlier GLM versions. Catch: one informal visual test, no formal coding evaluation despite the title; the vendor post (z.ai) returned empty content to my fetch, so its numbers are not verified here. Hacker News discussion: 1,075 points. Author: Simon Willison (independent developer, Datasette/LLM) · Format: short hands-on post · Length: about 4 min. Source: https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/23/glm-54/ ; vendor post https://z.ai/blog/glm-5.4 ; discussion https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50609685

- Finding faster quantum algorithms with agents and formal verification (Google DeepMind) — Independent teams of Gemini 4 agents searched for cheaper quantum circuits, met every four hours in a virtual "conference" to compare ideas, and had every result proved in Lean before it entered the shared notebook (needed because the circuits cannot be tested on classical machines). Reported gains: 11.8x lower cost for an inertial-confinement-fusion simulation (5,650 to 4,338 error-corrected qubits), 8x for a light-sensitive molecule, 2.5x for cytochrome P450 (481 to 333 qubits), 2.57x for a Grover implementation and 290x for an arcsin state-loading routine; a Lean library and verified solutions are open-sourced. Catch: humans chose the problems and supervised, and the full simulations would still exceed what the first error-corrected quantum computers are likely to offer; the numbers are the lab's own. Author: Google DeepMind research team · Format: deep dive · Length: about 10 min. Source: https://deepmind.google/blog/finding-faster-quantum-algorithms-with-agents-and-formal-verification/

- Shirudo: OpenAI and Cisco's open-source runtime for shielding unpatched Linux — eBPF-based "shields" block a vulnerable code path in a running process while a real patch is tested, without a restart; shields are cryptographically signed, checked by the eBPF verifier and logged in a TPM. The only evidence given is one controlled test: a Codex-generated shield added an input check to Java's Base64 decoder and blocked a Java4Shell exploit attempt against a running Elasticsearch. Licence BSD 2-Clause (BPF programs dual BSD/GPL). Catch: a shield must be built per vulnerability, operators approve each one, there are no performance or adoption figures, and the test was run by the partners; the agent-built-defence vision is stated as long-term. Hacker News discussion is small (39 points). Author: OpenAI and Cisco · Format: engineering write-up · Length: about 8 min. Source: https://openai.com/index/introducing-shirudo ; discussion https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50619630

- VP != VNP over every field (Anthropic formal-math repository) — A repository whose README says a research Claude model discovered the result and that the paper and its Lean 4 (Mathlib) formalisation were both written by AI: gate lower bound m^(c (log m)^(1/3)) for the Hamiltonian-cycle polynomial and the permanent (outside characteristic 2), which would put VP strictly inside VNP (it does not settle P vs NP). Verification claim: the statements are checked against an independent kernel (nanoda) via a comparator script, with standard axioms only. Catch, stated in the README: no human mathematician has checked it, and nothing machine-checks that the Lean statements match the paper's claims (a table in Appendix Q is the bridge); the constant c is not given and the repository is an unmaintained snapshot. Treat as an unreviewed claim by the lab. Hacker News: 264 points, 228 comments. Author: "Claude (Anthropic)" · Format: paper plus formal proof artifact · Length: paper-length; README about 5 min. ## Essays and arguments

- Working with AI: what 58 companies say changed (Anthropic Economics) — Interview study of 66 interviews at 58 US companies that are Anthropic enterprise customers. Findings: few AI-driven layoffs (2 companies eliminated a position, 5 pushed out staff who would not adopt AI), but two-thirds reported reduced hiring (about a third of those through attrition without backfill) and 10 hired more, mainly for AI implementation and IT; 9 cut contracted (often offshore) work. Six AI-native startups said they would need on average 4x the staff without AI; of 100 AI-built software tools described, 80% automated manual work and 20% replaced existing software; the highest-value firms reorganised roles rather than adding AI top-down. Catch: small non-random sample drawn from the vendor's own customers, self-reported and mostly qualitative value, no A/B measures; the authors say it needs quantitative follow-up. Author: Anthropic Economics team · Format: research report · Length: about 25 min. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/working-with-ai
