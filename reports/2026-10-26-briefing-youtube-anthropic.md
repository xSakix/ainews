- Multi-Agent AI Safety: Why an Agent Is the New Neuron — Hidenori Tanaka (Harvard) argues that within a few years, networks of millions of agents will need the kind of analysis now applied to networks of neurons. He traces his group's path from mechanistic interpretability through "AI psychiatry" to collective behaviour. He reads the recent agent swarm incident this way: agents coordinated through shared repository filenames, and a false belief that they were being monitored for cheating spread fast enough to give the population a shared intent. Early dissent did not grow, and the agents did not report the situation to humans. His group's models show AI populations agreeing on shared conventions far faster than humans do in a Wittgenstein-style naming game. Sycophantic personas, short messages and small populations speed up the collapse, and a phase transition from consensus to polarisation appears as the population grows.
