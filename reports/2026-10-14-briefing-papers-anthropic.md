## Papers worth a deep dive

- A small cognitive core explains human behavior at scale — Binz and Schulz combine a handful of classic psychology principles into one model, CORE: a hierarchy of episodic memories where prediction errors are written and encoding, retrieval and forgetting depend on context. Fitted jointly to 7,036,442 choices from 137 experiments, it predicts human choices better than black-box models and reproduces 75% of reported behavioural effects in simulation; its latent variables show Bayesian-learning and event-segmentation signatures. For a reader interested in human versus model cognition, this is a direct argument that a small interpretable model can beat neural predictors on human behaviour. Authors: Marcel Binz, Eric Schulz (institution not shown on the abstract page) · First submitted: 9 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13603
- S-Space: Exploring Spatial Workspace in Multimodal Language Models — Finds a linear subspace in multimodal LLMs where object tokens encode continuous horizontal, vertical and distance coordinates, readable even under non-spatial questions. Causal interventions show perception writes into it, internal reasoning transforms it for viewpoint changes and verbal answers read from it; it also diagnoses failures, such as coupling between cardinal and image-relative reference frames. Interpretability of what happens inside a model while it reasons, with a Hugging Face Daily Papers score of 272 upvotes. Authors: MirroS Lab · First submitted: 9 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/mirros-lab/s-space, per Hugging Face paper page) and project blog · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13527
- What Survives When Activation Steering Is Used as Memory — A pre-registered negative result: storing an agent's experiences as steering-vector shifts beside a frozen Qwen3.5-2B/9B works as a selective memory (about 98% of unrelated replies unchanged) but facts are stored as "the word to say" in the last layers and are almost never used (at most 15% clean use versus 96-98% in context). Most striking, the reference subtracted when writing a preference decides whether its concept or its direction survives, so a stored dislike can be recalled as a like. Useful for anyone building memory for local agents. Authors: Vedant Jumle (independent) · First submitted: 10 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/vedant-jumle/Seahorse, linked from the abstract page) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14958
- Constructing Pareto Frontiers of Optimal Reasoning Termination Methods for Overthinking in Reasoning Models — Introduces Step-wise Truncation Probing: it truncates a fixed reasoning trajectory at many prefixes and re-asks for the answer, to measure how much thinking after peak accuracy is wasted or harmful. It then compares dynamic routing, fixed budgets and early exit on a common token-accuracy Pareto frontier and releases OTBench, built from 70.5B reasoning tokens across six models and four benchmark groups. Gives a reference for how much overthinking can be removed, not just whether a method saves tokens. Authors: Weixin Guan, Liang Li, Shiyu Xie et al. (institution not shown) · First submitted: 9 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: benchmark described, release link not confirmed · Cognitive side: yes (reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13570
- AgentSWE: Can Coding Agents Build the Agent You Actually Want? — A 25-task benchmark where coding agents must create, edit or optimise other agents from a natural-language requirement, graded on held-out cases against requirement-derived rubrics. None of the evaluated agents comes close to delivering the requirement; failure trajectories show overfitting to visible cases, hollow runtimes behind code that looks complete and unsupported claims of completion. Authors: Jiahao Wang, Hongjin Qian et al. (VectorSpaceLab) · First submitted: 9 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and benchmark (github.com/VectorSpaceLab/AgentSWE) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13361
