Microsoft did not comment; the reason for leaving is the paper's, not Microsoft's. Shows grid connection, not chips, as the binding constraint on a heavily financed AI cloud. - **SemiAnalysis: more than 300 local data-centre moratoriums have delayed only three US projects, about 2.3 GW of 21 GW in affected areas, against 38 GW expected online in 2027** — Tom's Hardware summarises a SemiAnalysis report (shared on X on 17 October, cited by The Economist). The firm tracks 400-plus local instruments in 17 states and tested each against records of thousands of projects: moratoriums usually freeze only new applications, do not revoke existing approvals, expire before later projects need permits, or miss the parcel (Michigan has 45 with essentially zero pipeline exposure; a SpaceX site in Brownsville sits on land the city removed from its limits). The firm itself says moratoriums "could" delay the build-out but "aren't yet at scale"; the analysis is one firm's, partly done with AI research agents. Nvidia reportedly asked Samsung, Micron and SK hynix to build the base die on TSMC 3nm with a sixth-generation (1c) DRAM core, so it verifies one standard design rather than each vendor's own; Nvidia is said to promise up to 30% more bandwidth than standard HBM4E at up to 15% lower power, for its Feynman-generation GPUs. Samsung is said to be keeping its own foundry as a fallback if TSMC capacity runs short. Single-source report with no company confirmation; the performance figures are Nvidia's reported claims.
