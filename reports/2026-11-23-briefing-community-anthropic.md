## Hacker News

- OpenAI's chief scientist proposes perplexity as a shared yardstick, and HN pushes back on what it measures — Jakub Pachocki's post (22 Nov) argues that cross-entropy loss, which OpenAI has tracked since GPT-3.5 on a held-out snapshot of its own internal codebase, predicts when new capabilities appear. Reported log-perplexity: GPT-3.5 about 0.99, GPT-4 about 0.91, current models below 0.8, a later model about 0.76 "began producing notable mathematical results", with 0.68-0.70 named as the next window. He proposes that labs agree on common perplexity-linked limits they would not cross until a monitoring or understanding milestone is met. The post itself concedes that labs use different datasets, that chain-of-thought and post-training gains do not show up in perplexity, and that the data's entropy floor is unknown. Discussion: commenters note the dataset is private and not comparable across labs; one reports measuring perplexity 5.7 for Inkling-2-Mini on their own small mixed text set (log about 0.756 on OpenAI's axis, which would put it between GPT 6.2 Astra and OpenAI's internal model) as evidence that perplexity says little about understanding versus memorisation (poster's own measurement, different dataset, so not comparable); a self-identified OpenAI employee replies that the correlation with quality is "not 1, but neither is it 0" and the metric will be replaced if it breaks down; others raise synthetic pre-training data lowering entropy. Signal: 502 points, 348 comments. Source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50601525 — article https://openai.com/index/perplexity-and-intelligence/

It does not name its operator, models or cost (connecting your own agents is "coming soon"). Alongside it, a Lean-formalisation progress dashboard (a claude.ai artifact) tracks an attempt to formalise, repair and check Alessandro Masullo's roughly 900-page purported proof of the Riemann Hypothesis; its progress bar is a fraction of issues filed, not of work remaining. Commenters stress that one hole sinks a proof, one says a major hole is already reported in the proof (unverified), another notes the bar's denominator is unknown, and a third compares the format to "Twitch plays Pokémon" for research. A separate Show HN, a Riemann Proof Handbook (https://github.com/tamnd/riemann-handbook, 86 points), builds a PDF/EPUB walkthrough of the same manuscript. Nothing here is a verified result; the item is about the format (public, Lean-checked agent work) and the sceptical reception. Signal: P vs NP 121 points, 107 comments; Lean map 167 points, 145 comments; handbook 86 points, 78 comments (see page). It cites CISA KEV and CVE data plus Bridgewater's own code output, and says defensive programmes (Anthropic's Glasswing, OpenAI's Daybreak, Google's Fairwind) have increased CVE submissions while AI coding tools add buggy code. This is an investment manager's analysis, and the one-in-three figure is its own estimate. In the thread, commenters disagree on whether the rise in attacks tracks a specific model release (GLM 5.3 was named) or earlier agentic-coding adoption; an incident-response practitioner says the work is becoming "a 24/7 job" with too few responders (anecdote).
