## Papers worth a deep dive

- Cross-Model In-Context Neurofeedback Reveals (Limited) Activation Control in LLMs — Probe-based monitors assume a model cannot steer what its own activations encode. The authors run in-context "neurofeedback": a subject model sees a score derived from its own activations each turn and tries to raise or lower it. Across ten open-weight models (9B to 32B), most move the score no more than a different writer model could; with a fixed prefilled reply, three of ten show small but consistent control. They also test whether models can evade an activation monitor by repeating flagged text. A direct, careful test of introspective access and of how far activation-based monitoring can be trusted. Authors: Baldelli, Kozierok, Bronzi, Fornasiere et al. (institution not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and data · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20179

- Beyond Belief Nesting: Rethinking Higher-Order Theory of Mind in Large Language Models — Shows that many "higher-order" theory-of-mind benchmarks admit lower-order shortcuts (order inflation) and introduces HopToM, with certified full-order questions. Accuracy of four frontier LLMs falls from 73.5% at first order to 20.0% at fourth order under direct prompting, and humans struggle too. Causal interventions tie a common error class to evidence for shallower belief prefixes. HopTrace, an inference-time method that encodes each interaction once into perspective records and resolves nested queries deterministically, adds 22.0 and 38.5 points at fourth and fifth order with 20-39x fewer tokens than multi-call methods. Authors: You, Wang, Liu, Liu et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 13 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated in the abstract · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17777

- Vision-Language Models Integrate Conflicting Evidence Like Biased Bayesian Observers — Proposes a two-parameter cue-combination model (evidence strength plus a modality-specific integration bias) that predicts how VLMs resolve text-versus-image conflicts, including under unseen degradations and without refitting. It reconciles the "blind faith in text" and "vision dominance" reports under one rule: a model can follow text while weighting visual evidence more heavily. A cognitive-science style account of model behaviour. Authors: Barua, Mahowald, Rudman (institution not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19407

- Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure? Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration in LLM Agents — Calibrates an agent's per-step verbalized confidence against the eventual episode outcome (using GRPO rollout groups and a proper scoring rule) instead of step-level correctness. On a Qwen2.5-7B agent in ALFWorld, TextWorld and WebShop, step-confidence AUROC against outcome rises from near chance to 0.68-0.85; online RL cuts expected calibration error from 0.42 to below 0.12. The agent recognises from step one which tasks it will fail, but telling a good attempt from a bad one on the same task is harder. Metacognition and self-knowledge in agents. Authors: Hathidara (SAP) · First submitted: 13 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614

- Monitoring latent reasoning in looped language models — An interpretability protocol with five lenses applied to eight looped LMs from four families (Ouro, LoopUS, Parcae, Rec-OLMo, Huginn). The main split is whether the prompt travels only inside the loop state ("carry loops") or is re-supplied at each iteration ("re-read loops"), which shapes whether the extra iterations improve the computation and how stable models are under prompt perturbation. Useful for anyone asking what happens inside a model while it "thinks" in latent space. Authors: Olszowiec, Tomaszewska, Żelaszczyk, Byra et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 13 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18127

- Does self-distillation improve reasoning? — A careful negative result. Revisiting SDPO and OPSD across code, science, tool use and math with Qwen3 (1.7B-14B) and OLMo-3-7B, the authors find the reported gains do not consistently generalise to unseen problems and are largely explained by overfitting or transfer of existing reasoning behaviour, not by use of privileged information; self-distillation gradients align weakly with policy gradients. Authors: Adhinarta, Wu, Sharma, Balakrishnan et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 13 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/sleepypandu/does-sd-improve-reasoning) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18613

- Cortex: Consolidating Context into Parametric Memory — Frames "memory consolidation" as internalising the gain a context gives, then discarding the context, while keeping unrelated abilities. Cortex generates prospective queries and distills context-conditioned outputs into mergeable low-rank or feed-forward updates; after 50 sequential factual edits on a 14B model it recovers 88.9% of the context-induced gain while keeping 99.8% of non-target performance. Brain-inspired framing of how a model might learn from context. Authors: Zhang, He, Liang, Xu et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: not stated (technical report) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20399
