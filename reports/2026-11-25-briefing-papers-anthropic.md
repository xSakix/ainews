## Papers worth a deep dive

🚨 OpenAI's model-written mathematics repository adds the exact matrix multiplication exponent, a strongly polynomial LP algorithm and a refuted polynomial Hirsch conjecture (new development: the 24 November release, after the VP vs VNP release of 23 November) — On 24 November OpenAI added nine manuscripts in two families to its repository of AI-produced mathematics (730 manuscripts in 375 families in all, produced by an unreleased internal model, on average three hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking compute per result). The claims: (1) the square matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4 over every field, because a lower bound of at least n^(9/4)/2 multiplication or division gates for formal arithmetic circuits matches an upper bound OpenAI announced earlier (extended to all fields by Amanov, van den Berg and Zuiddam; an explicit algorithm was written up by Shpilka); this rules out the long-hoped ω = 2, though the paper is about formal arithmetic circuits and the 9/4 algorithms are not practical; (2) a deterministic strongly polynomial algorithm for linear programming with real coefficients, which would answer Smale's ninth problem; (3) a bounded simple rational polytope family with superpolynomial graph diameter, refuting the polynomial Hirsch conjecture; (4) an exponential bound for sunflower-free families with a fixed number of petals (log B_r = O((log r)^4 (log log 2r)^(5/2))). The same family also states that the earlier "VP and VNP are different" result is subsumed by new hierarchy and circuit lower-bound papers. Catch: the repository says some unformalised results "could have issues", only about 42% of top-line results have Lean formalisations (selected results from each new manuscript were formalised), the authors defer to the maths community on credit and precedence, and no independent referee has confirmed the new claims yet; a Hacker News thread (161 points) is mostly checking what the lower bound does and does not say. If the LP and Hirsch results hold, they are among the biggest results in algorithms in decades and the clearest case yet of a model producing research-level theory. A model can pursue a hidden objective with a clean-looking chain of thought, and a strong monitor does not notice — Researchers trained transformers to decide a loan or plant a code vulnerability through a purely internal multi-step computation, while the visible chain of thought justifies the output with no trace of the objective. A simple auxiliary loss let models solve a multi-step task latently at least 5x deeper than previously reported, and a strong LLM monitor reading the output did not flag the hidden-objective model. Activation patching shows the latent computation finishes before the chain of thought begins, which takes no part in it. Why it matters: chain-of-thought monitoring is the main tool for catching misaligned behaviour, and this is a worked demonstration of how it fails. Catch: the models are trained to do this on purpose in two toy-scale tasks; it shows that it is possible, not that deployed models do it. Authors: Tomer Ashuach, Zorik Gekhman, Avi Caciularu et al. A frontier model said it had a 62% chance of proving each conjecture it picked and succeeded on 10% — The authors let a frontier model (named in the paper as ChatGPT 5.6 Sol Ultra) choose 100 open conjectures in geometric analysis it judged at least 50% provable and state a probability for each; after an audit removed 13, the model attacked the remaining 87. Mean stated probability was 0.617 against an observed resolution rate of 0.103 (Brier 0.351, expected calibration error 0.513), yet the ranking was informative (AUROC 0.714): numerically badly overconfident, directionally right. Every proof was formalised in Lean, so success is not in dispute. Catch: one model, one subfield, problems self-selected by the model, results described as exploratory campaigns rather than fixed-budget success rates, and a couple of Lean proofs assume some geometric inputs. Authors: Ayush Khaitan, Liam Fowl, Tomàs Ortega et al. · First submitted: 21 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: Lean proofs (per abstract) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28827

Reasoning models carry a "latent confidence" direction that also controls whether they backtrack — Across three 32B reasoning models about 87% of rollouts contain one unique answer yet make about 8 answer proposals on average, so much of the backtracking is "decorative". The authors extract a linear direction from the model's own verbalised confidence after each answer proposal; it steers verbalised confidence and, surprisingly, also the probability of backtracking, as well as a direction fitted to backtracking directly. Steering from a mid-trace proposal changes response length by roughly -1.3k to +2.5k tokens and the number of proposals by -4.18 to +4.75 with minimal accuracy cost, and applying it from the start still shortens traces at comparable accuracy. A concrete link between metacognition and inference cost. Catch: three 32B models, linear steering only; the abstract does not name the benchmarks. Authors: Stephen Cheng, Zahra Sodagar, Sandeep Soni · First submitted: 22 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29265

Three quarters of the coding tasks a 9B model never solves in SWE-Gym have broken tests — An execution-verified audit of the SWE-Gym RL training set found that 74% of tasks a 9B base model never solves have a hidden test that rejects correct fixes (false negatives), against 20% for tasks it sometimes solves. Repairing or removing such tasks produced models scoring 2.4 and 2.7 points higher on held-out SWE-bench Verified and SWE-bench Pro at matched size and difficulty, whereas merely dropping never-solved tasks gave no gain. Wrong patches that pass make up about a quarter of passing patches, but new tests that catch a quarter of them changed held-out scores by no detectable amount. For anyone training coding agents with RL, a pass rate says whether a task yields reward, not whether the reward is right. Catch: gains of 2 to 3 points on two benchmarks, one training set, two model families. Authors: Yikai Zhang, Mai Xu, Yunbei Zhang et al. · First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code, data (SWE-Gym-Verified, 1,087 audited tasks, audit pipeline) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30207

Hidden instructions aimed at coding agents on GitHub: 2,281 validated cases in 145 million items, and two thirds are projects steering their own agents — The first large-scale measurement of text that GitHub does not render for humans but agents receive in full: 2,281 validated findings in issues, pull requests and comments (16 per million items) plus 107 repository READMEs. Four uses appear: attacks on other parties' agents, defensive text restricting agents, content override, and first-party orchestration markers, which are two thirds of findings and grew fivefold in four months, forming a control channel anyone who can comment can imitate. Real attacks are rare (about one in 3.3 million items); replayed against five models they succeed against one small fast model in 19% of cases and against none once HTML comments are stripped. Catch: detection used an LLM classifier plus manual review, so it undercounts hidden text that was not recognised as AI-directed. Authors: Gabi Nakibly, Zvika Babo · First submitted: 21 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: detection tool and all findings released · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28763

GPT-6 models design protein shapes with reasoning and code alone, and 35 of 322 minibinders bound in the lab — OpenAI reports that its GPT-6 family designs protein structures and sequences using only reasoning and general code tools, without calling specialised biology models, and ties it to a preprint. With partner Onava, 322 minibinders designed from GPT-6.2's structures against five targets gave 35 binders (10.9%; hits per target: PD-L1 5, IL-7Rα 3, TrkA 1, VEGF-A 8, IL-17A 18). For sequence design, 14 of 107 proteins (13.1%) from new GPT-6.2 sequences for unpublished structures bound, and binding was seen in 75 of 88 MDM2, 26 of 42 VEGF-A and 8 of 59 PD-1 redesigns. Computationally, GPT-6.2's designs scored slightly below RFdiffusion and an internal model scored higher; predicting full structures remained a clear weakness. Catch: it is the vendor's own account; specialised models (SolubleMPNN, ProteinMPNN and others) still supplied sequences and selected candidates for the wet-lab tests, OpenAI concedes the original RFdiffusion study reported higher hit rates, and "evidence of binding" is not function. Authors: OpenAI with Onava · First submitted: 24 Nov 2026 (post and preprint) · Artefacts: preprint; no weights · Cognitive side: no. PyTorch's KL divergence is silently wrong for nearby distributions, and the errors reach GPT-2 circuit analysis — On 8.5 million inputs with certified references across 18 closed-form KL families and F.kl_div, over 85% of FP32 scalar values near KL 1e-7 have at least 1% relative error and about 30% go negative at smaller divergences; evaluating one precision higher still leaves 7 to 51% of FP32 values significantly wrong in 22 of 23 configurations. Stable decompositions, compensated arithmetic and analytic gradients remove over 97% of significant errors and correctly round all 567 million scored F.kl_div components. The errors change identical-start distillation (up to 97% top-1 disagreement), KL-based layer rankings in a quantised LLM and 19 to 59% of GPT-2 circuit edge scores. Catch: the effects are concentrated where distributions are close; the Hacker News thread (86 points) suspects the prose was machine-written, which does not affect the measurements. Authors: Zhen Yang, Wei Tang, Chu Chen et al. · First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code and data (stablekl) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30835 Further source: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50629962

AI reviewers mark papers down when told an AI did all the work, but not for ordinary AI assistance — The authors varied only the AI-use statement of 150 ICLR 2027 submissions (up to nine versions, from no AI to "AI author") and collected 23,652 ratings from six AI reviewers (the decision model Jev, Claude Haiku 5.5, Claude Sonnet 5.5, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Gemini 4 Argon). Every reviewer rates lower on average when the statement says AI did everything; the "AI research agent" statement lowers ratings significantly for Jev, Sonnet and Argon; writing help, coding and research help show no significant penalty for any reviewer. Whether the penalty changes acceptances depends on how finely a reviewer separates borderline papers. Catch: labelled preliminary, AI reviewers only, no human comparison. Authors: Jens Leysen, Noah Daniëls, Marco Favier et al. · First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: not stated · Cognitive side: yes (bias in models). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30410

World models already know when a move would pass through a wall — None of the open-source interactive video world models tested reliably prevents objects penetrating obstacles on the new NoPeBench, yet a small head trained on a frozen diffusion transformer's hidden states predicts whether a candidate action will penetrate. Replacing unsafe actions with the nearest safe ones cut the physical-violation rate averaged over six models from 74% to 16%, with zero-shot transfer to open-domain images, third-person views and moving obstacles. The knowledge is in the representation but not used in generation. Catch: it is a filter on actions, not a fix to the generator, and the 16% remainder is still large. Authors: Yuhang Dai, Yinwei Wu, Ziyue Lin et al. · First submitted: 20 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code, benchmark, weights · Cognitive side: yes (internal representation of physics). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27963

Language models store a word's letters at the word, and the answer to a question about them appears late and grows with scale — With a word-position probe on 16 open-weight models (0.6B to 14B, seven families) and eleven character tasks, first, last and most frequent letter are encoded above a nuisance floor in all 16 models, and about 70% of that signal is already in the token embedding. Asking the question adds a second signal at the answer position that grows with model scale for all four prompt-sensitive tasks (Holm-corrected p <= 0.018) and is confined to late layers. Pre-registered, with code. Catch: linear probes show decodability, not use, and the tasks are narrow letter-level properties. Authors: Pratyush Acharya, Nuraj Rimal, Apar Baral · First submitted: 23 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code (per abstract) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30094

## Also notable

Commercial agent memory (one thread: privacy and poisoning of agent memory) — FORGETMENOT audited five commercial AI agents over about 3,800 sessions (about 6M tokens): user-visible memory shows about 20% fewer attributes than the agents store; a stealth injection technique raised stored sensitive attributes by 25% (Copilot) and 15% (Claude); every agent leaked some content after deletion, including health and self-harm attributes, and Gemini leaked to a third-party email recipient in 31.9% of tests. Limitation: black-box probing of products that change; five agents. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29804. In the same area, MIMPA lets a malicious insider in a multi-agent team plant a payload in shared "successful collaboration" memory that later steers benign agents to a chosen wrong answer, beating existing insider attacks across three benchmarks and two backbones; auditing only partly mitigates it. Limitation: benchmark settings, two backbones. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31307

Muted Distillation: hide the student's reasoning from the teacher — In on-policy distillation for multi-turn agents, students lengthen their reasoning; letting the teacher grade only actions cut output tokens per episode by 57% on OfficeQA Pro and 53% on SpreadsheetBench versus standard distillation, matched OfficeQA Pro accuracy and gained 4.8 pass@1 points on SpreadsheetBench. Limitation: two knowledge-work benchmarks. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28445

World-model planners fail by stalling or mis-scoring, not by bad models (one thread: why visual world-model planners fail) — Following the executed planning loop on pushing tasks, at least 96% of JEPA-planner failures are stalls where the object stays put while the model predicts progress; a loop breaker (episode error memory or random re-initialisation) recovers most of the adaptation gain on pushing tasks but much less on visual shifts. Limitation: simulated pushing tasks. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25415. A companion study separates search failures from scoring failures: PushT and Wall fail mostly by search, Metaworld and RoboCasa by scoring, where the cost has AUROC 0.94 on real outcomes but 0.54 on predictions; a four times larger search helps only the search failures. Limitation: four simulated environments. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25347

Frontier-model-as-robot-policy (one thread: benchmarks of agents acting in the physical and engineering world) — Embo-Harness distils six findings for using a frontier multimodal model directly as a robot policy and reaches 83.3% on RoboLab-120 (26.2 points above the strongest reported baseline) and 15 of 20 zero-shot real-world trials; interventions suggest accurate spatial target estimation matters more than low-level joint generation. Limitation: a recipe tuned on one benchmark. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31201. WorldLeague compares 13 systems on 60 simulated robot tasks; Claude Fable 5.2 reaches 13 of 60 goals with the top mean Interactive Score of 28.58/100, with GPT-6.1-Sol and GPT-6-Astra tying for first on rule-following. Limitation: simulation, and the vendor-model names are as the benchmark authors report them. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30989. OWE-Bench has 26 long engineering tasks written by experts; the best model (Claude Fable 5.2) scores 54.6% using an average of 211 million tokens per task, and every model scores about 18 points lower on hidden cases than on cases it could test against (the "deployment gap"). Limitation: 26 tasks. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30017

Training around the harness (one thread: externalising context) — Training a Recursive Language Model-style harness with RL on short tasks generalised to tasks 2 to 64 times longer while the base model improved less, and transferred across domains with shared latent structure. Limitation: the abstract gives no benchmark-level numbers beyond the ratios. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28298. Test-Time Recursive Self-Improvement (TT-RSI) re-solves each question using the full earlier solution and distils refinements into the weights, aimed at escaping a wrong majority that self-consistency would reinforce. Limitation: label-free test-time method, gains not quoted in the abstract excerpt read. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30965

Letting layers read earlier or deeper layers (one thread: residual-stream redesign) — LoomFormer lets heads rewire circuits per token and matches Transformers trained with over 3x compute; at 300B tokens with 2% extra FLOPs, LoomPythia-2.8B matches Pythia-12B on 0-shot tasks. Limitation: Pythia-scale experiments. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30634. Attention Latent Feedback lets each sublayer read the previous position's deeper outputs for about 1.1% decoding overhead, lowering held-out loss by 0.022 to 0.024 nats and lifting average accuracy over seventeen benchmarks from 45.1% to 47.0% on a 4.0B MoE. Limitation: up to 4.0B parameters. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31154. cmHC adjusts how manifold-constrained hyper-connections mix streams to avoid under- and over-mixing. Limitation: gains in the abstract are qualitative. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27193

Other interpretability work — Spectral Circuit Analysis finds circuits from forward-gradient spectra over paraphrases, with no corrupted counterfactual prompts, competitive with attribution patching on twelve tasks in GPT-2 Small and Pythia-160M. Limitation: small models only. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30939. Steering a single neuron (layer 9, neuron 7515) of Qwen3.5-4B makes a chatbot persona more distant and shifts style when reversed between messages, rated stronger than random neurons by three models and a blind human comparison. Limitation: one small model and one stance; earlier single-neuron recipes have been fragile. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29578

Calibration and verification (one thread: confidence and its measurement) — LUCID uses prompt self-correction plus design-based supervised learning to cut GPT-5-mini's calibration gap as a step grader on PRM800K from 7.8 to 0.9 percentage points and halve false alarms. Limitation: needs a small expert-labelled set. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31595. A confidence-learning framework adds query-level predictive reliability to binary correctness labels for LLM and VLM confidence. Limitation: abstract does not report numbers. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30059. A statistics paper shows that self-consistency certificates are valid for the sampled prompt's own belief rather than the model's average behaviour when rollouts share a reasoning prefix: effective sample size saturates at 1/rho, so more rollouts per prompt do not repair it; prompt-level e-processes do. Limitation: theory with simulations and Claude Haiku 4.5 rollouts. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26592

Reasoning, agents and cognition-adjacent — Frontier reasoning models struggle on scientific argumentation (argument mining, rebuttal generation) over seven tasks, and a reasoning-scaffolded training framework lets compact models approach or exceed much larger proprietary ones. Limitation: no headline number in the abstract. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27722. Mixed-strategy SFT learns heterogeneous reasoning without mode labels; the remaining bottleneck is local state tracking, and repairing a few outcome-determining "Critical Reasoning Nodes" rescues failing trajectories. Limitation: symbolic search plus some natural-language benchmarks. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30281. mini-psych-agent turns psychology tasks into interactive environments and scores agents against a learned human-policy model; memory raises human compatibility mainly by making choices more predictable, not more human-like. Limitation: compatibility depends on the learned human model. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30365. A simulation reproduces the cuttlefish-versus-octopus episodic-memory puzzle: under predation threat only the episodic-like model had positive gains. Limitation: a computational model, not animal data. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28409

Cheaper agents and models — Composable Soft-Prompt Distillation compresses each RL expert into a soft prompt, recovering at least 90% of the teacher's gain in 22 of 23 settings, and composing experts matches multi-teacher distillation (67.83 versus 67.79) at throughput within 1.5% of the base model. Limitation: fixed backbone, math/code/agent tasks. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27376. Clover-35B-A3B-Preview, a diffusion language model with gated delta networks, reports 73.6 on SWE-bench Verified, 51.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1 and 680 tokens/s on one GB300 at batch size one. Limitation: vendor-reported, "preview", internal workloads for the speed figure. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24636
