## Papers worth a deep dive

- Base Models Can Reason By Taking a Cue From Training Data — Fixing a single opening token cue makes a base model competitive with its RL-trained counterpart on math and coding: ".\n\nOkay" lifts Olmo-3-7B on MATH-500 pass@1 from 42% to 78%, and "Alright," lifts Qwen3-14B from 72% to 87%. RL mostly makes such cues more likely, and causal data interventions (turning an arbitrary word like "chicken" into an effective cue) trace the effect to associations in the pretraining data. This reframes what RL adds and gives a prompt-prefix trick the reader can try. Authors: MIT · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06851

- Self-Generated Feedback Destabilizes Test-Time Training: A Causal Decomposition of Long-Horizon Adaptation — Across 128K-token streams, test-time training that keeps updates from the model's own generated text worsens prediction on independent human text, in three TTT-E2E sizes and also with Adam on Qwen3-4B. Matched comparisons (frozen-generator, recorded replay) locate the damage in the feedback loop, not in writing itself: a frozen generator removes over 98% of the damage at 125M and 760M. A careful negative result for anyone building models that learn while they run. Authors: KAUST · First submitted: 4 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: yes (learning and memory in weights). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05076

- Language Models that Play Chess and Explain Their Moves (Queen) — A 4B chess-language model couples a silent expert chess encoder to an instruction-tuned LM via cross-attention, trained on a question-answering curriculum plus iterative distillation, so it plays at roughly Grandmaster level while explaining moves and plans. Interesting as a way to expose what a superhuman engine "knows" in language. Authors: Princeton (Danqi Chen group) · First submitted: 2 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: yes (concept extraction from an expert's representations). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03695

- From Evidence to Action: How Tool-Using Agents Fail — Across ten model-harness configurations, strong static judgement of whether to act coexists with much weaker interactive execution. Failures start before execution: agents stop with incomplete investigation or act before required evidence is established; once evidence is gathered, single actions are reliable but multi-action workflows expose unresolved prerequisites. Introduces SafeActBench. Authors: National University of Singapore · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code, benchmark · Cognitive side: partly (planning and knowing when evidence suffices). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07753

- Memadapter: Counterfactual Adaptation Against Memory-induced Sycophancy — Argues that even correct, objective memories make agents over-align with users' past beliefs, so filtering bad memories is not enough; proposes adapting how much a memory influences the answer by context, using counterfactual training. A concrete look at a bias that arises from long-term memory. Authors: Jilin University · First submitted: 4 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: yes (memory-induced bias). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05162

- Towards Looped Models Done Right, Part II: Rethinking at Fixed Points — Looped LMs whose recurrent states approach fixed points allow truncated backpropagation, terminal KV sharing for decoding with almost no accuracy loss, a distilled student prefilling up to 1.79x faster, and RL gradients 2x faster from saved rollout states. Relevant to latent-reasoning-by-recurrence research. Authors: Institute of Foundation Models · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code · Cognitive side: partly (iterative latent computation). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06833
