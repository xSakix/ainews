- 🚨 **Anthropic launches Claude Haiku 5.5 at roughly a quarter of Haiku 4.5's cost, and halves Sonnet 5.5's cache-read price** — Haiku 5.5 (`claude-haiku-5-5`) is Anthropic's small, high-volume tier, now with a 1M-token context window, 128k max output and adaptive thinking with an effort setting (a first for Haiku). Breaking change for migrators: manual extended thinking (`budget_tokens`) returns a 400 error, adaptive thinking is on by default, and a new tokenizer uses slightly more tokens per task. Anthropic's own figures, with named customer quotes, put it at OSWorld 2.1 (offline subset) 72.4% against 15.7% for Haiku 4.5 and 83.9% for Sonnet 5.5, Terminal-Bench 4.0 39.2% against 70.6% for Sonnet 5.5, and Humanity's Last Exam 45.9% without tools; it also compares against OpenAI's GPT-6 Luna. The post says plainly that Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 remain the choice for complex agentic coding and that Haiku suits compaction, summarisation and subagent work. Pricing per million tokens is $0.10 input / $0.50 output for prompts up to 100k tokens ($0.50 / $2.50 above), against $1 / $5 for Haiku 4.5. Cyber safeguards are stricter than Haiku 4.5's and somewhat looser than Sonnet 5.5's; biology safeguards match Sonnet 5.5. Alongside it: Sonnet 5.5 cache reads drop from $0.20 to $0.10 per million tokens (Anthropic says about 20% cheaper on most agentic work), Max and Team plans get monthly API credits ($100 for Max 5x, $200 for Max 20x, up to $500 pooled for Team) rolling out this week, and the Python and TypeScript SDKs gain beta toolset classes for browser and computer use. - **Liquid AI opens the weights of d1: a 3.1B vision-language decision model and a 587M text, image and speech version** — Yesterday's briefing listed d1 as API only; both repositories went public on Hugging Face on 7 October (first public commit 16:56 UTC), so this is the material new development. A decision model returns calibrated yes/no, choice and score answers in one forward pass with zero output tokens. d1-3B is built on LFM2.5-VL-3B (3.12B parameters, 32,768-token context, SigLIP2 vision encoder, 16 languages) and d1-omni-600M on the LFM2.5-Encoder-350M (587M parameters: 381M trunk and head, 94M vision encoder, 112M FastConformer audio encoder, 16,384-token context, up to 30 s of speech). Liquid scored d1-3B itself with the official Decision Index 0.2.1 scorer: 48.57, behind only Winnow-12B (50.02) and ahead of Decider 35B-A3B (47.11); the card says it is not a leaderboard submission. Measured latency for one question is 8 ms on an RTX 4090, 30 ms on an Apple M5 Pro and 50 ms on a Jetson Orin Nano. The image score (74.1 mean on 11 benchmarks against 73.9 for the base model) shows the decision head did not cost vision quality, though d1-3B is weakest on the Knowledge category (23.8). Use needs transformers 5.14 or later and `trust_remote_code`. GGUF and w8a8 variants exist, and a community WebGPU demo of the 600M model is on r/LocalLLaMA. All benchmarks are Liquid's. ## Rest of the world

- **KandinskyLab publishes Kandinsky 6.0, MIT-licensed text-to-audio-video models of 3B (Lite) and 29B (Pro)** — A family of dual-stream diffusion transformers that generate five-second 24 fps clips with synchronised 44 kHz audio, including lip-sync, from text or a first-frame image, with a separate super-resolution model to Full-HD. A video stream is joined to a newly trained audio stream by bidirectional cross-attention; the audio stream was first trained alone on audio corpora, then both jointly, followed by supervised fine-tuning, RL post-training and distillation (10-step distilled variants for Pro and Lite). The paper (arXiv 2610.05608, v1 on 4 October) claims Pro clearly beats Kandinsky 5.0 Pro in side-by-side human evaluation and is competitive with leading audio-video models, especially on speech; that is the authors' own evaluation. Code works in Diffusers, vLLM-Omni, SGLang, FastVideo and ComfyUI, and community int8 and NVFP4 quantisations appeared within a day. Dating caveat: the lab's repositories were created in September and made public in stages (Pro's repository now shows as access-restricted to the API, Lite's README is readable); community quantisations of the released weights date from 6 October, so I treat the release as surfacing on 6 to 7 October, inside the 72-hour late-release rule, and found no earlier briefing mentioning it. The Lite card gives no hardware requirement beyond CPU offload in the example. Artefacts: weights (MIT) / technical report on arXiv / code / Diffusers integration / HF demo · Sizes or variants: Lite 3B (3.18B parameters counted on the hub), Pro 29B (30.1B counted), pretrain, SFT and distilled versions, VSR upscalers · Released: weights public by 6 October 2026, paper v1 4 October 2026.
