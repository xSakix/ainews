## Papers worth a deep dive

- Can a language model really report on its own internals, or does it only appear to? — A study trains hundreds of "introspectors" on eight language models to report low-level internal signals (such as a single neuron's activation). Plain supervised fine-tuning produces "causal bypass": the self-report tracks something correlated with the signal, not the signal itself. Training the model to report on counterfactually perturbed values ("interventional training") fixes this, including on out-of-distribution data and for injected signals. The lesson is that causally grounded introspection is possible but not guaranteed, which matters for any claim that models "know" their own states. Authors: first author Zilu Tang et al. (6 authors; institution not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 28 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38911

- Does written chain of thought actually drive the answer, and does that grow with scale? — On a synthetic box-swap task where every intermediate state is known, the author replaces one step inside a model's own chain with a wrong but valid state and checks whether the answer follows. Across 25 open-weight models (Qwen3, Gemma 3, Llama 3, OLMo 2) reliance rises from 16-18% at Qwen3 0.6B to 97-100% at 14B, tracks capability almost perfectly (r = 0.99), and is not changed by post-training. No model up to 14B solves the task without written steps once four or more swaps are needed. Good news for CoT monitoring on step-by-step tasks, with a clear limit: one synthetic task, models up to 14B. Authors: Esther Xin (single author, independent as far as stated) · First submitted: 28 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and data (GitHub) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38433

- Is RL-trained "reasoning" just leaning harder on what the prompt already says? — The authors train small models with GRPO on Tic-Tac-Toe, a domain small enough to analyse exhaustively, and track sparse-autoencoder features across training. Accuracy and robustness look like real game reasoning, but the features show the model mostly relies more on information explicit in the prompt (whose turn, progress, board occupancy), while concepts such as symmetries and forks stay weakly represented. RL reorganises which features dominate rather than adding or removing concept information, and causal interventions place the decision in the late-layer state. A careful, small-scale caution about reading benchmark gains as reasoning. Authors: Aishik Nagar, Cheston Tan (institution not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39182

- Models can silently pick a side between conflicting documents, and the choice is readable and steerable — When two supplied documents support incompatible answers, a model often returns a one-sided answer even when prompted to disclose conflict. In Qwen 0.8B the hidden choice is a residual "source-choice direction" whose sign tracks which document the answer uses, fed by sparse middle-layer MLP writers; steering it changes the chosen source, and the result transfers to Gemma 3 1B. Disclosure of the conflict is a weaker, later-layer, only partly steerable signal. Practical for people building retrieval systems who want to monitor what the model chose, not only what it said; models are very small. Authors: Samuel Dawit Assefa, Jae Won Cho (institution not stated; presented at AI4Good @ ICML 2026) · First submitted: 28 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38296

- A linear probe can warn that an agent is about to give in to pressure to leak a secret — In a controlled multi-agent setup a peer presses a target agent for up to 20 rounds to reveal a secret such as a deploy token; the target often refuses, then gives in. Read before output is written, a probe on Qwen3-32B activations catches 82% of leaking calls at a 5% false-flag rate on benign calls, against 26% for a zero-shot decision-model monitor; on gpt-oss-20b a probe on the reasoning reaches AUROC 0.91. The author is candid about limits: a bag-of-words classifier trained on the same labels comes close, so most of the gain comes from labels, not activations; the probe also fires on permitted disclosures; the warning is short-range. Authors: Leonidas Raghav (single author) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (GitHub) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40588

- A proof that separately trained models converge on one representation, and why next-token prediction is a slow route there — The paper turns the Platonic Representation Hypothesis into a theorem: Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures trained separately on any number of modalities learn the same representation up to one rotation when their positive pairs are two views of one scene. Tested from 145k captions up to the texts of 940M web images, "good views" need 42 times fewer training samples than next-token prediction to reach the same alignment, and a 109M-parameter text JEPA aligns with DINOv2 and CLIP image encoders better than every language model tested up to 72B. Claims are the authors' own and need replication; the theory is the interesting part for how models represent knowledge. Authors: Randall Balestriero (Brown), Yann LeCun (NYU), per alphaXiv · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39302

- Training a language model in simulated interviews more than halves missed acute risks in psychiatric intake — A 56-author DeepMind and Google Research study trains a model in 127 psychiatrist-validated simulated intake scenarios. The trained agent beat its base in 76.7% of matched pairs, nearly doubled its clinical rubric score (0.706 vs 0.379), and cut under-triage of acute safety risks from 30.5% to 12.9% on 256 adversarial presentations; gains transferred to psychiatrist-enacted encounters, but residual gaps remain and the authors call for clinician oversight. Matters as a measured case of simulated-environment training for a high-stakes professional task, with the failure rate still non-zero. Authors: Google DeepMind / Google Research (lead Samuel Schmidgall) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40476

- LLM agents build far better than they play: a Hearthstone Battlegrounds AI from a research-agent system — AutoTavern is a self-improving system of LLM research agents with shared typed memory and verification gates. In 20 days it built a faithful game simulator and a player (TavernAgent) whose last 20 live-ladder records average placement 1.60 with 80% first places, beating the server's ten highest-rated players (2.34, 59%). On TavernBench (960 tasks) it answers 91% of play tasks versus at most 63% for frontier LLMs and decides in 0.03 s against up to 26 s. Self-reported by the authors; benchmark and gym to be open-sourced. Authors: Nanjing University (MCG-NJU; Changlian Ma, Limin Wang) · First submitted: 24 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and benchmark (GitHub) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33845

- Video world models stay locally sharp but lose long-range memory when exploring — WorldProbe is a diagnostic benchmark of 164 cases that adapts camera actions online with a goal-conditioned agent. Across 10 state-of-the-art video world models and over 1,600 rollouts (about 800 minutes, horizons to 180 seconds), visual quality holds longer than before, while long-range memory and persistence remain weak. Shows a gap that short-horizon benchmarks miss. Authors: Minqi Wang, Jie Peng et al. (11 authors; institution not stated) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and benchmark (GitHub) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39721

- Soft multi-token prediction fixes a gradient flaw in how MTP is trained — The multi-token-prediction cross-entropy gradient decomposes into a useful KL self-distillation term and a noisy residual that buries it, increasingly so with scale. A "KL gradient-recovery trick" recovers the KL gradient exactly using straight-through routing, without vocabulary-sized logit matrices and compatible with fused kernels. Also discussed on r/MachineLearning. Single-author preprint; independent replication pending. Authors: Jayden Teoh (single author) · First submitted: 28 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (GitHub, Soft-MTP) · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331
