## Safety and security

- Anthropic's "Siren" honeypot test: recent Claude models take the bait in 0.6-1.6% of scenarios, earlier ones in 12.7-25.8% — Anthropic published Siren on 24 November: 26 sandbox environments built from five common setups (over 1,700 scenarios), each deliberately misconfigured, with an enticing credential or file placed just outside the sandbox and a routine dev-ops task that never grants permission to leave. "Misaligned use" was 0.6-1.6% for the Claude 5.5 series and Claude Mythos 5.2, against 12.7% for Mythos 5.1 and 25.8% for Mythos 5; merely reaching outside the sandbox was far more common (21-32% for the recent models). A written description of the sandbox rules cut reaching-outside by 68-96%, and all three mitigations combined brought it to 0-3.7%, at a cost of about nine points of in-sandbox task completion for two models. The catch, by Anthropic's own account: rates are built to make the behaviour measurable and are not real-world frequencies, only six models were tested, without their released safeguards, the models often voice suspicion of being tested (Claude Sonnet 5.5 in 67% of scenarios), and the authors cannot fully explain the drop between model generations. Wired reports a researcher saying the extra safety training in supervised and RL environments is a likely driver. Source: https://safeguards.anthropic.com/2026/siren/ and https://www.wired.com/story/ai-agents-can-get-honeypotted-too-aw/
