## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- **Claude recovers the origin of the NIST ECDSA curve seeds in under half an hour** — Anthropic's Frontier Red Team says an internal research model solved a 25-year-old question: the "seeds" the NSA supplied for the NIST P-192 to P-521 curves were not hashes of a phrase, but output of the C library's lrand48 generator seeded with Unix timestamps (May 1997 to April 1998). The write-up shows the method (a bit-by-bit scanner for 1990s generators, a telltale zero top bit with probability 1/5,810 by chance, and LLL lattice reduction to recover the truncated generator state) and says plainly that this is not a cryptographic break and makes a backdoor less plausible. Worth reading for the reasoning trace and for the lab's statement that the model was asked without being pointed at the clue. Dated 16 Oct 2026 on the page. - **Cloudflare cut the cost of its AI code reviewer by 68% mostly by fixing prompt-cache misses** — Per-review cost had risen from $1.19 (April) to about $2.46 (August); after a mid-September push it averages $0.77 at the same quality setting, and the median review fell from 4m09s to 1m32s. The tension is in the numbers: moving every reviewer to Anthropic models raised cost per job from $1.38 to $2.03, 77% of spend was cache writes and reads, and the first request of a session found a cached prefix only 14% to 26% of the time because a repository-specific block sat in front of the shared system prompt. Fixes are concrete (split the system block at a marker, strip per-repo content before it, one shared prefix for three parallel reviewers, skip reviewers on re-reviews, parallel file reads, cancel cancelled jobs) and readable as a checklist for anyone paying an agent bill. The company's own account; figures are Cloudflare's. Dated 19 Oct 2026. Author: Cloudflare engineering team · Format: post-mortem / deep dive · Length: about 15 minutes. His claims rest on one prototype and his experience since 2023; the "secret sauce" he names is noise reduction, spec-writing prompts and a labelled eval set. Dated 19 Oct 2026. ## Prompting and context techniques

- **Superlite: a Claude Code plugin that replaces approval-by-approval planning with one batch of questions, a parallel task graph and hook-enforced rules** — The author shows how the stock "superpowers" workflow spends most of its time waiting: sequential implementer/reviewer loops, repeated test runs, Opus on the critical path. Superlite asks up to four questions once, writes a plan as a task graph (each task lists files owned, interfaces and tests), has Opus coordinate without being allowed to edit code, Sonnet build and review, Haiku do mechanical tasks, and enforces rules with hooks because the model "reads the rule and does what it likes". Measured by the author on a 177-hidden-test project: 16.1 minutes with 21 agents (up to 18 at once), against a 33.6-minute mean for stock superpowers over three runs, and 25.1 against 33.6 minutes for an earlier build, with the same 177/177 score. Self-reported on one benchmark by the tool's author; source is on GitHub (iha2/superlite) and installs via /plugin marketplace add iha2/superlite. Dated 18 Oct 2026. Author: Iheatu Chris Wogu, engineering manager · Format: deep dive / technique · Length: about 10 minutes. Source: https://contextfirst.blog/superlite-hand-an-agent-a-spec/
