## Papers worth a deep dive

- On-Policy or Off-Policy? Rollout policy turns out not to be the variable that matters in distillation — A controlled study of strong-to-weak distillation varies rollout policy, token-level KL direction and learning rate independently across Llama3 and Qwen2.5 on science, medical and arithmetic reasoning. Contrary to the popular story that on-policy learning reduces forgetting and gives sparser updates, rollout policy plays no central role: KL direction shapes task performance and output coverage, and learning rate governs forgetting and update sparsity. A careful negative result that cuts against much of this week's on-policy-distillation hype, and one the reader can use when choosing a post-training recipe. Authors: University of Cambridge (van der Schaar lab) · First submitted: 2026-09-28 · Artefacts: none listed. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35259

- Latent world action models lose the generalization that justified them — World action models predict future video alongside actions. The paper compares "explicit" WAMs (denoise future frames at inference) with "latent" WAMs (discard the future for speed) under a matched backbone, data and budget. Latent WAMs match explicit ones in-distribution but degrade consistently on environmental perturbation, data efficiency and task generalization; the gap comes almost entirely from the action expert no longer conditioning on future representations. This answers a live robotics question (is the expensive video denoising needed at test time?) with a clean ablation. Authors: Tsinghua University (LeapLab, per Hugging Face org) · First submitted: 2026-09-28 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/LeapLabTHU/Simple-WAM). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34981

- Multi-teacher on-policy distillation is dominated by the loudest teacher — On Qwen3.5 models at three sizes, merging several RL-trained specialists into one student through multi-teacher on-policy distillation does not beat distilling from the single best specialist, and barely inherits the maths specialist's advantage. The diagnosis: instruction-following feedback has several times the spread of maths feedback and dominates the updates. Domain-Normalized MOPD rescales per-domain feedback to fix it. A concrete, inspectable explanation of why "merge specialists by distillation" underdelivers. Authors: Nanyang Technological University and collaborators · First submitted: 2026-09-28 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/LiXin97/DN-MOPD). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.35347

- Latent visual reasoning tokens barely look at the image — Analysing multimodal models that "think" in continuous latent tokens, the authors find an "evidence-credit gap": latent tokens respond only weakly to image perturbations that change the correct answer, because final-answer-only GRPO rewards give no signal about which visual evidence to keep. ReaLVR adds visual-evidence supervision to the latent tokens. Worth explaining because it is an interpretability-style probe of an increasingly popular reasoning format, with a plausible mechanism for why it under-delivers. Authors: Amazon and academic collaborators (per Hugging Face org) · First submitted: 2026-09-28 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/xixiaouab/ReaLVR-code), project page. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34563

- When does learning to generate images help a model understand them? (OmniTaskonomy) — Using paired image-to-image generation and image-to-text understanding tasks that pose the same problem, the authors show image-generation supervision improves understanding under the right recipe, with gains growing with generation data. They then build a taxonomy of 19 generation tasks and 25 understanding capabilities and map which transfers to which. Gives the unified-model debate (does generation help perception?) a measured transfer map instead of anecdotes. Authors: UC Berkeley (per Hugging Face org; co-author affiliations not verified) · First submitted: 2026-09-29 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/para-lost/OmniTaskonomy). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38079

- Box²-Bench: can models ignore bad workflow guidance? — Holds model and task fixed while varying how reliable a human-designed agent workflow is. Frontier models gain from correct guidance but are vulnerable when it is misleading. Training two open-weight models on bad workflows shows counterfactual SFT improves robustness while outcome-based RL can shift the balance. Directly relevant to anyone building agent harnesses, where over-trusting scaffolding is a real failure mode. Authors: Southern University of Science and Technology (per Hugging Face org) · First submitted: 2026-09-30 · Artefacts: none listed. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39578

- Direct-decision models squeeze ordinal scales (JEV-like evaluators) — Analysing JEV 1.13 and three open KEV models, the authors show these low-latency classifier/judge models use only 67–76% of the effective gold label support on 36 ordinal datasets (versus 87–102% on nominal tasks), e.g. JEV sends 38.8% of ANLI predictions to Neutral at 74.95% accuracy. Refining the scale from 2 to 14 levels makes utilization fall for every model; randomizing candidate order only weakens it. Useful measurement insight for anyone using small judge models for automatic evaluation; it also follows the site's earlier JEV coverage. Authors: Jilin University (per Hugging Face org) · First submitted: 2026-09-30 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/Glax147/jev_ordinal_scale_bia). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38827

- LANTERN: activations of a pretrained model rank unexplored OEIS relations — A classifier over model activations ranked 50 million pairs among 10,000 frequently referenced integer sequences; staged filtering, hypothesis generation and executable verification yielded 62 verified relations lacking an OEIS cross-reference, of which 13 were retained, 9 informative and 4 apparently entirely novel. A small but checkable example of models steering mathematical discovery rather than proving assigned theorems. Authors: institution not verified (authors include Tutubalina, Oseledets, Ignatov) · First submitted: 2026-09-26 · Artefacts: none listed. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.32264
