## Papers worth a deep dive

Goal Encoding in LLMs — The authors localise an encoding of a model's next sub-goal in the outputs of a sparse set of attention heads in a coding task. The same heads can be decoded into a natural-language statement of the model's next action, and transplanting the encoding into another programming language triggers the same operation realised with different tokens, so it is causal and plastic. Since a transformer without recurrence cannot carry state longer than its remaining layers, they propose that the model re-reads the goal from the task text, compares it with progress visible in the context and pursues what remains, and test this with targeted interventions. Direct evidence on how a model plans while it acts, on the cognitive side of interpretability. Authors: Bau lab project page (author list in arXiv metadata; affiliations not given there) · First submitted: 12 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: code and data (project page goals.baulab.info) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17297

A Closer Look at Language Model Introspection: Probing Information and Reporting — Tests whether models can "report" where their activations were perturbed by comparing against a plain linear probe. In OLMo 3 32B with an injection four times the typical activation norm, the probe reaches 98.3% accuracy while the model's own zero-shot answer stays at chance (11.1%); at the released base checkpoint probe and model reach 99.1% and 89.6%. Location is decodable even in randomly initialised networks, so successful "introspective" reporting can reflect information lying near the surface rather than broader access to the model's own computation. A cautionary result for self-knowledge claims. Authors: Rubin, Timmel, He et al. (affiliations not in metadata) · First submitted: 11 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15267

Pretraining Data Statistics Shape the Phases of Learning Entity Comparison in Language Models — On questions like "which of France and Brazil is larger?", small 163M-parameter models pass through three phases: choosing by entity frequency, then by position in the prompt (first or last mentioned), then by the correct answer. The authors show the phases are causally set by training-data statistics (switching a Zipfian entity distribution to a uniform one weakens the frequency phase and speeds learning), and the same three phases appear in OLMo-2 32B. A clean account of how shortcuts give way to knowledge during training. Authors: Chan, Huang, Elazar et al. · First submitted: 12 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated (NeurIPS 2026) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16882

ShadowBench: Exposing Lexical Anchoring and the Illusion of Forgetting in Large Language Models — Models know facts about an entity but retrieve them poorly without its name: removing explicit name tokens costs over 20% accuracy across scales, up to GPT-5.4 and Claude-Sonnet-4.6. Applied to unlearning (Gradient Difference, NPO), the new leakage metric shows that for popular entities the model reconstructs the "forgotten" entity in its reasoning trace in over 74% of cases, so current methods look like output filters rather than erasure. Tension between "unlearned" claims and measured behaviour. Authors: Maharjan, Liu (affiliations not in metadata) · First submitted: 11 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: benchmark introduced (release not confirmed) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15925

Privileged Information Does Not Guarantee Privileged Guidance: Auditing On-Policy Self-Distillation for Reasoning Models — Audits on-policy self-distillation on maths across three Qwen3 scales. The teacher with the worked solution does not reliably recognise errors, and problem-only teachers often match or beat privileged-solution teachers: the gain tracks the teacher's thinking mode, not the solution ("reasoning arbitrage"). Warns that accuracy gains alone do not show reasoning was transferred. Authors: Qiu, Agarwal (affiliations not in metadata) · First submitted: 11 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated (marked work in progress) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16064

Linking Rapid Learning to Compositional Structure in Neural Networks: Evidence from Additive Analogies in Meta-Learned Parameter Spaces — The "king minus man plus woman" trick applied to whole functions: parameter vectors of a meta-learned network after rapid adaptation can be added and subtracted according to symbolic relations among the functions, and the recombined vectors implement the intended new functions with no training on them. Gives a geometric account of how meta-learning builds in inductive bias, with a human-learning comparison in the framing. Authors: Tenenbaum, McCoy (affiliations not in metadata) · First submitted: 12 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17314

Classical Energies Learn to Reason 1,000x Faster than Looped Models — A learned version of the 1980s Hopfield-Tank energy (CRAM) solves 99.996% of Sudoku-Extreme and 100% of Maze-Unique after 42 ms and 132 ms of training on one GPU, at least 1,000x faster than looped reasoning models, and matches their best accuracy with at least 282x fewer inference FLOPs. The authors also caution that Sudoku and Maze are too easy to test the scalability of looped reasoning. Authors: Hoover, Blayney, Whittington et al. (affiliations not in metadata) · First submitted: 12 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: partly (reasoning as energy descent). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17309

Where Is Uncertainty Observed? A Mechanistic Understanding of Uncertainty Quantification in LLMs — Organises uncertainty-quantification methods by where in the computation they look (structural, representational, distributional, semantical lenses) and relates them through the residual stream, arguing the observation point decides what an estimator can recover. Useful frame for calibration and "knowing when it does not know". Authors: Liang, Ceccatelli, Eusebi et al. (affiliations not in metadata) · First submitted: 11 Nov 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15735

MuSeToM — 2,415-instance human-validated benchmark of whether LLMs can tell when perceptual evidence justifies another person's belief revision (theory of mind), tested with direct and chain-of-thought prompting. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16662
Analysis choices decide whether untrained networks beat trained ones at V1 — A multiverse analysis of model-brain RSA: under a pre-specified readout the untrained network scored below the trained one, and the sign of the V1 contrast depended on analysis choices; code released. A careful negative result on a widely repeated claim. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15533
Objects as Addresses: Instance Binding in Vision Transformer Attention — Across eight pretrained ViTs, object instances are separated in attention routing ("attention address") more than in token content; links to the cognitive-science notion of object binding. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17240
Symmetry in Time: A Representation Theory of Linear Recurrent Memory — Explains SSM state-tracking failures (diagonal SSMs track only commutative groups) and introduces HyperSSM with quaternion channels, exact on S5 and generalising to sequences 25x longer. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17162
The Value of Thinking: Learning When to Reason in Hybrid Decision Models — One frozen model either answers from a single forward pass or reasons for T tokens, routed by an estimated value of thinking; reports 72.5% lower latency on JevBench and 80.1% lower token cost on MiniWoB. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16961
What Roles Do System Prompts Really Assign? — Extracts and validates activation-level "occupational role vectors" for Qwen2.5-7B and Llama-3.1-8B, stable and separable, and tests steering by injecting them; code and data on Zenodo. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15989
Actor, Judge, or Teacher? Understanding Decision Models in Long-Horizon Agents — A poor actor can still teach well: judging actions after the episode lifts a Qwen2.5-1.5B policy from 72.8% to 82.9% on ALFWorld and 56.8% to 71.4% on WebShop over GRPO; code released.
