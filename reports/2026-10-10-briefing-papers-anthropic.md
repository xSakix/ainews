## Papers worth a deep dive

- Identifying Introspection From the Inside — Can a model's claims about itself be told apart from confabulation by looking at its weights and activations rather than its behaviour? The authors fine-tune LoRA adapters to make decisions for fictitious characters according to hidden linear preference functions. Accurate self-report of the learned preferences emerges without any self-report supervision, and the preference representations move to earlier layers as that happens. Attribution patching then shows that faithful models have much higher attribution similarity between the decision task and the self-report task. This is a mechanistic signature of faithful self-report that needs no understanding of the report's content, although it is shown only in a small, controlled setting. Authors: Northeastern University (David Bau's group; lead author David I. Atkinson) · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07186

- Learning to Learn a Language — A 300M-parameter byte-level transformer is trained only on synthetic sequences, each drawn from a randomly sampled recurrent causal model, so every training sequence is a new "language". With frozen weights it then learns real languages in context: next-byte loss on Wikipedia falls from about 8 bits per byte to 0.9-2.4 after a million bytes in English, Chinese, Hindi, Arabic, Japanese and Korean. It also learns to count, compare numbers and predict the primes in context, and compresses six non-text domains below gzip and PPMd. The authors' summary is "It has not learned a language. It has learned to learn one." This extends prior-fitted networks (TabPFN) from tables to sequences and is a clean test of in-context learning with no linguistic pretraining; it was also discussed on r/MachineLearning on 6 October. Authors: Lennart Carstens-Behrens and Holger Fröhlich (independent/academic, institution not stated in the metadata) · First submitted: 5 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and weights (github.com/cbl/prior-fitted-language-model, huggingface.co/lennartcb/pflm1) · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.05879

- BeliefScope: Diagnosing Evidence-Driven Revision and Pressure-Induced Shifts in Large Language Models — A black-box framework that separates two reasons a model changes its answer about a fixed claim: new relevant evidence versus directional user pressure that adds no fact. Across a 36-family Qwen/Llama study (plus Gemma3-12B checks), the profiles depend strongly on the evaluation context: model-level differences can vanish under matched controls, decoding or response interface, and reduced pressure-following can mean stable resistance or movement the opposite way. Useful for anyone measuring sycophancy or belief revision, because it says when a measured shift can be attributed to evidence or to pressure at all. Authors: Shuai Guo and Yidong Cui (institution not stated in the metadata) · First submitted: 8 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11305

- How High Is 0.6? Floors, Ceilings, and Headroom in Interpretability Probing — A probe score only means something against a floor (what simple inputs already predict) and a ceiling (what the full input predicts); the gap is the headroom in which a probe can show the model computes something extra. Tested on transformers trained for in-context meta-analysis, where both reference points are known, the model keeps a similar share of headroom under distribution shift even though error rises 12-15 times. The author then revisits four influential LLM probing claims (geography, Othello board state, truth, user demographics): against a text-only floor, some hold and others are largely explained by the text itself. A methodological correction that applies to a great deal of "the model represents X" work. Authors: Pranjal Garg (single author, institution not stated in the metadata) · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none found · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08544

- A Drosophila Whole-Connectome Network Can Learn Human-Designed Cognitive Tasks — The released MaleCNS v1.0 fruit-fly connectome is used as the fixed recurrent topology of an artificial network with one learned scalar per anatomical edge. On held-out addition it reaches 92.77% against 67.93% for degree-preserving rewired graphs, and on a grounded relational language task 61.59% against 44.17% for matched rewires; shuffling task-defined sensory features drops it from 60.94% to 19.27%. The claim is that real biological wiring is a reusable inductive bias beyond the behaviours it evolved for. Small tasks, but the controls (rewired and 21-graph comparisons) are the interesting part. Authors: Joonghui Cho, Minchan Kang, Daeshik Kim (institution not stated in the metadata) · First submitted: 7 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (github.com/joonghui0926/drosophila-connectome-cognitive-tasks) · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10014

- Learn2Play Bench: How Well Do LLM Agents Learn from Experience in Unfamiliar Environments? — Text games with novel or counterintuitive rules force agents to acquire knowledge by interaction rather than from pretraining. Three findings: keeping complete action-and-feedback records beats summarising experience into rules or strategies; top human players reach higher peaks, explore more varied strategies and repeat actions less; and changing the harness with the backbone fixed can raise scores while lowering cost. A direct measurement of agent learning, with human comparison. Authors: National University of Singapore · First submitted: 6 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and project site (github.com/liushiliushi/Learn2Play-Bench) · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08215

## Also notable

- RL-ARC: Calibrating Large Reasoning Models via Reasoning-guided Uncertainty — RLVR training degrades calibration and causes overconfidence; this method uses confidence in the reasoning as an auxiliary signal to regularise answer confidence, improving calibration in and out of distribution without a large loss in reasoning accuracy. (Authors: Gukhyeon Lee, SangKeun Lee; v1 8 Oct 2026; code none found; cognitive side: yes) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11352
- Now in Nature: Retrofitting language models to operate over bytes (Ai2, 7 Oct 2026) — The Bolmo "byteifying" method, which converts a subword model into a byte-level one with a short extra training run, is now published in Nature, with new checkpoints (Bwen 8B from Qwen 3 8B and Blama 8B from Llama 3 8B, which Ai2 says outperforms Bolmo 7B on its aggregate suite) and Stage 1 checkpoints on Hugging Face. Bolmo itself was introduced last December; the new part is the Nature paper and the cross-family checkpoints. (Artefacts: weights; cognitive side: no) Source: https://allenai.org/blog/bolmo-nature
- When Should Agents Think? Adaptive Reasoning via Cross-Turn Estimation (RACE) — Finds that the drop in likelihood of later reference actions when reasoning is removed signals whether earlier reasoning still supports them, and trains agents to reason only when needed; cuts reasoning cost on four agent benchmarks with equal or better performance. (v1 8 Oct 2026; code none found; cognitive side: partly) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12061
- Use and Disuse: Intent-Structured Experience Consolidation for Memory and Learning in LLM Agents — Hippocam, an agent memory architecture modelled on goal-relevant working memory and gradual consolidation: completed intents are compressed to outcomes and state, and unused history is recursively abstracted while originals remain recoverable. (v1 8 Oct 2026; code none found; cognitive side: yes; unrelated to the 4MT-VLM hippocampus-style benchmark covered on 6 Oct) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12124
- Self-Retrospection Distillation: Turning Post-hoc Experiences into Prior Foresight (Salesforce AI Research) — Distils what a finished trajectory reveals into the same policy's pre-interaction foresight, supplying a learning signal when all RLVR rollouts get the same reward (37-98% of groups in their runs); gains up to 24.2 points over 10 tool-use and long-horizon tasks. (v1 6 Oct 2026; code: github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/SRD) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08077
- Questioning the Questions: Sustaining Self-Evolution in Reasoning Models (R-Quest) — Explains why self-evolving reasoners collapse over rounds (invalid questions rise, and lexical diversity controls miss mathematically equivalent repeats) and fixes it with validity and novelty feedback; best average on 12 benchmarks. (v1 3 Oct 2026; code none found) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04299
