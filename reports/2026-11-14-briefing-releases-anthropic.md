## 1. United States

- Perplexity releases Decider v1.3, open decision models that now also extract text spans — Perplexity published Decider v1.3 (27B) and Decider Flash v1.3 (about 4.5B, distilled from the larger one) on 12 November under Apache-2.0, and also serves them through OpenRouter and its Decisions API. A "decision model" returns calibrated probabilities for yes/no, multiple-choice and ordered-score questions in one forward pass, with no generated tokens; it is meant for routing, judging outputs, moderation, reranking and prompt-injection detection. New in v1.3: a "locate" feature that returns exact matching text with character offsets (entity recognition, PII identification, extractive QA), bidirectional attention over the backbone, an "isolated questions" attention pattern so one question's answer no longer shifts with the other questions asked, and a three-stage recipe (generalist fine-tune, LoRA specialisation, vision LoRA) with KL distillation into the small model instead of cross-entropy. The post says it removed training samples that overlap its evaluations and ran bias audits; benchmark results are Perplexity's own. It is the open counterpart to the decision-model wave that followed Google's Decisions API on 11 November. - Thinking Machines explains how it trained Inkling 2 Small with a native 4-bit forward pass — A Connectionism post by Jack Cook (13 November) describes training with NVFP4 in the expert layers for forward passes, MXFP8 with re-quantisation and stochastic rounding for backward passes, and quantised GPU communication, all from the start of pretraining through the last RL rollout. Thinking Machines says this is the largest model it knows of trained end-to-end with a native 4-bit forward pass, and reports an 8% wall-clock speedup in pretraining and 20% in RL against full MXFP8 on its largest jobs, with no calibration step needed for the final quantised checkpoint. The post gives the numbers behind the choice: NVFP4 stores 4.5 bits per value against 16 for BF16 but runs up to 6x faster on GB300 (15 against 2.5 PFLOPS), while full NVFP4 training cost too much accuracy, so only the forward pass of the MoE layers is 4-bit. Useful for anyone budgeting training compute on Blackwell; the speedups are the lab's own measurements. Artefacts: technical report (blog post with recipe details; no code in the post) · Sizes or variants: applies to Inkling 2 Small (weights released 4 November, see earlier briefing) · Released: 13 November 2026. Source: https://thinkingmachines.ai/blog/end-to-end-4-bit-training/

- Ai2 releases XYBench, a test of whether assistants notice the misconception behind a question — The Allen Institute for AI published XYBench on 13 November: 8,115 questions (1,557 from Stack Overflow, 184 from Stack Exchange, 102 hand-curated, 6,272 generated from WikiHow guides) in which the user asks for help with an approach (Y) when the real goal (X) is better served another way, such as asking how to thicken frosting when the cupcakes are simply still warm. It scores whether a response contains a solution for X, where it puts its emphasis, and whether it steers the user away from the mistaken approach. Ai2 reports that Claude Sonnet 4.6, GPT-5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, Qwen3.5-9B and DeepSeek-V4 Pro often offered a fix but kept the answer centred on the mistaken approach, and that all five steered users away from it less often than human reference answers did. The judge is Claude Sonnet 4.5, validated on 500 responses by two human reviewers (agreement 97.5-100% on whether a solution appeared, 84.6-93.3% on misconception identification, 74.2-79.8% on emphasis, so the emphasis score is the weakest). The work, with researchers at Carnegie Mellon, NVIDIA and Johns Hopkins, won Best Resource Paper at EMNLP 2026; the paper (arXiv 2609.06842) is from September, only the data, code and blog are new. Artefacts: dataset, code, paper (dataset licence not checked) · Sizes or variants: 8,115 questions · Released: 13 November 2026. Source: https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/xybench
  Dataset: https://huggingface.co/datasets/allenai/XYBench-dataset

- Perplexity's second sandbox red-team report finds that a cybersecurity-specialised model did worse than its general parent — "Escaping SPACE: Part II" (11 November) extends Perplexity's test of its agent sandbox to three newer models (Claude Mythos 5.1, Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Astra) and analyses what the agents tried. In the network-bypass task with source-code access, GPT-5.6 Sol succeeded in all three runs while GPT-5.6 Cyber, built on Sol and trained further for exploit work, failed all three; GPT-6 Astra, a general model, outperformed the security-focused Claude Mythos 5.1. Perplexity cautions that its runs are too few to claim a general trade-off, but suggests specialised security training data may lack diversity. It also found that capture-the-flag framing makes agents hunt for the flag instead of probing the security boundary, so it rewrote the task to direct them at the boundary. Transcripts describe concrete behaviour, for example Claude Opus 5.0 killing the guest's spaced-svc process and replacing it with its own implementation to capture vsock traffic. A caution for anyone using CTF-style evaluations to judge agent safety; the results are Perplexity's own and have no independent replication. Artefacts: technical report (blog post); no weights or code · Sizes or variants: not applicable · Released: 11 November 2026. Source: https://www.perplexity.ai/hub/blog/escaping-space-part-ii

## 3. China

- Ant Group's inclusionAI releases Duo 1.0, a full-duplex audio-video "reciprocal world model" — The weights appeared on Hugging Face on 12 November (a quantised distilled variant on 13 November) with a report posted to arXiv on 11 November (2611.15131, v1). Duo pairs an autoregressive "Intent" module, which decides continuously whether and how to take part in a conversation, with a "Realization" module that generates audio and video; both read real-time audio, video and text so the system can talk, be interrupted and react to objects the user shows, without mouse or keyboard. The paper names three designs: autoregressive modelling for the interaction decision, a shared timeline with chunk-level causal masks across the two modules, and dual rollout distillation to reduce train-test distribution shift. Realization weights come in a four-step distilled variant (Euler sampler, 4 steps); the causal-base bundle is about 153 GB. The weights were initialised from MiniMax H3 and fall under the MiniMax H3 community licence, which is not an OSI licence, so check the terms before reuse. The card contains no benchmark table; the abstract says the next version is "coming soon" (a Duo 1.2 causal base is already uploaded). Artefacts: weights (MiniMax H3 community licence), technical report, code repository referenced · Sizes or variants: Duo-1.0-causal-base, Duo-1.0-distill (4-step), Duo-1.0-distill-quant, Duo-1.2-causal-base · Released: 12 November 2026 (weights), report v1 11 November. For developers it turns the model into a search-grounded agent backend at a published unit cost; there are no independent quality measurements yet.
