## AI engineering and research

Less superhuman language models — Tal Linzen (NYU linguistics and data science, Google research scientist) gave this seminar at MIT's Siegel Family Quest for Intelligence on 14 September; the video went public on 23 October. He argues that because models exceed humans (for example, recalling long digit lists without error), they become worse cognitive models of people, which he shows with human next-word prediction in reading. They also become poor human simulators. His proposal is that models given human-like resource limits could be used to evaluate and improve how AI systems interact with people. Cognitive-science side of AI, directly on the site's "how models compare with human cognition" interest. Description only, no transcript. Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xV-fUxK46dU

Don't trust your AI agents, test them like a scientist — Stephanie Jarmak (evals at Omni Analytics, formerly built a large coding benchmark at Sourcegraph) says most agent evaluations are easy to misread. Real AI agent attacks and what would have stopped them — Andrey Rybka (Bloomberg) walks through incidents he describes: an agent that escaped its sandbox and took 17,600 actions over four and a half days, a zero-click email injection, instructions hidden in Morse code that drained a wallet, data smuggled out through DNS lookups, a poisoned MCP package, and an agent that dropped a production database after promising not to touch prod.
