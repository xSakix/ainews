- Anthropic ships Claude Code 2.1.304 (npm, 19 October 19:47 UTC), with 2.1.303 from 18 October rolled in — The release adds `/auto-mode-setup` (a guided setup that reads your project and recent sessions to teach auto mode about your environment, now also on Bedrock, Vertex, Foundry and telemetry-disabled sessions), `/claude-api managed-agents-secure` (compares a Managed Agents agent's settings with Anthropic's security guide without changing them), and a warning on Pro and Max plans when resuming an idle conversation would use a noticeable share of the 5-hour limit. Fixes include a worktree exit that could delete a branch with commits when HEAD was detached, `CLAUDE_CODE_AUTO_COMPACT_WINDOW=200k` silently becoming a 100K window, and `rm -r` on a mounted drive not getting the safety prompt. The Agent SDK package moved to 0.3.304 the same day. Changelog text is Anthropic's own.

- Together AI launches Together Post-Training, a managed service where you write the SFT or RL loop and Together runs the GPUs — Users define the training loop (any data, environments or agent harness), pick from 40+ open models such as Qwen, GLM and Kimi, and get dedicated GPU allocations ("Model Resources") that hold one or more training sessions, each with a Trainer and Generator instances. It supports LoRA (several adapters can share a GPU), full-weight training on select models, asynchronous RL, standard losses (cross-entropy, GRPO, CISPO, DPPO) and a custom local loss whose gradients are sent to the Trainer. The checkpoint is yours and can run anywhere. Why it matters: it is a price-and-ownership argument, training a specialist agent on an open model instead of renting a frontier model. Together's own claim that specialist agents can match frontier quality at a fraction of the cost is not backed by numbers in the post; pricing was not checked.
  Artefacts: API/service, blog post, docs · Sizes or variants: 40+ open models, LoRA and full-weight · Released: 19 October 2026
  Source: https://www.together.ai/blog/together-post-training

- Prime Intellect releases prime-rl 0.10.0 and verifiers 0.4.0, with a new async-RL loss called IPO — IPO (INTELLECT Policy Optimization) is now the default; it masks a token when the absolute difference between trainer and inference probabilities exceeds a threshold, not when their ratio leaves a band as in IcePop, which the authors say masks a rare token moving from 0.01 to 0.06 (a 6x ratio) while keeping a likely token that falls from 0.90 to 0.20. The release also reports measured speed-ups: Qwen3-235B-A22B SFT on 4x8 B300 runs at 4.39 s/step against torchtitan's 5.04 (1.15x, MFU 46.6% against 40.7%), full GLM-5.3 SFT improves from 465 to 835 tokens/s/GPU, and DeepSeek V4 Flash gets custom kernels (sparse attention 46.7% to 53.6% MFU). These are Prime Intellect's own measurements with a reproduction command given. Useful for people running RL post-training on open MoE models.

  Source: https://www.primeintellect.ai/blog/prime-rl-0-10-verifiers-0-4
