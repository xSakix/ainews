## Papers worth a deep dive

- Task context rewires what the same attention heads do — Bassett's group takes a brain-network view of language models: the same attention heads are reused across tasks, but which heads matter and what they influence shifts with the task, measured with interventions on five open-weight models (1.5B to 14B). In Gemma-2-9B and Qwen3-14B a single edit to an early activation vector switches which graph fact the model reads while the written instruction stays fixed, and the authors build linear response maps before and after the edit. It is a direct interpretability result on how a model reconfigures itself mid-computation, framed with neuroscience. Authors: University of Pennsylvania (Mohapatra, Bassett) · First submitted: 2026-10-09 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13895

- AI models can provably hide arbitrary capabilities — The authors embed an encrypted neural network inside a host model's weights using digital lockers. The hidden circuit decrypts and runs only on a secret trigger, and under standard cryptographic assumptions it stays hard to elicit or interpret even with full weight access. A proof-of-concept on chemical reaction prediction (a stand-in for CBRN-relevant skill) resisted fine-tuning, weight noising, gradient-based trigger search and latent adversarial training. The point for practitioners is a stated limit of elicitation-based capability audits. It is also on Hacker News today. Authors: Draguns, Motwani, Douglas, Schroeder de Witt (Oxford-affiliated authors) · First submitted: 2026-10-09 · Artefacts: code and models released per the abstract · Cognitive side: no Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13928

- Finding a subliminally transferred trait without knowing the trait — When a student is distilled from a teacher with the same base weights it can inherit the teacher's traits, including misalignment, from semantically unrelated data. The earlier defences needed the trait to be known in advance. This paper proves the trait can be recovered from the parameter update alone, because it shows up consistently across many teacher-generated datasets while task skills vary. The result is a weight-space direction you can amplify to read the trait, or project out to suppress it. On the published animal-preference benchmarks it recovers the traits with no labels and little loss in task performance. Authors: Egan, Gilligan-Lee, O'Riordan, Zhu · First submitted: 2026-10-09 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13536

- UPTURN: redirect a failing chain of thought by adding a new user turn — A 130K-parameter MLP head on a frozen encoder learns where in a failed rollout to intervene, trained only on final-answer correctness. The surprising part is how the correction is delivered: inline injected instructions do not reliably change the model's course, but the same instruction presented as a new user turn does. With no fine-tuning of the reasoner, accuracy improves in every setting across four reasoners and six math benchmarks, and it beats self-correction and reflection-token baselines at matched budget. It is a prompting and context technique the reader can try. Authors: Jeon, Cho, Kim, Sung (Korean group) · First submitted: 2026-10-09 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (self-correction) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13672

- Vision transformers learn word meaning from images alone — Two large DINOv3 models, trained with self-supervision on images only and no captions, organise words rendered as images by meaning. Brand names cluster by industry, nouns by semantic domain, and country-capital pairs share consistent vector offsets, as in text embeddings, with spelling similarity controlled for. The representations also line up with those of the visual categories the words name. It is a clean test of how knowledge about language can emerge without language supervision. Authors: DTU and collaborators (Gjølbye, Mason, Frank, Hansen) · First submitted: 2026-10-09 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13252

- Can language models learn when to abstain? — On Qwen3-14B and factual questions, a separately learned answer selector beats the model's own calibrated confidence filter: on TriviaQA coverage rises from 20.2% to 30.9% while the error rate among answered questions falls from 23.0% to 14.5%. The tested reinforcement-learning changes to the model itself give no consistent gain, and on NQ-Open the selector still does worse than always abstaining. It is a careful, partly negative result on metacognition: knowing when not to answer is trainable in one dataset and does not carry over reliably. Authors: Terven, Córdova-Esparza, Gomez et al. (Mexican institutions) · First submitted: 2026-10-09 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (calibration) Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13821

- Station: can agents do open-ended science? — Multiple agents in an open-world environment simulating a scientific ecosystem are given the research question of three ICLR oral papers, with the results withheld and web access disabled. Station rediscovers 62.7% of the original findings on average, against 15.4% for Codex Multiagent-v2 and 14.4 to 20.6% for AI Scientist-v2. The gain is credited to a Supervisor mechanism and periodic Meta Reflection that keep agents exploring when no intermediate metric exists. Authors: University of Hong Kong (Du, Chung) · First submitted: 2026-10-06 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/dualverse-ai/station-open-reseach) · Cognitive side: no Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08927
