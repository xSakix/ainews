## 1. United States

- OpenAI: "Perplexity and Intelligence" — chief scientist Jakub Pachocki publishes OpenAI's internal pretraining loss figures and argues that perplexity (cross-entropy on a held-out set) should be a shared metric across labs and the basis for industry limits on further development. The numbers: log-perplexity of about 0.99 for GPT-3.5, about 0.91 for GPT-4, below 0.8 now, and about 0.76 for the model that began producing "groundbreaking" results on open mathematics problems; he expects new capabilities in the 0.68-0.7 window and says AI-driven pretraining research could cause an "avalanche" of advances as soon as 2027. He proposes a first development limit tied to a measurable milestone, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. Caveats stated in the post: the figures come from OpenAI's own dataset and cannot be compared across labs, perplexity does not track tasks directly, and chain-of-thought reasoning raises capability without lowering perplexity. The figures are OpenAI's own and unaudited; the post names no model for the 0.76 value. Why it matters: it is a frontier lab publishing a number about its own pace and asking competitors to do the same, which bears on who gets to set safety pacing.
