## 2. Policy and regulation

- 🚨 **US Homeland Security bans DeepSeek and anything built with its models from civilian federal agencies, in a notice due in the Federal Register on 20 October that shows the order was signed on 26 June** — CyberScoop reports Secretary Markwayne Mullin's letter cites the Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Act and a Federal Acquisition Security Council review, naming data stored on servers in China, collection of personal data and "digital fingerprints", and the risk that prompts and responses reach the Chinese government. Agencies must remove existing installations and may ask for exceptions only 60 days ahead with a "compelling justification"; the Defense Department is outside the order, and the ban covers DeepSeek alone though the article notes other Chinese open-weight models raise similar worries. BBC adds that Snowflake, Vercel and OpenRouter use DeepSeek models in their offerings, and says the ban took effect at the end of July, which differs from the June date in the letter reported by CyberScoop. Matters to anyone building for US government customers on open-weight models. Source: https://cyberscoop.com/dhs-bans-deepseek-federal-agencies/ (also https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cjkgeq0k1wevo )

Michael is the official who led the Defense Department's push to label Anthropic a supply-chain risk after it refused to drop safety guardrails, and he is now one of four administration officials running the task force, tasked with protocols for cyber breaches and other critical incidents. - **EU: the first harmonised standard supporting the AI Act, EN 18286:2026 on quality management systems, is published in the Official Journal, giving a presumption of conformity with Article 17** — The Commission's AI page says Implementing Decision (EU) 2026/2274 references the CEN and CENELEC standard, which lets providers of high-risk AI systems show compliance by following it, builds on ISO/IEC 42001 and ISO 9001 rather than replacing them, and is the only AI quality-management standard with that presumption; more standards are to follow. A procedural step, but it is the first concrete compliance route ahead of the high-risk requirements. Source: https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/first-harmonised-standard-supporting-ai-act-published-official-journal

## 3. Hardware and infrastructure

- **Amazon releases TorchNeuron, an Apache 2.0 PyTorch backend that runs existing code on its Trainium2 and Trainium3 chips by changing one device setting; public beta opens 20 October** — The Next Web, drawing on Amazon's PyTorch blog post, says Amazon showed the same training script reaching the same learning rate on a standard chip and on Trainium, that the first release lacks features such as dynamic input shapes and skips about 22,000 PyTorch tests, and that early users include Cartesia, Deepgram and the Lieber Institute. TechCrunch frames it as a direct attack on Nvidia's CUDA advantage, quoting Amazon's claim that about 85% of AI apps are written in PyTorch and an AWS executive arguing startups should not bet on single-purpose neoclouds. Amazon says its custom-chip business has passed a $25 billion annual run rate (its own figure). Matters to anyone weighing a move off Nvidia hardware: the porting cost is the claim to test.
