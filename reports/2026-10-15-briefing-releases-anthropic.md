## 1. United States

- Ai2 releases HiRO-ACEv2, an AI emulator that produces global 3 km rain, wind and pressure fields at about 1/300th the cost of the physics model it imitates — The system pairs ACE, a 100 km climate emulator, with HiROv2, a diffusion model that sharpens its output to 3 km. Version 2 predicts precipitation, 10 m winds and sea level pressure together instead of rainfall alone, and Ai2 says it matches the reference model X-SHiELD's distributions out to the 99.9999th percentile (within 3% on the rarest events when fed coarsened X-SHiELD data) and reproduces tropical cyclone energy, including storms not seen in training. Stated limits: it only works with the ACE variant tuned to X-SHiELD, so it cannot yet produce data for warmer climates or the ERA5 historical record, and winds and pressure around hurricane eyes are somewhat lopsided. All figures are Ai2's own. Not a language model, but a concrete open generative model and paper from a lab the site follows. - Microsoft publishes UserRepGen-4B, a small model that turns a conversation into a description of the user's task and behaviour for a user simulator — A LoRA fine-tune (rank 64) of Qwen3.5-4B that reads a multi-turn user-assistant dialogue and outputs JSON with a `task` and a `user_behavior` field (communication style, how constraints are disclosed, feedback, when the user stops). It does not simulate user turns itself; it conditions a separate simulator, unlike Microsoft's earlier UserLM-8b and UserLM-2.0. Training used online supervised fine-tuning with hindsight distillation, comparing simulated and original user behaviour, and inference needs no rollouts. Of interest for anyone building agent evaluations with simulated users. The card gives no benchmark numbers in the part I read. Artefacts: weights (MIT; base-model licence file included), model card, example script · Sizes or variants: 4.5B parameters in BF16, text-only English, 9,216-token training sequences · Released: Hugging Face repository created 14 October 2026 20:30 UTC. ## 3. China

- 🚨 Ant Group's inclusionAI opens Ling-3.1-flash, a 560B-parameter hybrid-linear mixture-of-experts reasoning model with 1M-token context, under the MIT licence — The model has 25B active parameters per token, 512 routed experts (8 active) plus one shared, and a 72-layer stack of 63 Kimi-Delta-Attention linear-attention layers and 9 gated multi-head latent attention layers (7:1). Weights are stored mostly in FP8 (about 571B FP8 parameters in 54 shards). Day-0 serving recipes exist for SGLang (with multi-token-prediction speculative decoding, 8x H200 or 4x GB300) and vLLM. inclusionAI says it improves on Ling-3.0-flash for agents, search, office work and software engineering and cites Terminal-Bench 4.0, SWE-bench Pro, FrontierSWE, GDPVal-AA V2.1, MCP-Atlas and SkillsBench; the scores are in images on the model card and are the lab's own, so I could not check numbers. It also describes LingCode, a long-horizon coding harness built on the open-source agent Pi, which is not released (open-sourcing "under consideration"). The model is also on OpenRouter. The repository was created 13 October and the card and serving instructions were finalised through 14 October, so the public release date is uncertain within that range.
