## Papers worth a deep dive

Understanding the limits of latent world models — Latent world models are pitched as the way agents will plan over long horizons. The authors define a "faithful" latent world model (the learned latent dynamics must match the environment's transition map) and a metric, latent rollout consistency, then find that none of the state-of-the-art latent world models they test solves its task by faithfully modelling the environment's dynamics. Task scores hide the gap, which matters to anyone reading world-model results as evidence of understanding. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 27 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (what a model's internal "world" actually represents). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37588

Sensorimotor behaviour from an untrained brain-and-nerve-cord connectome — The adult fly connectome (172,433 neurons) is run as a spiking network with untuned parameters inside a simulated body. It steers around obstacles to 95% of odour sources; a rewiring that keeps each neuron's connection counts and signs reaches 18%, and replayed leg commands without senses reach almost none. A dopamine-gated change at the mushroom body flips odour approach into avoidance. Wiring alone carries a behaviour and its learned reversal, with the failures marking what the map leaves out. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 24 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated (42-page manuscript with supplement) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33881

Verifiable Efficiency Limits of Innate LLM Planning in Sliding Puzzles — Isolates unaided planning: seven model conditions write whole plans for 4x4 sliding puzzles in one response, with no tools or execution feedback (8,400 outputs). Success-weighted plan efficiency is split into execution reliability, coordination among legal plans and action economy; the tension is that models completing every trial still get less economical as the horizon grows, and efficient completions can coexist with poor goal attainment. Trajectory replay and certified optima make the scores checkable. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (planning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36415

Reinforcement Learning Done Right for Explicit Confidence Targets (RLEC) — A user writes a target accuracy p in the prompt and the model decides whether to answer or abstain so that answered questions reach at least p. Earlier attempts could not tell targets apart because the distinguishing reward signal vanished and the error penalty blew up as p approached 1; RLEC fixes both with a stable reward family and reward scheduling, giving, the authors say, the first single LLM that follows different confidence targets without a post-hoc threshold. A concrete take on behavioural calibration. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (calibration and self-knowledge). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35059

What Survives Distillation? A Mechanistic Interpretability Study of GPT-2 and DistilGPT-2 — Compares teacher and six-layer student on two known circuits using patching, logit lens, CKA and sparse autoencoder statistics. The indirect-object circuit collapses entirely (35.2% accuracy vs 94.8%, no name-mover heads preserved) while the greater-than circuit survives at 87.7% recovery by remapping a layer-9 head into layer 4. Aggregate metrics hide which mechanisms distillation kept. Small model, but a clean negative result. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 27 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (interpretability). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37157

Cross-modal Steering Vectors Exist Across Both Image and Text Generation — In unified multimodal models, a steering vector found on text tokens (concepts such as emotion or object size) steers image generation zero-shot, and the reverse, so the concept is not implemented separately per modality. Steering is causal, so this goes beyond representational similarity. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 26 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code, data and demo linked from the arXiv comment (github.com/saintsauceee/cross-modal-steering) · Cognitive side: yes (shared concept representation). GEPA-Curator: Curated Learning for Self Improving Agents — A prompt-optimisation technique the reader could try. Reflective optimisers such as GEPA learn from every failed rollout, but failures can come from noisy judges, harness limits or randomness, not the prompt. GEPA-Curator filters out tasks with unreliable signal and feeds past successful trajectories as context; on GoBrowse, ALFWorld, AppWorld and tau-bench it gains up to 20.7% over GEPA and 21.2% over ACE, with Gemini 3.1 Pro and Claude Opus 5 as optimisers. Authors: not stated in the abstract · First submitted: 27 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no (prompting/context technique). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37651

Rare, Relevant, and Still Wrong: How Memory Fails LLM Group Decisions — When a multi-agent discussion is compressed into a memory for a final decision maker, keeping the rarest claims or the claims relevant to the criteria both fail on hidden-profile tasks. The CrossProfile benchmark (60 templates, 480 tasks with verified keys) shows a perfect relevance filter readmits decoys and loses 23 to 31 points to rarest-first selection in the classic configuration, tested on Qwen2.5-7B, Llama-3.1-8B and two DeepSeek models.
