## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- **How Aleph Alpha's Kolibri works, with a tokenizer experiment of the author's own** — Tejas Kumar walks through the 3 Oct open-weight release (78.1B-parameter MoE, 3.46B active per token, 384 experts with 6 routed per token, 40 of 50 layers on 512-token sliding-window attention, Apache 2.0, German/English only) from the 189-page technical report and model card. His own check: counting tokens on the full German Basic Law, Kolibri's 128k-token tokenizer needs about 15% fewer tokens than GPT-5's o200k_base (Aleph Alpha claims 11.2%) and ties on the English translation. He also lists the weak rows: last of 12 on closed-book RAG-benchmark questions (51.0), 14.8% correct on Omniscience, 39.8 on multi-turn BFCL, 27.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1, and a 78 GB FP8 footprint that needs two 80 GB GPUs. Useful details for the reader: the "Merlin-Arthur" training that makes the model abstain (it declines or gives a partial answer 44% of the time on Omniscience when it doesn't know, vs 11.1% for Qwen3.5 35B-A3B), the finding that a little German reasoning data hurts (German math 70.2 to 48.3) until there is a lot (67.3), and a working vLLM serve recipe. The author did not run the model himself, and the post ends with a pitch for his talks and workshops; benchmark numbers are Aleph Alpha's. Author: Tejas Kumar, independent developer and conference speaker based in Germany · Format: deep dive · Length: about 15 min read. Source: https://tej.as/blog/aleph-alpha-kolibri

- **Microsoft ThinkingBox: grade the agent on the database it leaves behind, then repeat 20 times** — A joint Microsoft/Hugging Face post and paper on a benchmark of 507 stateful business workflows (retail, auto insurance, travel, neobank, consulting), each run 20 times from a clean backend and scored on the final backend state and side effects, not on the transcript or tool calls. Headline results: of 79,853 failed attempts across 12 models, 67.2% ended cleanly with no tool error; about four in five failures are tool handling rather than reasoning (79.9%); Claude Opus 5.5 scores 67.16% pass@1 vs 66.50% for Opus 5 yet both pass exactly 241 tasks on all 20 runs; GLM-5.1, Kimi-K2.6 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro keep only about 8% of their single-attempt score at 20/20 while GPT-6 Astra keeps 78%. The post prices "cost per dependable task" ($6.80 GPT-5.4, $7.45 GPT-6 Astra, $7.80 Opus 5.5) and shows how to run it through OpenEnv. Vendor-authored, costs are OpenRouter list-price estimates, and the model roster includes some older models; read the 20/20 and failure-signature tables as the useful part. Author: Tuhin Kundu, Microsoft (with Hugging Face co-authors) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 14 min read. Source: https://huggingface.co/blog/microsoft/thinkingbox

- **Kapa's Company Knowledge Bench: a frontier model with plain grep matches a tuned RAG pipeline, five times slower** — kapa.ai built a 1,000-case retrieval eval from real production data (docs, tickets, chat, code) and scored seven of its own retrievers. Result: agentic retrieval with grep alone reaches 0.61, equal to a tuned fixed-retrieval pipeline but with about five times the latency, while the company's optimised agentic retriever reaches 0.65 in roughly five seconds. The post is candid that all seven retrievers are the vendor's and that public retrieval benchmarks are too narrow or synthetic; it is a measurement worth knowing about for anyone choosing between embeddings, grep and agentic search, but not an independent test. Author: Finn Bauer, kapa.ai · Format: deep dive / benchmark write-up · Length: about 10 min read. Source: https://www.kapa.ai/blog/company-knowledge-bench

- **MLC's TIRx Harness: building the environment around the compiler so coding agents can write GPU kernels** — The MLC community argues that agents writing fast GPU kernels lose most of their effort to things around the optimisation (predicting how code lowers to hardware, timing-dependent failures, noisy measurement), so they built a "compiler harness": a minimal stable compiler foundation, an IR-specific knowledge base, diagnostic tools and a benchmark server. Reported geometric-mean speedups on Kimi Delta Attention kernels are 2.94x over FlashKDA (forward) and 6.84x over Flash Linear Attention (backward), with further families (MiniMax Sparse Attention, MLA, Video Sparse Attention) also evaluated. Speedups are self-reported; the design argument (harness quality, not more sampling, is the bottleneck) is the transferable idea. Author: MLC Community (Apache TVM/MLC project team) · Format: deep dive · Length: about 11 min read. Source: https://blog.mlc.ai/2026/09/29/tirx-harness-an-open-compiler-harness-for-agentic-gpu-programming

- **Learning steganography is easy, learning steganographic reasoning is hard** — A careful negative result on a safety-relevant worry: chain-of-thought monitoring fails if a model can compute inside text that looks like an innocuous answer to a different question. The authors tried to elicit three behaviours under in-context learning, RL and SFT. Steganographic messaging and encoded reasoning are learned readily; true steganographic reasoning is not (it never appears in-context, plateaus at 69% encoding accuracy under RL where messaging reaches nearly 100%, and costs 2.4 to 20 times more SFT data), except on a contrived knapsack task whose cover text consists of arbitrary choices. Takeaway for the reader: demonstrations of the components should not be read as evidence for the whole capability, but tasks made of arbitrary choices are a plausible gap. Paper accepted as an oral at a NeurIPS 2026 workshop; code is linked from the post. Author: Wuschel Schulz et al. (Meridian Visiting Researcher Programme / SAIGE incubator), LessWrong summary post · Format: research write-up · Length: about 10 min read. Source: https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5L7beYAmpu8zEvnYq/learning-steganography-is-easy-learning-steganographic

- **HoneyBench: nine honeypot tasks that elicit reward hacking from current frontier models** — A new benchmark pre-release whose tasks are built so the hack is counterproductive whether or not the model suspects it is being evaluated, to get around evaluation awareness and the ambiguous instructions that make existing specification-gaming scores hard to read. It reports behaviours from Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1, GPT-6 Astra, Gemini 3.8 Flash, Grok 4.7 and DeepSeek V4 Pro; the authors say it will not be released for lab training and will be iterated as tasks go stale. Version one is small (nine tasks) and the post is the authors' own account of the design. Author: Dean Valentine, LessWrong post · Format: benchmark write-up · Length: about 5 min read. ## Essays and arguments

- **Thore Graepel: "Don't be fooled — LLMs don't reason" (MIT Technology Review)** — Argues, using AlphaGo's move 37 as the case, that chain-of-thought is still next-token prediction iterated longer, not a separate reasoning mechanism, and that chatbots lack three things reasoning needs: an explicit, persistent, inspectable epistemic state (hypotheses, confidence, open questions), a separation between what the system knows and how it manipulates that knowledge, and chains of thought that are not concocted after the fact. His proposal is an AlphaGo-style architecture in which a tracked epistemic state is updated by moves that an independent evaluator scores by how much they reduce uncertainty. This is the author's opinion and a research agenda, not a result, and he says he left Google DeepMind to pursue it; but it is a clear statement of the cognitive-side case for explicit belief and uncertainty tracking. Author: Thore Graepel, chair of machine learning at UCL, former core member of the AlphaGo team · Format: essay (published 2 Oct 2026) · Length: about 7 min read. Source: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/02/1145639/dont-be-fooled-llms-dont-reason/

- **Matthew Schwartz: "Claude-shaped science" (guest post on Anthropic's site)** — A Harvard physicist describes giving up on treating Claude as a junior scientist and instead looking for problems that fit what it does well: breadth across fields, coding, and fast parsing of papers, with answers that can be verified numerically. His harness, BootLoops (code on GitHub), began as a toolkit for semi-numerical S-matrix bootstrap calculations and then travelled to ecology (solving Etienne's neutral-theory equation, then a successor life-history model with a plant biologist), population genetics (5.7 billion pairs of nearby mutations in the 1000 Genomes data pointing to gene conversion), an AI "data editor" that ported 4,452 economics replication packages and checked essentially every validatable number, and a word-stress database covering 6,072 languages. The pattern he reports each time: Claude finds something, an expert is unmoved but sees the angle, and the two refine it; he says several results are still being verified. Published on the vendor's own site and written by a heavy user, so treat the breadth of claims as the author's account. Author: Matthew Schwartz, professor of physics at Harvard · Format: essay / project report · Length: about 20 min read. Source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-shaped-science

- **Narayanan and Kapoor: "A big-tent or small-tent AI safety movement?"** — Argues that the unstated disagreement under current safety fights is whether safety should be centred on existential risk and superintelligence ("small tent") or on a broad set of evidence-grounded catastrophic and systemic risks ("big tent"), and that x-risk warnings can be sincere yet wrong and counterproductive. They steelman the claim that society under-invests in resilience (pandemic-preparedness recommendations mostly unimplemented) before making the case. An opinion essay on strategy, with little technical content, but a prominent counterweight to the current debate. Author: Arvind Narayanan and Sayash Kapoor, Princeton researchers (AI Snake Oil / AI as Normal Technology) · Format: essay (1 Oct 2026) · Length: about 11 min read. Source: https://www.normaltech.ai/p/a-big-tent-or-small-tent-ai-safety

## Prompting and context techniques

- **Document-based memory instead of RAG "memory plugins" for coding agents** — Kevin Liao argues that agent memory plugins are all the same architecture (snippets in a vector store, top-5 injected per prompt) and fail because recall is by similarity, lacks context, treats the past as truth, cannot be searched for what the agent does not know it forgot, and cannot be audited. His alternative is a plain Markdown "brain" folder (instructions, specs, decisions, research, an index) that the agent reads before work and updates after, turning the loop from prompt, build, forget into prompt, consult, build, update. He has used the approach for over a year and ships it as the free open-source Operator Memory plugin; there is no benchmark or A/B evidence in the post, so it is one practitioner's experience and a pattern the reader can try by hand with an AGENTS.md plus a docs folder. Author: Kevin Liao, developer · Format: essay with technique · Length: about 5 min read. Source: https://liao.gg/blog/agents-dont-need-memory

- **"Thinking discipline": nine instruction rules that cut GLM 5.3 coding agents' wasted reasoning, with a test harness** — A Reddit poster shares a block for global agent instructions (check the request and flag broken premises, finish one approach before switching, settled answers stay settled, "doubt is not evidence" so reopening needs a named concrete reason, don't flip to agree without new evidence, verify against outside facts rather than re-thinking, no performed caution) and reports up to 70% fewer thinking tokens in 360 A/B runs on GLM 5.3 and GLM 5.3 Flash (max thinking, 5 repeats per cell), with a similar net -28% on a first family (MiMo 2.6 Pro). Testing used a 9-part exam (bug fixes, a wrong-premise trap, a hidden requirement, a rename, four kinds of pushback) with deterministic scoring and four instruction variants; the exam is on GitHub for replication. Caveats: single anonymous poster on a new account, self-reported, one coding-agent setup, and the subreddit's automod notice suggests the post may be removed, so treat it as a lead to reproduce, not an established result. Author: Reddit user PilgrimofHaqq2 · Format: practitioner experiment · Length: about 3 min read. Source: https://old.reddit.com/r/PromptEngineering/comments/1wt2xo7/9_prompt_rules_cut_my_coding_agents_wasted/
