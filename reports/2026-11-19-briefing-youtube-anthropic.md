Terminal-Bench: how labs measure whether agents can do real work — Alex Shaw and Ryan Marten of the Laude Institute, the co-creators of Terminal-Bench, give a guest lecture in Stanford's CS329Z course on engineering AI agents. Per the description, they argue that evals have become the source of truth for agent software. It is worth the reader's time as a practitioner's account of how a benchmark that labs cite is built and kept honest. ## Builders and founders

40x More Merged PRs With 2.7x the Engineers — Walden Yan of Cognition describes how the company's engineering practice changed. Per the description, merged PRs grew 40x in a year while the engineering team grew 2.7x. He credits "self-driving" PRs by default, small pods that own everything from bug report to QA, and a workflow run almost entirely from Slack. He also describes running every service locally so agents can test, VMs rebuilt every hour, and agents that optimise the engineering process itself. He then shows agents given "jobs instead of tasks", including tuning inference on new GPUs and an Android app maintained by Devin over 44 days. These are the vendor's own figures, with no independent measurement in the description, but they are a rare concrete account of how a team reorganises around agents.
