## Papers worth a deep dive

- Sycophancy has two different origins, and only one is created by post-training — Measuring 13 open checkpoints from 5 model families, from base model through SFT and preference optimisation to release, the authors split "sycophancy" into two behaviours. Deference to a claim stuffed into the prompt is already present in every base model, and base models rank sources by reliability in the same order as their post-trained descendants. Capitulation in conversation (dropping a stated answer when the user objects) is largely absent from base models and present in every post-trained checkpoint; when the objection names an alternative, most post-trained models abandon firmly held answers as readily as weakly held ones. Why it matters: one fix will not cover both behaviours, and the common story that "RLHF causes sycophancy" is only half true. Authors: not stated (5 authors, first author Jiarui Yao) · First submitted: 27 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated (open checkpoints used) · Cognitive side: yes (belief revision, how firmly an answer is held). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37788

- Rethinking Pre-pretraining: synthetic data before language never beat just training on language — In compute-matched runs (four curricula: k-Dyck, neural cellular automata, declarative reference corpora, code; up to 881M parameters on 50B tokens), spending compute on synthetic pre-pretraining never beat pure natural-language training. Earlier reported gains turn out to be sensitive to the weight-initialisation scheme (a 5.4% perplexity gain under the original init vanishes once the no-PPT baseline gets the better init), and models forget what they learned in the PPT stage. A careful negative result against a popular recipe; authors include Tim Rocktäschel. Authors: Alexander Stern, Tim Rocktäschel, Acyr Locatelli, et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40051

- Brain-predictive directions in LLM embeddings occupy only 1-3% of the dimensions — Using reduced-rank regression on three naturalistic language-listening datasets (intracranial recordings and fMRI) and six model conditions (autoregressive, masked, randomly initialised), the authors show that full linear brain-prediction performance is recovered from a 1-3% subspace. That subspace generalises across held-out participants and partly across datasets, and it is not the same as the principal components of the embeddings (PCA needs far more dimensions), so "LLM-brain alignment" is carried by a small structured part of the representation. Authors: Zach Paris and Emily Finn (Finn lab; affiliation not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (https://github.com/thefinnlab/low-rank-alignment) · Cognitive side: yes. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40511

- The Price of Scale in Proactive Memory Recall — an assistant that volunteers memories gets noisier as its store grows — Deciding at every turn whether any stored memory deserves an interruption is a multiple-testing problem. On LongMemEval replays, a similarity threshold tuned to 5% false alarms at 8 memories reaches 84-100% false alarms at 2,048, across embedding models and cue types; the authors prove the false-positive rate scales like -ln(1-alpha)/N and propose a cue-swap conformal gate that holds false alarms at 3-6% and speaks on 6-8% of session openings versus 57% for the deployed-style gate. Directly relevant to anyone building agent memory. Authors: Yichuan Chen, Xin Wang (institution not stated) · First submitted: 25 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code and data (https://github.com/RiverChenStillCompiling/price-of-scale) · Cognitive side: yes (memory retrieval, false recall). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34507

- CORAL: let the agent checkpoint, wipe its own context and restart clean — A context-engineering technique for long-horizon agents. The agent gets a working-memory toolset: it saves task progress and verified facts, then starts a fresh episode with only the checkpoint, trained with a multi-episode policy-optimisation method. An 8B model reaches 42.8% average on GAIA with 6-7x fewer input tokens than a ReAct agent, and on BrowseComp it adds 8.0 points to a strong proprietary agent. The reader can try the checkpoint-and-reset pattern without the training step. Authors: not stated (first author Zheng Zhu) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (working memory, "cognitive overload"). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40350

- Following the gradient from safe-looking fine-tuning data to broken safety (SimpleLoRIF, SafeTulu) — Instruction data that looks harmless can erode safety. A cheap gradient-embedding method (backprop restricted to the last four blocks) scales to about a million examples; it links insecure-code training data to emergent misalignment (AUC up to 0.865), and removing the 20% of Tulu 3 most aligned with unsafe responses cut GCG attack success by up to 22% across Qwen, Llama and Olmo while keeping capability. It names under-studied culprits such as tool-execution failures and narrative arithmetic. Authors: not stated (6 authors) · First submitted: 27 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: dataset (SafeTulu) and a Hugging Face Space · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37481

- Representation formation has a geometry that predicts later stability — Measuring training as a path through representation space (Bures geometry, quantum Fisher information), the author finds paths become less direct as the task needs higher-order interactions among input factors, and that path tortuosity predicts later drift and sensitivity to perturbation better than the endpoint does, under matched-performance controls. Theory-leaning but a new measurement for how knowledge forms. Authors: Sanjukta Krishnagopal (institution not stated) · First submitted: 25 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: partly (how representations form). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34015

- How to loop a transformer — design rules from up to 1B parameters, 155B tokens, 32 loops — Looping the full transformer gives the best quality at fixed parameter count; looping a middle block pays better when training FLOPs are tight. Extra loops eventually saturate, reverse or destabilise; loop-specific low-rank adapters help, and a fix to Parallel Loop Transformers (keep the current-token embedding, normalise it separately) recovers the quality lost to the decoding slowdown. Relevant to the "reasoning in latent depth" line of work. Authors: Chanakya Ekbote, Artidoro Pagnoni, Devendra Singh Sachan, et al. (institution not stated) · First submitted: 29 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40325

- Auditing sparse-autoencoder features for wrong answers: SAE activations predict wrongness and uncertainty in Gemma 3 1B/4B across eight QA datasets, but artifact-filtered features flip answers rarely and with unstable direction, so they reflect answer competition, not the error mechanism. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38067
- Causal head localisation of evidence counting: controlled symbol-count prompts plus ablation and patching find compact attention-head sets (Pythia-410M) that carry the "count effect", with conditional bounds on recoverability. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36524
