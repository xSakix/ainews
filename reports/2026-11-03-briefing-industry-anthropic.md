## Safety and security

- 🚨 Anthropic's amended S-1 says internal estimates mean it "may imminently meet" its own Responsible Scaling Policy threshold for "dramatically accelerated progress attributable to AI" — The S-1/A filed on 2 November adds risk-factor text saying Anthropic has in the past "not identified" a significant acceleration of its own development until after it occurred, because its evidence comes from lagging indicators. It says "our internal estimates and our expectations regarding future models, rather than our current models" point to possibly meeting the RSP definition, that no current model has been designated as exceeding it, and that it is "considering updates to our Responsible Scaling Policy to keep humans in control of frontier model development". The filing notes that Mythos was released in April 2026 as a gated preview through Project Glasswing because of its cyber capability, and that future models may face similar access limits. Tension: a lab telling investors that AI-driven acceleration can be seen only after the fact, while also saying it is evaluating the AI R&D capability threshold. TechCrunch reads the passage as a warning of recursive self-improvement and notes an S-1 is both disclosure and marketing; the quoted text is in the filing. - Google and Mandiant: "LLMJacking" now costs victims over $100,000 a day, up to $1 million before access is revoked, and it has a division of labour — Report of 2 November on 2026 incident-response cases in North America, South America and Asia Pacific. Initial access is mostly stolen or exposed cloud identity tokens (IAM credentials, service-account keys, GitHub personal access tokens, session tokens), not the LLM interface. Cases include a cluster (UNC6993) that created extra API keys and enabled services in the victim's cloud projects for $100,000 of charges; a hijacked-session phishing page that tricked users into pasting raw JSON access tokens, bypassing MFA; automated key-validation sweeps using `max_tokens=1` to stay below volume alerts; and exposed Ollama endpoints where attackers registered their own models. GTIG describes "LLM Mixers" that proxy and resell stolen access; per The Register, one such service (Jet Night) sold an "unlimited" frontier-model plan for about $60 a month and one intruder made over 500,000 calls in under eight hours. Why it matters: it is a billing risk for anyone with an API key in a repository or a public Ollama port. ## Hardware and infrastructure

- Qualcomm validates its HBC stacked-memory design: over 5x HBM4's bandwidth per watt, 18x the bandwidth of external LPDDR5X (company figures) — ServeTheHome (2 November). HBC puts a small compute die under DRAM layers, so far less bandwidth has to cross to the main die and cheaper organic substrates can replace CoWoS or EMIB; it uses LPDDR5X dies, and projected capacity per stack exceeds today's HBM4. Caveats the article states: the test sample had 2 DRAM layers (shipping parts will have 4), no absolute bandwidth was disclosed, and the efficiency result is a bit short of Qualcomm's own projection. Samples of the Dragonfly AI250 are due in 2027. Source: https://www.servethehome.com/qualcomm-validates-hbc-stacked-memory-ahead-of-2027-dragonfly-ai250-sampling/

Nvidia invested $2 billion in Lumentum eight months ago. The deal value is reported differently (FT $115 billion; The Information's headline says $80 billion), so treat the figure as unsettled. Source: https://www.theregister.com/systems/2026/11/02/marvell-wants-to-be-a-copper-topper-bets-115b-on-photonics-leader-to-push-optical-networking/5307028
