## 3. China

- Ming-Image-0.2-Design (inclusionAI, Ant Group) — Open-weight text-to-image model for graphic design: e-commerce hero images, UI drafts, text-heavy layouts. The card says it upgrades composition, lighting and text rendering, and that a related layer-decomposition model now splits design images down to character parts for editing and animation. The headline claim is its own screenshot of the Artificial Analysis text-to-image leaderboard (AA-Image-T2I v2.1, as of 25 November): number one among open-weight models overall. That is a leaderboard position from a card, not an independent evaluation, and it covers open-weight models only. The DiT has 6B parameters; the validated setup is one 80 GiB GPU at 2048x2048, 12 sampling steps, CFG 1.0, served with vLLM-Omni. Why it matters: a permissively licensed (MIT) design-oriented image model that follows long structured prompts with exact text is directly usable by the people who build landing pages and ads. Source: https://huggingface.co/inclusionAI/Ming-Image-0.2-Design
Artefacts: weights (MIT), code (inclusionAI/Ming-Image), no technical report linked from the card · Sizes or variants: 6B DiT · Released: 25 November 2026 (HF upload 04:02 UTC)

- WePocketModel 1.0 (Tencent, WeChat AI) — Family of on-device vision-language models (2B and 4B) in which one backbone does three jobs: text generation (OCR, captioning, VQA), multimodal embeddings (2,048 dimensions, truncatable to 64 through 1,024 with Matryoshka learning) and "decision" outputs (structured choices, ordinal scores, yes/no judgements) for app logic. A technical report (arXiv 2611.27365, "Hierarchical Conflict Reconciliation for Unified MLLMs") and Apple-silicon MLX builds with a numpy runtime appeared on 25 November. The base weights went up on 16 to 17 November, so this is a late-surfacing release, included because no earlier briefing or post carried it. The 4B build is based on a Qwen3.5 backbone with a Qwen3-VL vision tower, and Tencent recommends at least 16 GB of GPU memory (24 GiB of RAM on CPU). The card gives benchmark charts only as an image; I could not read the numbers, so none are reproduced here, and they are Tencent's own. The card itself says developers should validate the decision outputs before using them to trigger actions. Source: https://huggingface.co/tencent/WePocketModel1.0-4B (report: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27365)
Artefacts: weights (Apache 2.0), code, GGUF and MLX builds, technical report · Sizes or variants: 2B and 4B (4.54B parameters in the 4B safetensors) · Released: weights 16 to 17 November; report and MLX builds 25 November 2026

## 4. Rest of the world

- Sakana Namazu Small (Sakana AI, Japan) — 30B-parameter reasoning model built by continued pre-training and post-training on top of the base model of NVIDIA's open Nemotron 3 Nano, aimed at everyday dialogue, web-search research and long-document question answering with cited evidence. Sakana's own comparison over 25 benchmarks says it beats both Nemotron 3 Nano (same size) and Nemotron 3 Super (120B-A12B, about four times larger) on the average. The largest gaps are in web research: BrowseComp 73.5 against 29.3 for Nano and 36.1 for Super. Sakana states that the knowledge-only category (no search) did not improve much. Context length is 262,144 tokens in the test setup. The page names no weights, API or price; on-premises delivery is planned, with details promised for the first half of December. The work used support from the Japanese GENIAC programme. All numbers are Sakana's, run against Nemotron models with the settings it chose. Source: https://sakana.ai/namazu-small/
Artefacts: none stated (on-premises offer announced, details due early December) · Sizes or variants: 30B · Released: 25 November 2026

- Sarvam Extract (Sarvam AI, India) — API that pulls structured JSON (value, source page, bounding box, confidence) out of documents of up to 500 pages, in English and 22 Indian languages, powered by Sarvam Vision 2.1. Sarvam reports 95.4 out of 100 on the public ExtractBench in Advanced mode at Rs 2.50 per page ($25 per 1,000 pages) and 94.4 in Basic mode at Rs 1 per page ($10 per 1,000); only successfully processed pages are billed. Its main comparison is long documents: on test documents over 50 pages it scores 93 against 28 for sending the whole file to Gemini 3.8 Flash in one call, and on lists of 100 or more rows it returns about 98% of rows against about 10%. That comparison is Sarvam's own setup, and a single-call baseline is the weakest way to run a general model; the page-by-page Gemini setup is also on its chart. Available to users in India and the US, with on-premises deployment for enterprises. Source: https://www.sarvam.ai/blogs/extract-api
Artefacts: API only (on-premises for enterprises) · Sizes or variants: Basic and Advanced modes · Released: 24 November 2026

- Kandinsky ImageSR 2.0 (KandinskyLab, Russia) — One-step diffusion image super-resolution model, one set of weights for x2 and x4, built on the Kandinsky 5 transformer in Diffusers 0.41 or newer; the text tower and all 50 cross-attention layers are replaced by precomputed per-timestep tables, which is how it avoids text conditioning. It works in 1024-pixel tiles. The card publishes timings measured on one H100 80GB (median of 5 runs after 2 warm-ups): a 1K image upscaled x2 to 2K takes 2.78 s, 1K to 4K takes 7.80 s, and 2K to 8K takes 37.5 s. No quality benchmark in the card text I read; quality is shown through a demo video. The bundled VAE comes from FLUX.1-schnell (Apache 2.0). Source: https://huggingface.co/kandinskylab/Kandinsky-ImageSR-2.0
Artefacts: weights (MIT, bundled VAE Apache 2.0), code, demo Space · Sizes or variants: about 4.5B parameters in the safetensors · Released: 24 November 2026
