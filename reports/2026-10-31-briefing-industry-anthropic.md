## Safety and security

- 🚨 The official Claude Code extension on Cursor's marketplace was replaced with a backdoored build for about 11 hours, reaching at most 5,000 users — Cursor says versions 2.1.312 and 2.1.313 (Windows and macOS) were live between 02:27 and 13:35 PT on 29 October and tried to add an attacker SSH key, enable SSH, open a reverse tunnel with persistence, and send hostname, username, environment-variable names, Git email and public IP to the attacker. Because of bugs, the tunnel step fails unless Cursor runs as administrator, but the key, persistence and the beacon still happen, and simply updating does not remove them. Anthropic (Felix Rieseberg, quoted by The Register) says its own systems and its official channels were not compromised and that someone with access to Cursor's marketplace published the tampered copy; neither company has said how the attacker got in, and Ars reports Anthropic had not warned customers directly. Phorion, which found it, updated its advisory on 31 October to add 2.1.312. Why it matters: agent extensions run with the developer's full permissions and auto-update, so one marketplace account is a path to source code and cloud credentials. - Wiz counts at least nine AI developer platforms breached in October, and the latest, Northflank, told all customers to assume every environment variable was decrypted — Wiz lists Fireworks AI, Andromeda, Zeabur, Lightning AI, OpenCode, Railway, Warp, CommandCode and Northflank; the three that share root causes point to stolen credentials, and every report shows attackers hunting stored environment variables that grant AI access. Wiz traced two stolen enterprise keys: the first use came within 30 minutes of the vendor breach, tried the newest Claude model, fell back to Opus 5.5, then one key ran about 800,000 Bedrock calls in 30 hours (about 90 percent succeeded) because its policy allowed global cross-region profiles. Northflank's status page (updated 31 October 01:04 UTC) confirms users saw unauthorised use of their AI API keys and has switched audit logs on for all customers. Wiz is the source for the pattern and the "distillation" and "agent swarm" attributions made by some victims; those are the victims' claims, not verified. Why it matters: AI keys are now the monetisable prize in platform breaches, so a leaked key is a bill and a model-access incident at once. Source: https://www.wiz.io/blog/black-october-for-ai-developer-platforms ; https://status.northflank.com

- A ransomware crew called n0n spent more than $7,216 on AI services and left its whole workspace exposed — SOCRadar found a public file server with 1,719 files (attack scripts, ransomware source, extortion infrastructure, an AI coding agent environment) that shows the operator used AI to write and debug hundreds of scripts, yet every documented intrusion began with weak or stolen credentials (RDP scanning, FortiGate VPN credential stuffing, an exposed AWS key), not a software flaw. n0n claims 108 victims since September; its 38 maritime-sector listings with demands of $400,000 to $28 million show no on-chain payments. SOCRadar found no sign of a fully autonomous attack system. Why it matters: a measured counterpoint to "AI finds every hole" stories: the gain here was speed and tooling, not new ways in. Source: https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/30/crim-spent-7k-on-ai-services-in-extortion-attacks-then-left-evidence-for-all-to-see/5306754/

## Policy and regulation

- OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic say they will honour one shared robots.txt "policy token" for AI-training preferences — proposed at the IETF by Anthropic's Kevin Kelley, so a publisher can opt out once instead of per company. Anthropic has already adopted it; Google and Microsoft plan support next year and OpenAI "in the coming months"; Microsoft will also offer separate opt-outs for training and for grounding, and sites stay in Bing search either way. Meta is quoted in an update but the Verge excerpt does not say whether it will honour it. The IETF draft itself was not read, and OpenAI's own post sits behind a JavaScript check. Why it matters: it makes the opt-out machine-readable, but it is a signal crawlers choose to honour, not enforcement. Source: https://www.theverge.com/tech/1023763/openai-google-microsoft-ai-scraping-standard-opt-out

- The Wikimedia Foundation filed an amicus brief in the New York Times v. OpenAI and Microsoft cases asking courts to protect training on copyrighted works but not chatbots reproducing them — it takes neither side, warning that a publisher win could let rightsholders "gatekeep" transformative uses, while an AI-company win would be "a green light to snub licensing terms". Why it matters: a rare third position in the fair-use fight, from the owner of one of the most used training sources. Source: https://www.theverge.com/policy/1022880/wikipedia-openai-new-york-times-copyright

- Australian experts warn that letting AI companies scrape copyrighted work could breach the constitution's "just terms" rule — constitutional lawyer Anne Twomey says it is "potentially" a problem depending on drafting, and a Nationals senator says Australia could face compensation claims from "every rights holder on Earth"; the attorney-general's department says it is "extremely alive" to the issue ahead of a federal AI announcement. Why it matters: shows how a text-and-data-mining exception can fail on property law, not only on copyright policy.
