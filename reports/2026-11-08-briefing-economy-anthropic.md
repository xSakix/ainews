## How teams work with agents

- Dropbox: coding agents took 53 test-cleanup tasks in one week, work it had deferred for two quarters — the engineering post (5 November) describes delegating defined tasks (each starting from a written plan agreed with the engineer, local builds and tests, results recorded for review), 22 accessibility features delivered with AI assistance, and shared instruction files kept in the code. Engineers moved from AI help on single coding steps to delegating implementation and testing for review; all eight initial design partners kept the new experience on. These are the company's figures, in a post that also promotes its product.
  Who is affected: product engineers, QA and front-end teams · Evidence: company account with counts · Tension: backlog work that "required substantial engineering time" cleared in a week, but review stays with humans

## What AI costs

- Datadog: internal AI apps with no cache hits cost $0.74 to $0.86 per request on 180,000+ input tokens, against $0.02 to $0.14 with caching — the company's engineering post (6 November) traces misses to a changing value (time, feature flags, incident context) placed early in the prompt, and to caching switched on without the needed fields reaching the provider. It matters because a prefix change silently multiplies the bill. Datadog sells the tooling it used, and the figures are estimated costs from its own traces.
  Who is affected: teams running agents on provider APIs · Evidence: company account with per-request estimates · Tension: caching "enabled" did not mean caching used
  Source: https://www.datadoghq.com/blog/prompt-cache-investigation-ccm-agent-observability/
