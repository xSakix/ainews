## United States

🚨 Thinking Machines Lab releases Inkling 2 Mini, an open-weights looped MoE that runs on a laptop — Thinking Machines' post (20 Nov; Hugging Face weights created 17 Nov) describes a multimodal mixture-of-experts with 26.4B total and 3.4B active parameters whose middle 12 of 24 layers run three times per token, so it behaves like a 48-layer model while storing 24. Passes exchange "cross-pass messages" through a gated controller; weights are trained natively in NVFP4 (last four layers MXFP8), about 20 GB in memory, with a 1M-token context, image and text input, reasoning-effort levels and a DSpark drafter. The lab claims it leads every open-weights model under 100B total parameters on DeepSWE v1.1 (48.2%) and Vals Index v2.2 (46.5%); Hugging Face's launch post reports it ahead of Gemma 4 31B, Muse Glimmer, Qwen3.8-27B and Nemotron 3.5 Super VL on 32 of 48 benchmarks and calls it "at least comparable" to Qwen3.8-27B in vibe tests, while saying more validation is needed. Benchmarks are the lab's and Hugging Face's own, run on varying setups. For the research reader, a variable-loop checkpoint is released too: the lab reports the no-CoT time horizon rose from 16 s at one pass to 37 s at three, serial-reasoning scores kept improving to ten loops (twice the training depth), chains of thought got shorter but stayed legible, and agentic-sabotage tests did not trend up with more loops. That bears directly on whether latent (unwritten) reasoning can still be monitored. Day-zero support in transformers, llama.cpp (GGUF 4-bit and 8-bit), MLX and SGLang. Artefacts: weights (Apache-2.0) + model card + blog with monitorability and mechanistic-interpretability sections · Sizes or variants: Inkling-2-Mini (fixed 3 loops), TML-d24-varloop-research (variable loop) · Released: 17 Nov weights on Hugging Face, 20 Nov announcement. Source: https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-mini/

## Europe

Kyutai releases AURA, a real-time audio-driven streaming avatar model — A 4-step causal student distilled from the 50-step bidirectional TalkVerse teacher (Snap, built on Wan2.2-TI2V-5B): a portrait plus a speech stream become talking-head video chunk by chunk (about 0.64 s chunks) with a rolling KV cache and no look-ahead. The paper (BMVC 2026) reports 9.7 fps end to end at 720p on one H100, 17x faster than the teacher (30x on the diffusion transformer alone), and constant memory so 60 s+ streams are possible; these are the authors' figures. Checkpoints come as 1.3B and 5B variants (the 5B is a 5.7B-parameter, 22.8 GB fp32 DiT) in merged and LoRA-only forms, plus a dataset. The licence is Snap's non-commercial licence, not open source in the strict sense, which limits who can build on it. Artefacts: weights (Snap Inc. non-commercial licence) + paper + code + dataset · Sizes or variants: AURA-1.3B, AURA-1.3B-Teacher-TalkVerse, AURA-5B · Released: 18 Nov weights on Hugging Face, 20 Nov blog post. Source: https://kyutai.org/blog/2026-11-20-aura/
Supporting: model card https://huggingface.co/kyutai/AURA-5B and paper https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26540

## Tools, APIs and platform updates

OpenAI details how it detects and blocks adversarial distillation, with a number on protected reasoning — The post (20 Nov) argues that a student model trained on a teacher's exact reasoning traces picks up extra, riskier capability (it describes an internal cyber-transfer study) without inheriting the teacher's safeguards. It says GPT-6.2 Astra and Sol ship with strengthened protection of reasoning content, and that in an internal adaptive red-team evaluation the average attack success rate at recovering protected reasoning fell from 54.0% against GPT-6 Astra to 0.2% against GPT-6.2 Astra. This is OpenAI's own measurement, not independently verified. For developers it signals that raw reasoning traces will stay hidden or summarised on frontier APIs, and that API-level detection of coordinated extraction campaigns is now part of the service; it also sharpens the argument over open versus closed reasoning traces. Artefacts: none (policy and safeguards description) · Sizes or variants: GPT-6.2 Astra, GPT-6.2 Sol (released 17 Nov) · Released: 20 Nov. Source: https://openai.com/index/combatting-adversarial-distillation

OpenAI introduces MedChemBench, a 99-problem medicinal-chemistry reasoning benchmark — Built from 117 papers and patents with 761 molecular structures; it grades structure reconstruction, evidence-based reasoning, compound ranking and property prediction. At xhigh reasoning GPT-6.2 Astra scores 84.5% of available credit versus 83.3% for GPT-6 Astra, 82.0% for GPT-6.1 Sol and 58.2% for GPT-5.6 Sol, all OpenAI's own runs on its own benchmark; the post says selection and numerical property prediction were the harder parts for the earlier model. Worth noting mainly as a measure of how fast OpenAI's own model line moved on one domain. Artefacts: benchmark description (check the post for data availability) · Released: 20 Nov. Source: https://openai.com/index/introducing-medchembench
