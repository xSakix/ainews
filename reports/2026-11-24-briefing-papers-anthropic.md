## Papers worth a deep dive

- Benchmarks' Last Exam: only 29.3% of tasks in six multi-step benchmark suites verified valid — A task-level quality review of 749 tasks across eight frontier benchmarks and 767 public model evaluations finds validity depends sharply on grading design: closed-form benchmarks revised after public error reports reach 92.5% verified validity, versus 29.3% across six multi-step (agentic, rubric) suites. Of confirmed defects, 71.7% withhold credit from compliant solutions and 28.3% allow shortcut passes, so raw leaderboard ceilings may understate frontier capability; grading patches in a fresh container cut confirmed runtime bypasses from 14.7% to 2.7% (5.5x). Limitation: a synthesis of reviews by two authors, billed as the initial milestone of an ongoing initiative; the review process behind "verified" is theirs. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27622

- Chain-of-thought faithfulness and efficiency measured as two separate axes — The paper turns a chain of thought into a semantic reasoning graph and splits the model's predictive dependence into what semantic support explains versus extra reasoning history (faithfulness), and answer-reaching versus off-path steps (efficiency). Controlled interventions show the metrics respond to hidden dependence and induced off-path reasoning; on maths and multi-hop tasks, answer-level unfaithfulness goes with higher predictive uncertainty, but both unfaithfulness and inefficiency correlate only weakly or inconsistently with correctness, so accuracy and brevity are incomplete measures of reasoning quality. Graph-aware fine-tuning objectives improve the targeted properties without hurting accuracy. Limitation: the abstract gives no headline effect sizes and the metrics rest on the authors' graph construction. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27100

- Language models organise a selection prompt as compress-then-compare — Token covariance maps track how token positions co-vary in hidden-state space per layer. Across 14 decoder-only models from 4 families and 12 structured-selection benchmarks, early layers are diffuse, middle layers squeeze the prompt into a shared low-rank bottleneck, and late layers re-expand into context-option and option-option geometry; the pattern also appears in instruction-tuned models, several languages and open-ended generation, and emerges during training from a flat profile. Low-rank intervention near the bottleneck disrupts late comparison geometry, and altering one candidate's representation in the compressed region can shift which candidate is later preferred. Limitation: one-author NeurIPS 2026 paper; claims here are drawn from the abstract. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26874

- LLM "synthetic publics" compress human preference variation to 0.4-10.5% — Replaying matched pairwise policy choices from 1,991 Chilean, 843 French and 434 Brazilian adults through three open-weight model families, no condition (27 in the Chilean grid) jointly reproduced individual choices, population heterogeneity and political organisation. Synthetic participant variance was only 0.4-10.5% of the human reference and below it in all 270 matched refits; reversing proposal position changed 12.3-31.2% of decoded choices, and labelling the left-right scale endpoints changed 20.5-28.1% while improving matched agreement by only 3.1-4.2 points. Limitation: open-weight models only, three countries, one elicitation family. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28383

- Skipping reasoning makes fast decision models easy to poison — Auditing four fast "System-1" decision models on 1,440 paired synthetic browser tasks, clean accuracy averages 91.0% but two-shot attack success (false conclusions or evidence readings injected via page comments) reaches 81.2%, and mean expected calibration error rises from 2.6% to 36.7% (conclusion poisoning) and 36.1% (evidence poisoning). On 48 matched tasks the same base model answers 95.83% of poisoned inputs correctly with a short chain of thought (under 30 reasoning tokens) versus 38.54% for the native fast-decision variant, at 1.94 s versus 0.29 s latency. Limitation: synthetic browser tasks and a 48-task matched comparison; relevant to anyone replacing agent reasoning steps with fast classifiers (see AnyS1 below). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27679

- A security block in the prompt cuts findings by a third in 400 AI-generated web apps — 100 frozen specifications were built four times (Claude Code and Codex CLI, each with and without a fixed OWASP-aligned security block) and a static and dynamic pipeline triaged 6,721 findings. The block cut findings per application by about a third (incidence-rate ratio 0.65, 95% CI 0.60-0.71) in both tools and nearly halved high-severity first-party findings, but roughly doubled the declared dependency surface, so raw dependency advisories rose (flat per application once adjusted for surface). Limitation: single-author study of two tools and one prompt block; scanner findings are not exploited vulnerabilities. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28408

- Parasite features: interpretability tests that pass for the wrong reason — A class of spurious features passes causal-abstraction equivalence tests: if one direction discriminates an input feature and another steers the output, Distributed Alignment Search can fake the combined behaviour through pathways passing through them, giving the impression the model implements the feature. Experiments find such parasite features with linear alignment maps in syntactic agreement and two-digit addition. Limitation: two tasks; a caution for mechanistic claims, not a replacement method. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26711

- State of Mind: does a brain model track the person or just the movie? — BrainStateBench scores a model's latent state with a frozen linear probe on whole-cortex movie-driven fMRI (State, Forecast, Updating). Stimulus-only encoders reach 0.320 prediction correlation yet 0% on State; prior deep brain world models reach 0.239 and at most 0.4%; the proposed linear two-timescale model Spectator gets 0.349, 5.5% State, 16.0% Forecast, 20.3% Updating, forecasting over 20x faster. Limitation: absolute State scores stay low and the benchmark is the authors' own. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28508

- What does matching a human effect in an LLM simulation actually prove? — Equivalence tests against humans pass trivially when the human interval lies inside the margin (a zero reference passes). In a 70-experiment reanalysis, 72% of GPT-4's +-0.20 SD passes were zero-reference passes; in a preregistered audit a Claude Sonnet 5.5 simulation passed three intention targets (all zero-reference passes) but norm beliefs shifted only 0.065 SD against humans' 0.351. The authors advise reporting the zero-reference threshold beside every equivalence decision. Limitation: partly exploratory, with post-hoc additions the authors flag. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27186

- Parametric memory that forgets on purpose — Some Memories Must Fade puts a fixed-capacity LoRA adapter with independent temporal decay behind a learned controller deciding what to remember and for how long; on a controlled long-horizon user-trajectory dataset it reaches 86% of oracle performance, balancing recall and abstention where continual-learning and RAG baselines default to one behaviour. Limitation: the dataset is the authors' own controlled construction. The related Internalized Experience paper (https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28613, first submitted 2026-11-21, Xianxuan Long et al.) finds unchanged task rules get harder to internalise beside more action types, and that cumulative corpora mixing short and long trajectories can beat long-only training with fewer tokens; also synthetic worlds. ## Also notable

- Prompting caution: asking a model to "verify" retrieved text raises false-answer adoption — FutureBench (3,629 questions, six models) finds a Verify prompt (check plus passage priority) raises false-answer adoption in all five models on pre-cutoff events, mostly by displacing answers that were right without it; an Adversarial-Aware prompt that warns of misleading passages and favours model knowledge restores 82.5-98.9% of displaced answers with under one point change in true-passage accuracy. Caveat: one benchmark family from two authors. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23601

- Self-rewarding optimisation collapses when self-reward fidelity falls — Prompt-level attacks (re-ranking, preamble) corrupt the self-generated preference signal and trigger iterative collapse; improvement holds while self-reward fidelity stays above a threshold and collapses below it, and a Projection Decoupling Defense improves robustness. Caveat: the threshold is model- and task-dependent and the abstract gives no headline numbers. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28582

- Two AI partners lower human contribution in brainstorming — In an experiment with N=168 and human-human dyads as reference, two-AI rooms posted more ideas but human-authored ideas dropped substantially, and only a second AI increased within-session differentiation. Caveat: a small lab study on one task; single-agent results did not carry over. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26663

- Head specialisation as a phase transition in sample complexity — In a solvable high-dimensional multi-head attention model, specialisation is a genuine phase transition in dataset size, mirrored by syntactic-relation heads emerging in BERT as data grows. Caveat: theory on a simplified model; real-model evidence limited to BERT. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26228

- History-guided choice in a thalamocortical circuit model — In published mouse data, suppressing parietal cortex or pulvinar improved judgments most when history conflicted with the stimulus; in trained circuit models replaying pulvinar input reconstructed a historical trace and replaying the opposite history redirected later choices. Caveat: circuit models fitted to published data, not new recordings. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28799

- Do LLMs share human language attitudes? Straattaal test — Humans (IAT) and models (LLM Word Association Test) both link the Dutch multiethnolect Straattaal with people of migration background, more strongly in larger and newer models, and explicit ratings are comparable. Caveat: one language variety, and the authors flag how far human implicit-attitude concepts transfer to models. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22335

- Pragmatic inference does not transfer across languages: PILAPS — A bilingual English/Mandarin benchmark across eight pragmatic phenomena tests eight LLMs from four families: open-source models struggle with humour, models are more literal than humans in dialogue, Theory-of-Mind prompting lowers the literal share of errors and Chain-of-Thought slightly raises it. Caveat: the abstract gives no headline accuracy. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25459

- DualSAE separates interpretability from reconstruction in sparse autoencoders — A residual branch with a decorrelation loss takes the non-semantic remainder, giving about 29% better reconstruction fidelity and 34% better monosemanticity score on average across two vision datasets and two text corpora. Caveat: relative gains on the authors' metrics; code released (AtlasAnalyticsLab/DualSAE). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25165

- Diffusion-specific Activation Oracle reads image content from early denoising — Extending LatentQA to diffusion models, it describes the final image from activations at early denoising steps, can steer generation, and via attribution patching locates components behind attributes. Caveat: no headline numbers in the abstract; code released (yamanoko/dao). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28621

- AnyS1: one 4B System-1 for agent decision points — Trained on decision points mined from over 1,000 public skills, it cuts episode cost by 75-91% and model-call time by 63-92% across three unseen benchmarks, falling back to the large model when calibrated confidence is low. Caveat: read with the poisoning result above; robustness to adversarial input is not tested here. Project page available. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23751

- Openhagen Worlds: calibrated scientific-discovery environments — 27 procedurally generated families, 178 calibrated levels; on 84 withheld frontier instances DeepSeek V4.1 Flash solves 32 at default effort and 57 at maximum effort (oracle 79), and expert iteration lifts a 9B model's core-tier success from 0.090 to 0.148, partly through better action format. Caveat: single-author benchmark; hosted evaluation at eval.openhagen.ai. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24711

- Chiron: a foundation model for predicting human behaviour — Fine-tuned on 288,307 person records and 1,411,750 behavioural decisions, it predicts individual behaviour up to 28.4% (relative) better than frontier LLMs and cuts effect-size error 82.2-94.3% in simulated social-science experiments. Caveat: author-chosen benchmarks; read next to the synthetic-public and zero-reference papers above, which question this kind of simulation. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24830

- Grid cells as spectral successor features for continual RL — Building base features from transition-matrix eigenvectors makes successor features closed-form, so adapting to a new goal is a linear regression; in a two-goal gridworld agents keep near-maximal return across goal changes where SimpleSF and DQN lose most of theirs. Caveat: toy gridworld, workshop paper (NeurReps/WiML). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26504
