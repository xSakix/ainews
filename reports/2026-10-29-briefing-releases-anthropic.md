## 1. United States

- 🚨 Google announces Gemini 4 Carbon, its "strongest model", two weeks after Gemini 4 Argon, and opens it only to a subset of cyber defenders through the Fairwind Program — Google says Carbon ranks first on version 5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index with a score of 59, and leads cybersecurity benchmarks: 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro (finding vulnerabilities), 54.8% on ExploitGym (exploitation) and 71.0% on CWE-bench (patching), all scored "without guardrails". The tension is in access: the model is not yet available to developers, businesses or consumers, Google says it is "actively engaged in the U.S. government's voluntary process for pre-release model testing", and it will "gradually phase access" to trusted partners. The post also reports behavioural numbers, all Google's internal tests: successful cheating on Terminal-Bench 4.0 down to 0.0% (attempts 0.4%), high-severity misalignment alerts down almost 80% in live coding, unsupported claims cut about 2x against Argon, and a 0.0% attack success rate on Gray Swan's indirect prompt-injection benchmark. Planned pricing equals Argon's introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens (cached input 95% off), rising to $4 and $20 after the introductory period. On Hacker News (830 points) commenters pointed out that the Artificial Analysis page had not yet shown the new score, and that the pricing and the "not released for a while" pattern invite scepticism. Every figure is Google's own; no independent evaluation or model card was linked from the post. - NVIDIA introduces Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe, one open-weight 30B-A4B checkpoint (about 4.3B active parameters) that transcribes 40 languages, translates 31 of them to or from English, labels speakers in 13 and answers questions about the audio, steered by plain-language prompts — It handles recordings up to an hour with up to eight speakers, supports word-level timestamps, and the language list includes Czech and Slovak. NVIDIA says it is first among open-weight models on the Hugging Face Open ASR Leaderboard for English (5.33% average word error rate) and 4.62% across seven languages in the multilingual track, lowest speaker-attribution error on David AI's 21-language diarization leaderboard, and 80% fewer active parameters than the open-weight model ranked first on Artificial Analysis (the lab's own claims; leaderboards are third-party but the framing is NVIDIA's). Alongside it come Granary v2, an open multilingual training dataset with its curation pipeline, and fine-tuning recipes; in a medical case study fine-tuning cut word error rate on held-out speakers by 45.8% relative to the best prompt. The model's repository was created on 7 October, but the announcement and public launch are 28 October. Quantised versions (NVFP4, for RTX Spark, DGX Spark and Jetson AGX Thor) are "coming soon". Artefacts: weights (OpenMDW-1.1 licence, "other" on Hugging Face), model card, technical blog, dataset (Granary v2) and fine-tuning code via NeMo Speech · Sizes or variants: 30B total, about 4.3B active, built on Nemotron-3.5-Lightning-30B-A3B · Released: 28 October 2026.
