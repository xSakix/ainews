## Papers worth a deep dive

- Believed but Not Said: hidden belief collapse in multi-agent LLM discussions — In a ten-agent factual discussion, one adversarial agent that keeps asserting a plausible false answer raises the share of benign agents ending on the false answer from 0.6% to 44.8%, and three adversaries raise it to 60.5% (six models, three factual QA datasets). With three adversaries, benign agents hold the false answer privately in 51.6% of exposed turns but endorse it publicly in only 19.7%, the reverse of the classic human pattern of public agreement without private change. Agents that keep the true answer often fail to defend it, and baseline knowledge does not predict robustness. For anyone building "agents check each other" pipelines, the peer-review assumption does not hold against a minimal adversary. Authors: Liangjie Huang, Henry Peng Zou, Wei-Chieh Huang et al. (affiliation not stated on the abstract page) · First submitted: 15 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (belief, conformity). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22138

- From Copying to Composition: how language models develop in-context learning — A controlled diagnostic separates copying a nearby demonstration from integrating information across the prompt. Across 17 models about a third prefer the copied answer, a few are undecided, and the rest prefer the rule-consistent answer, which goes with higher accuracy. Checkpoint analyses show models start prioritising task-relevant prompt tokens long before accuracy rises above chance, and synthetic documents that define and then query word endings shift models toward compositional answers. The paper argues ICL is not one capability but several (prompt control, answer preference, construction without candidates, transfer) that develop on separate trajectories; useful for anyone who writes few-shot prompts with conflicting examples. Authors: Minglai Yang, Xinyu Guo, Renxiong Wang et al. · First submitted: 13 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (how a model uses context). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19113

- Causal Syntax Circuits in CodeLMs: Budget and Probe Decide What Looks Localised — A careful negative result on interpretability claims. Across ten open code models and 55 model-language settings, the measured Python preference falls from about 1.6 to about 0.01 when the aligned prompts are replaced by parallel functions, and whether a behaviour looks "localised" in a circuit depends on the component budget: 1 of 15 settings beats random controls at budget 12, 6 of 15 at budget 40. Interventions another study reported as uniformly failing do succeed in some settings (patching in 2 of 45, steering in 9 of 45). The practical lesson is that localisation claims can turn on analysis choices papers rarely report; the full grid is released. Authors: Harsh Kumar, Tanmay Shailesh Joshi, Rahul Maity · First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: data (full grid, per the abstract) · Cognitive side: yes (interpretability). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20683

- The Density of Thought: predicting and steering reasoning in large language models — Measures how unevenly information is spread over the steps of a chain of thought (variance of per-token negative log-likelihood, plus a compression ratio). Lower variance predicts correct answers in supervised-fine-tuned models, but the relation weakens with scale and reverses in RL-distilled models, so training paradigm matters more than size. Forcing the density at inference time drops accuracy from 60.0% to 8.3% on 60 GSM8K problems; a hint plus contrastive KV-cache nudge gains 13.3 points and a self-supervised entropy-gated variant 10.0 points. Small test set, so treat the sizes with care. Authors: Tanush Kalhan, Anupam Chattopadhyay, Sriram Ranga · First submitted: 15 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (reasoning dynamics). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21286

- Decomposing Brain-LLM Alignment with Interpretable Semantic Representations — Uses a 65-property, neurocognitively grounded semantic representation (SCRIBE) to split LLaMA2-7B's ability to predict fMRI responses (seven participants listening to Moth Radio Hour stories) into shared and unique variance. Shared variance dominates across the whole brain; concrete properties share more variance with the LLM than abstract ones, while the interpretable representation also keeps unique brain-predictive information the LLM misses. A direct comparison of what a language model and a cognitive feature set each explain in brain data. Authors: Camila Kolling, Mariya Toneva · First submitted: 15 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (neuroscience comparison). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21508

- Behavioral Compositionality of Sparse Autoencoder Representations — Introduces SAE Additivity Residuals, which measure how much of the combined behavioural effect of several SAE features is left unexplained by adding their individual effects. Learned SAE coordinates are more additive than random rotations of the same subspace in all 18 model-and-behaviour settings, but not fully so: pairwise terms improve held-out ordering in every setting, and in a steering experiment pairwise modelling raises rank fidelity from 0.587 to 0.779. Faithful joint explanations and interventions therefore need feature interactions. Authors: Haotian Ma, Ziye Mei, Frederic Sala et al. · First submitted: 15 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: code (GitHub repository linked in the abstract) · Cognitive side: yes (interpretability). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21261 Code: https://github.com/OpenSAE/SAE-Behavioral-Compositionality

- What and Where in Identifying and Quantifying Conventions in Humans and AI Agents — In Hanabi the exact failure probability of a play can be computed, so the margin by which players beat it (the "convention gap") measures implicit conventions. Humans show a larger gap than every off-belief-learning agent level in two human corpora, yet on the conventions both use humans do no better than level 2; the human edge sits in one situation no agent learns, hints that point to a card other than the ones touched. Blocking several high-gap situations together costs far more than the sum of blocking each. A rare quantitative human-versus-agent comparison on implicit communication. Authors: Makoto Fukushima, Tim Puphal · First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (human comparison). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20205

- Scaling Discovery Requires Forgetting — For LLM agents doing automated research (one agent iterating, parallel agents, parallel agents that communicate), progress plateaus as history accumulates because each agent anchors on its past attempts. Periodically discarding every session and the candidate history and restarting from the best candidate found so far gives up to 15% over parallel agents and up to 39% over a single agent across four tasks (heuristic programming, kernel optimisation, model training). A context-management technique a practitioner can try: reset the context on purpose. Authors: Shangyin Tan, Ben Zhou, Lakshya A. Agrawal et al. · First submitted: 14 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20563

- Apertus 1.5: From Open Text Models to Open Multimodal Reasoners — Technical report for the fully open Swiss public-institution model family: continued pretraining (4T extra tokens for the 8B, 2T for the 70B) adds image and audio understanding and a 262,144-token context; thinking is a chat-template flag rather than a separate model and lifts maths reasoning by up to 20 points on the 8B. Weights, data preparation, training recipes and evaluations are released under permissive licences, trained only on openly available data with retroactive crawler opt-outs respected. The authors state that specialised vision and audio models remain ahead on perception and high-resource transcription. Authors: Project Apertus (Swiss AI) · First submitted: 11 Oct 2026 · Artefacts: weights, recipes, evaluation suites · Cognitive side: no. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15542 Model page: https://huggingface.co/swiss-ai/Apertus-v1.5-8B
