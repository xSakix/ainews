- Non-English text costs up to 6.8 times more tokens in the newest commercial tokenizer measured, and up to 10 times in older ones — "Inherited Weights" (arXiv, v1 10 November) measures the "tokenization premium" (tokens needed relative to English for the same text) across 31 languages and 7 tokenizers released in 2023 and 2024, on 27 articles of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, correcting for unknown-token collapse. Amharic reaches 10 times in older tokenizers and 6.8 times in the newest commercial one; Indigenous South American languages pay 1.6 to 2.7 times; a vocabulary built from Portuguese cuts the premium for 11 of 13 Latin-script Global South languages, by about 24% for Guarani and Quechua. Because APIs bill per token, the premium multiplies the bill and eats the context window for the same content. The data and code are included, but the tokenizers are 2023 to 2024 generation, one text corpus, and the authors also present a proprietary architecture of their own with preliminary internal results, so treat that part as promotion.
