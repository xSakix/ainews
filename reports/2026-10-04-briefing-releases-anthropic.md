## 2. Europe

- 🚨 **Aleph Alpha releases Kolibri, a 78B open-weight mixture-of-experts reasoning model for German and English, with 1M-token context** — Kolibri has 78B total and 3.46B active parameters, 384 experts per layer (1 shared, 6 routed), a 50-layer transformer with 4:1 sliding-window to grouped-query attention, trained with the Muon optimizer and "Exact Quantile Balancing" on 20T tokens (about 62.5% English, 23.9% German, 13.6% code), plus 3.44T of mid-training and 201B for long-context extension. Aleph Alpha publishes unusually complete training accounting: 768 B200 GPUs, 21 days of pre-training, 6.4e23 FLOPs. The model has an explicit reasoning mode and tool calling; weights are FP8 (about 78 GB), so it fits on 2× H100 or one H200/B200. The lab's own benchmarks put it ahead of Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, Nemotron 3 Super 120B-A12B and Mistral Small 4 on most agentic and math rows (AIME 2025 96.9, GPQA Diamond 84.3, LiveCodeBench v6 85.9, BrowseComp 29.4, τ³-bench banking 38.1) while trailing Qwen on BFCL v4 and τ²-bench telecom; these are Aleph Alpha's claims, not independent results. It is the first major open-weight model from the lab, which is positioned for "sovereign" public-sector and industrial use and signed the EU GPAI Code of Practice. It was the top Hacker News AI story of the window (516 points). A technical report accompanies the release; the blog also mentions an earlier internal "Kolibri Origin" (30B total, 3B active, 65k context) used to validate the pipeline, which I did not find published as weights. Note for context: the Cohere–Aleph Alpha merger agreement is already covered in an earlier post; this item is about the model, not the deal.

## 4. Rest of the world

- **Sber's ai-forever releases FRIDA-Decisions, an 823M-parameter encoder that answers choice, score, yes/no and ranking questions over Russian text in one pass** — Built on the ai-forever/FRIDA T5 encoder by the SberAI team, it takes a text plus a list of options written inside the request, so a new label set is a new JSON, not a new training run, and every answer is one of the declared options with a confidence. The card claims 0.893 on razvilka, a new 735-item benchmark released alongside, against 0.897 for the commercial TypeSafe Jev API (paired McNemar p = 0.84), and the highest score among open models the authors tried. It reports 28–34 ms per request on an RTX 5060 Ti, 1.8 GiB peak GPU memory, an int8 ONNX build that runs on CPU, and a vLLM server at about 60 requests per second. It joins the week's cluster of small "decision" models (Perplexity's pplx-decider, TypeSafe Jev), here for Russian. All figures are the lab's own.
