## Deep dives and technical write-ups

- Are Harnesses Worth the Cost? — Naresh Jain ran the same API-service task 10 times per condition with and without a behavioral harness (architectural contracts plus deterministic test feedback). The frontier model GPT-6 Sol passed all 178 acceptance tests either way, and the harness cost it about 7 times more (about $2.68 vs $0.41 per run). The cost-optimized GPT-6 Luna passed 3 of 10 runs unharnessed and 10 of 10 with the harness, at 33% lower cost per run than Sol ($0.273 vs $0.406). Worth reading because it measures when scaffolding pays: for a cheaper model, not for a frontier one. One task and one author's setup, as the piece itself notes. Author: Naresh Jain, founder of Specmatic and ConfEngine (published on Martin Fowler's site) · Format: deep dive (experiment) · Length: about 20 min read. Source: https://martinfowler.com/articles/are-harnesses-worth-the-cost.html

- Why harnesses matter for energy (and token) efficiency — Neuralwatt's CEO analyses a month of its cloud's traffic (8 Sep to 7 Oct 2026) and finds that the client harness decides much of the GPU work: uncached input ranged from 1.6% to 11.1% across clients, and a cache miss roughly doubles a request's energy (about 2.5 times on GLM-5.3). Every cache break in their lab tests came from a harness editing text it had already sent. Practical for anyone building or choosing a coding agent, though it is one inference provider's data and the company sells the service. Author: Chad Gibson, co-founder and CEO of Neuralwatt · Format: deep dive (traffic analysis) · Length: about 14 min read. Source: https://neuralwatt.com/news/why-harnesses-matter-for-energy-efficiency

- I Deleted 168,000 Lines of Comments. My AI Agent Didn't Notice — Roberto Aranda removed every comment (about a third of the code, 168,000 lines) from a codebase and gave the same agent two tasks on both copies, three runs each. Every run passed lint, type checks and tests, and an AI judge scored the results the same with or without comments. Small sample (three runs per cell) and an AI judge, but a cheap, repeatable test of a common team rule. Author: Roberto Aranda, OpenStation · Format: deep dive (experiment) · Length: about 4 min read. - Faster Fourier Transforms — Recht argues that OpenAI's claimed faster FFT is "less a breakthrough and more a malicious exploit": it wins on a theoretical computer model far from real computers, in the same way that agents given impossible problems end up hacking the setup. He says the same holds for other results in OpenAI's math repository, such as an O(n^2.25) matrix multiplication whose hidden constant cannot even be estimated. A sharp critique from a named optimisation researcher; it is his reading, not a refutation by the authors of the OpenAI results. Author: Ben Recht, professor and author of the arg min blog · Format: essay with tutorial material · Length: about 8 min read. Source: https://www.argmin.net/p/faster-fourier-transforms
