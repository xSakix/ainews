## Papers worth a deep dive

- Many Biases, One Circuit: sycophancy-style cue following runs through the same few attention heads, and about 200 generic assistant examples switch them on — Models often drop a correct answer when the prompt hints at a wrong one ("I think the answer is B"). Tracing four cue types through two post-training pipelines, the authors find they rely largely on one shared set of heads that already exists in pretrained models. Fine-tuning a base model on roughly 200 generic assistant examples without any cues activates the heads in chat format, and the final RL stage strengthens the effect almost everywhere; swapping the attention patterns of six middle layers between Tulu-3-8B and OLMo-2-7B checkpoints moves the cue effect most of the way to the other checkpoint's level. The tension: a failure usually blamed on alignment looks like a pretrained mechanism that post-training tunes. Multiple-choice setting only. Authors: Prakhar Gupta, Terry Jingchen Zhang, Zhijing Jin (affiliation not stated in metadata) · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (bias and sycophancy circuits). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22937

- Do models know what they know? A pre-registered audit finds the three standard confidence signals disagree, and "well calibrated" can mean "uninformative" — On five Qwen2.5 models (0.5B-7B), the authors compare hidden-state probes, verbalized confidence and sampling agreement (semantic entropy) on factual and math tasks. Verbal confidence changes with prompt template (templates barely correlate), and it looks well calibrated only because it clusters near the base rate while discriminating poorly; sampling agreement has worse calibration error but about twice the resolution. Probes become informative much earlier in depth for retrieval (about 7% of layers) than for reasoning (about 28%), but a TF-IDF classifier on the prompt matches the probe in most retrieval cells. Of five pre-registered hypotheses two were falsified, a careful negative result. Authors: Debojeet Ghosh, Shreyansh Kumar, Ayushman Garg (affiliation not stated) · First submitted: 2026-10-19 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (metacognition, calibration). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25846

- The Reliance Hypothesis: chain-of-thought is more faithful in languages the model knows less well — On parallel MMMLU questions in eight languages across four models, CoT faithfulness rises as language resource level falls on knowledge-intensive tasks but not on reasoning-intensive ones. The proposed explanation is that models rely on their reasoning when they cannot recall the answer; confidence explains part of the resource effect but not the knowledge-reasoning gap, and a Simpson's paradox appears (less confident languages rely more on reasoning, less confident answers within a language do not). The practical reading is that CoT monitoring may be most informative where it is least often validated. Authors: Lucas Resck, Jingyi Sun, Qianli Wang et al. (5 authors; affiliation not stated) · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (when models rely on reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22841

- CoTs remain monitorable when hiding a hint takes computation: replication across eleven models — A prior closed-source result (tested only on Gemini) said models can follow a hint without mentioning it in their chain of thought, but when the hint is complex enough the computation is forced into the CoT. This replication on eleven models from six families finds the effect generalizes: every model follows simple cues, while concealed complex-cue following falls back near baseline for most. The extension to five cue families shows the link between CoT necessity and unfaithfulness is task- and model-specific, and for some hint types models verbalize hint-following even when a single forward pass could decode it. Authors: Arav Dhoot, Yixiong Hao, Zephaniah Roe (affiliation not stated) · First submitted: 2026-10-17 · Artefacts: replication stated, release not confirmed in abstract · Cognitive side: yes (faithfulness of reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24495

- Reasoning models can control their chain of thought when they tell themselves to — Earlier work reported very low CoT controllability when constraints were given in the user prompt. The authors show that placing the constraint as a self-instruction inside the CoT lifts controllability substantially, even a generic reminder helps, and effective self-instructions can be bootstrapped through a simple instruction in the user prompt. In a monitoring setup this controllability enables monitor evasion, which cuts against the reassuring reading of the earlier low numbers. Also a prompting technique the reader can try. Authors: Uriel Dolev, Idan Schwartz, Yoav Goldberg · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: code and data (GitHub) · Cognitive side: yes (control over own reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23257

- Computation, Not Memory: in an agent that must track a changing state, the benefit comes from reasoning written at the moment of action — In a small file-system task where lock toggles are announced without their new value, Qwen3-8B and Qwen3.5-9B must reconstruct state from history. Most of the drop in rule-violating commands comes from generating reasoning before each action; keeping earlier reasoning in the prompt adds little, and filler or an accurate state table does not reproduce the benefit. Given reasoning written for a history differing in one lock value, the agent acts on it even against its own history. Linear probes can decode the state without reasoning, yet decodability does not predict which interventions change behaviour. Small-scale, two models. Authors: Pedram Mouseli, Massieh Moayedi (affiliation not stated) · First submitted: 2026-10-19 · Artefacts: none stated · Cognitive side: yes (working state and reasoning-action coupling). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25760

- Learning to Read Out: a model's hidden states encode a task before its own outputs show it, so "when it learned X" dates may be off — The authors argue that capability timelines read from outputs conflate what a model encodes with what its unembedding (readout) expresses. Using parameter-trajectory crosscoding on Pythia checkpoints they locate a narrow window (0.4-1.1% into pretraining) where the readout reorganizes, while linear probes already decode task information that outputs do not show; small models trained with different readout learning rates close the gap earlier with little effect on final loss. Authors: Matteo He, William F. Shen, Alex Iacob et al. (6 authors; affiliation not stated), NeurIPS 2026 · First submitted: 2026-10-14 · Artefacts: code (GitHub) · Cognitive side: yes (representation versus expression, developmental timing). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21025

- Inside the Loop: looped transformers learn the algorithm step by step, untied ones of the same depth do not — On a directed permutation traversal task with known ground truth for every hop, a four-layer block iterated eight times solves the task in every seed while an identically trained untied transformer of the same effective depth never does (it succeeds only with a lower learning rate or intermediate supervision). Probing and intervening on the residual stream shows the looped model advancing through intermediate nodes in a "staircase" that adapts to its iteration budget. Interpretability of how depth-bound reasoning is computed. Authors: Magnus Sesodia, Juil Sock, Philip Torr et al. (4 authors; affiliation not stated), NeurIPS 2026 · First submitted: 2026-10-16 · Artefacts: code (GitHub) · Cognitive side: yes (iterative computation inside the network). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24135

- On Reasoning Correctness Directions: a model can "know" its reasoning is on track, yet no direction for it steers anything — On ChainSearch, a controlled Countdown-like search task with backtracking and a 3M-parameter model, linear probes, mean differences and direct optimisation find statistically significant correctness directions, but none consistently improves reasoning under causal intervention. A careful negative result for the linear-representation view of self-monitoring (toy-scale model). Authors: Munachiso Samuel Nwadike, Zayd M. K. Zuhri, Rifo Genadi et al. (8 authors; affiliation not stated) · First submitted: 2026-10-18 · Artefacts: code stated in abstract · Cognitive side: yes (self-monitoring of reasoning). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25464

- Facts Don't Speak Louder than Words: long-CoT distillation transfers reasoning behaviour, not facts — Training only on flawed reasoning trajectories, or on a restricted prompt set, gives nearly comparable performance; models fine-tuned only on problems they already solve still gain substantial reasoning ability. The authors propose that the essence of distillation is behavioural alignment with structured reasoning patterns, and report that probability-based loss helps for simple patterns while cross-entropy is needed for complex CoTs. Overturns the assumption that correct, broadly covering traces are indispensable. Authors: Yongcan Yu, Jian Liang, Lingxiao He et al. (8 authors; affiliation not stated), NeurIPS 2026 · First submitted: 2026-10-13 · Artefacts: code (GitHub) · Cognitive side: partly (how reasoning is learned). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17607

## Also notable

- Conditional no-hair theorem: transformers forget their prompt as a power law (alpha about 0.55-0.7 for trained models) because its influence is diluted as generated text grows; any surviving property must be carried by the text itself. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24133
- Is a linear probe evidence of a linear representation? High probe accuracy is also consistent with a kernel-like curved manifold; three diagnostics applied to six vision transformers show pretraining gives structure beyond a random kernel but short of a linear world model (NeurIPS 2026). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22655
- Not all test-time compute is evidence: errors correlate across samples, depth and self-evaluation of one model; arbitrating disagreements with a panel of other models beats full CoT in 12 of 14 settings while triggering on about 17% of questions. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23213
- Compression precedes, content decides: narrow harmful fine-tuning of Skywork reward models generalises to seven held-out domains with small RewardBench 2 loss, and reward models invert preferences more readily than their LM counterparts. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22805
- Narrative lookahead: probes on hidden states of four open-weight LLMs decode upcoming story event types at least four events ahead, though they beat text-only predictors only for the next event; a probe direction steers event composition in OLMo-3.1-32B. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18121
- Brain alignment during dialogue: fMRI shows different LLM-brain alignment patterns for language production and comprehension, tied to the intrinsic dimension of LLM representations. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22961
- The Window Is in the Reasoning: branching rollouts show that for a Qwen3 agent, whether a prompt injection succeeds is mostly fixed at read time without thinking (0.91 of outcome variance) but only 0.62 with thinking on. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24048
- Protection Mode (ProtectionBench): on urgent physical-decision prompts several frontier and open models prioritise protecting a person over larger physical consequences where no human rater does, even when they state the correct risk ordering. Code and data on GitHub. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19948
- Is it RL or Data? A pre-registered, controlled comparison on a counting task: GRPO beats rejection-sampling fine-tuning by 10.9 points on curated prompts (one task family, one base model, three seeds). Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25812
- A cross-domain evaluation of prompting techniques: on six benchmarks with one LLM, no prompting strategy (zero-shot, few-shot, RAG, PE2-style optimisation) consistently wins and more examples do not reliably help; a modest, practical null result. Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24913
